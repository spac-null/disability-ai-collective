Dr Gail Allsopp is leaving the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) because the department did not renew her contract. Her three-year term as chief medical adviser ended in the autumn of 2026. Asked whether the post would continue, the department said it "does not comment on individual staffing matters." That is the whole event. No letter, no reason, a form field returning a date.

I keep circling a question I cannot answer. A rule outlives the people who wrote it. Nobody is left to address a complaint about it. I thought Allsopp's departure was the story. It isn't. The story is the length of the contract.

Consider what she did in three years. She reviewed clinical governance across the department and pulled it in line with NHS practice. Her review said the department's clinical work "should be held to the same standard a patient would expect from the NHS." She set up quarterly cross-departmental safeguarding meetings. She put more than 5,000 DWP clinicians through mandatory safeguarding training that had never existed before. She invited disabled activists to give evidence to a departmental review. That part surprised me. I did not expect anyone inside that building to ask.

She was appointed in autumn 2023, after [Errol Graham](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-london-45676474) starved to death due to DWP benefit failures in 2016. After [Philippa Day](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-lincolnshire-50457004) died in 2021 following her benefits being stopped. A decade of deaths produced one post with a three-year clock on it.

I assumed the cruelty was that they let her go. Then I read the contract term again. The cruelty is the term. A three-year contract on a safeguarding role means the safeguarding is temporary by design. Someone drafted that clause. That person is gone now. The clause is still running.

I have written before about eligibility rules that execute rejections with no human present to intend them. A birth year in a form field. A residency clause drafted before the current staff arrived. The department did not renew Allsopp's contract, and the mechanism worked the same way. The rule that sorts a claimant into "vulnerable" and the rule that renews or does not renew the person watching for vulnerability were both written once and then left to run.

[David Lupton](https://davidlupton.co.uk), an investigative journalist, once tracked the DWP quietly underpaying benefit recipients by £3.3 billion. The same quiet ran through everything else. A £106 million plan to prevent claimant deaths was abandoned in secret. Nobody announced it. It simply stopped, the way a contract simply ends.

Disability Pride Month occurs each July. The DWP's decisions about staffing, funding, and contract length are not coordinated by any single official. They accumulate from separate clauses, separate budgets, separate departments. Responsibility is distributed until it disappears. The department's accumulated shape keeps killing people, and there is still nobody to send the complaint to.
