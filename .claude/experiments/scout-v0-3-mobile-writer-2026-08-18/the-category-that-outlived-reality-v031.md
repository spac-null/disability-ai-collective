"For less than twelve months." That was the phrase Mississippi's environmental regulators used, in a letter to xAI dated July 29, 2025, to describe how long each gas turbine at the company's new data center site would stay.

A few days after the letter was signed, three turbines sat on the property outside Southaven, on flatbed trailers, exactly as the letter described them. A year later, there were sixty-nine.

The letter came from Jaricus Whitlock, who runs the air division at Mississippi's Department of Environmental Quality. Brent Mayo, a vice president at xAI, had asked the state to confirm how the company's turbines would be classified. Whitlock's answer rested on two facts. Each turbine sat on a flatbed trailer rather than a poured foundation, which made it mobile under state rules. And xAI intended each one to be off the site within twelve months, which made it temporary. Machines that are both mobile and temporary don't need the air permit a permanent turbine would require.

The first fact, Whitlock's office could see for itself. The second, it could only take xAI's word for.

By early September, xAI told the state to expect seventeen turbines. By the end of October, aerial photographs — thermal imaging confirming that at least nine were already running — counted eighteen on site. That December, xAI told MDEQ nine more were coming. The total reached twenty-seven.

On February 13, 2026, the NAACP, the Southern Environmental Law Center, and Earthjustice sent xAI formal notice that they intended to sue over those twenty-seven turbines. Six more turbines appeared in the following weeks. The count reached thirty-three.

By that May, the total had passed forty-six. Asked about the growing number, Mississippi's environmental agency said xAI did not need to notify it each time another turbine was added under the temporary-mobile designation.

No number was attached to that answer. Nothing in the record says how many turbines would have had to arrive before the agency wanted to be told.

By August, one count put the total at sixty-nine.

In July 2026, MDEQ issued a retirement schedule for the Southaven turbines. The original deadline for them to be gone was December 31, 2026. Thirteen of the turbines were granted permission to keep running past that date. Three of those thirteen were granted more time again, enough, by the agency's own accounting, to keep them in place until roughly July 2027.

Whitlock's letter had described machines that would remain less than twelve months. Two summers later, the schedule that replaced that promise had thirteen of them staying on past their deadline, and three of those staying on toward the middle of the year after that — turbines that were, on paper, still the ones that phrase was written for.
