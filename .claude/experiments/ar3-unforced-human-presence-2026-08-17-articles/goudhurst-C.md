TITLE: Six Hundred Signatures and a Database Field

A lorry wedged on a bend in Goudhurst, Kent. The driver followed the satnav. The satnav followed the map. The map said the A262 was the most direct path between Ashford and Tunbridge Wells, which it is. The road narrows to a width the vehicle cannot fit through, which the map did not encode. So the lorry stops, wedged against a building, and traffic backs up behind it, and someone photographs the scene, and the photograph joins a folder of identical photographs stretching back years.

The fix, when it came, was not a new road. It was a database field. Here Technologies, whose mapping data underlies several major satnav systems, confirmed it had added "restrictions in our database to guide larger commercial vehicles around, and not through, Goudhurst." One company changed one field. Some satnav systems updated. Some did not. Residents were asked to keep photographing.

The lorry driver in Goudhurst had the correct information. The A262 exists. It connects the two towns. The satnav's route-optimization algorithm selected it because it is the most direct path on the map. Nothing was hidden. Nothing was denied. The road was simply not encoded for the vehicle that would be sent down it.

This is not a software bug. It is the ordinary operation of a system that optimizes for one variable — distance — and treats everything else as a constraint to be added later, manually, after enough damage has been photographed and petitioned. Mike Martin, the local MP, gathered six hundred signatures before he could get a meeting with the mapping companies. Six hundred people saying: the map is wrong. Not wrong in the sense of containing false information. Wrong in the sense of being usable by some vehicles and not others, and not telling you which kind you are until you are already wedged on the bend.

Local authorities in Goudhurst discussed formally downgrading the classification of the A262 through the village. A classification change would automatically exclude the road from HGV routing algorithms rather than relying on manual exceptions added company by company. This is the difference between a field that must be added and a field that is already there. The manual exception requires someone to notice the failure, document it, petition, meet, convince. The classification change encodes the constraint into the structure itself.

The manual exception is a gift. The classification change is a right. The gift can be withdrawn. The right propagates.

Here Technologies added the restriction. Google did not confirm whether it had. TomTom did not confirm whether it had. The residents were asked to keep photographing. The gift was given to some systems and not others, and the residents became the mechanism for discovering which systems had received it.
