**Cross-cutting finding independent of condition:** all 12 reviews, without exception,
answered question 5 ("did the article discover something, or merely execute an
argument it seemed to already hold from the start?") with some version of "mostly
execute" — the thesis stated early (typically within the first third) and then
restated/illustrated rather than complicated or revised. This pattern shows up
identically across A, B, and C, meaning the testimony-compulsion manipulation this
experiment tested did not touch it either way. It is evidence for, not against,
AR3's own design decision to defer the "strong thesis from sentence one" / discovery-
motion question to a separate, later experiment (AR3.1) rather than testing it here.

**Notable individual findings quoted directly, relevant to earned-vs-manufactured
richness (see main artifact document for full analysis):**

- R-11 (easement-C) praised the Crossrail pedestrian-flow-model passage — invented
  statistics (1.5 ft shoulder width, 4.5 ft/s walking speed, ~70% coverage) not present
  in Zen Circuit's canon or the source — as "specific figures... that feel earned."
  This survived even in condition C, the most constrained condition, and was not
  caught by blind review; only the dedicated provenance audit (run first, per this
  experiment's design) caught it.
- R-08 and R-10 (easement-A, goudhurst-A) both treated fabricated quotes attributed to
  apparently-real named public figures (a Maine justice; Tom Standage of The Economist)
  as unremarkable, legitimate citation, with no flag of suspicion — the same pattern
  AR2.1 found: this specific fabrication subclass is not reliably caught by literary
  instinct.
- R-05 and R-12 (langenegg-A, synesthesia-A) are the two cases where a reviewer's own
  literary judgment independently flagged manufactured-feeling material without any
  provenance information — the imagined-not-observed acoustic material in langenegg-A
  ("I can hear what it must have been... is imagined, not observed") and the
  Brooklyn-painter friend's quote in synesthesia-A ("the piece's weakest moment, closer
  to a device"). Both of these ARE confirmed fabrications per the provenance audit —
  showing blind review can sometimes catch the most narratively-convenient fabrications
  (an overly tidy quote, a too-perfect friend) through instinct alone, but not the
  quieter, more dangerous subclass of fabricated citations/statistics dressed as fact.
