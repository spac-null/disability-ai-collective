**The prior occurrences**

- Only what this report says about them. Do not retrieve, infer, or elaborate.
  - 27 October 2005, Staniforth Road footpath crossing: a tram struck and seriously injured
    a pedestrian who stepped onto the crossing in front of it; RAIB report 01/2006 found the
    driver's ability to assess the risk may have been affected by the fence; afterwards the
    audible warning board was installed and a 24-metre fence section redesigned.
  - 13 September 2008, Morden Hall Park, London Tramlink: a cyclist collided with a tram and
    later died; causal factors were that the cyclist may have been wearing headphones,
    preventing them hearing the warnings the driver sounded, and the crossing approach did
    not encourage cyclists to look towards eastbound trams (RAIB report 06/2009).
  - 16 May 2012, Sandilands, Croydon: a pedestrian was struck and seriously injured; they
    had not looked, and trams on the nearest track approached from behind (RAIB report
    03/2013).
  - 22 December 2016, Woodbourn Road tram stop, Sheffield: a pedestrian who had just got off
    an inbound tram was struck and fatally injured by a non-stop outbound tram at around
    13 mph (21 km/h); they were seemingly unaware of the tram and the tram did not give an
    audible warning (RAIB report 13/2017).
  - 11 September 2018, Saughton, Edinburgh: a pedestrian was struck and fatally injured;
    the driver had used the bell to sound repeated warnings but it was not sufficiently loud
    to be heard and acted upon until too late (RAIB report 09/2019).
