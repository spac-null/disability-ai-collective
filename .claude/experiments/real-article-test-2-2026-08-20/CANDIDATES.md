# Real Article Test 2 — Candidate Shortlist

Three candidates. One selected. Screening stopped at three, per the brief.

## Edinburgh exclusion profile — what Test 2 must NOT primarily depend on

FORM-1.3's article depended on this mechanism stack (read directly from
`iterations/FORM-1.3/form1-3-writer-system.txt`, the frozen Form):

1. **Semantic arrival** — the piece lands on a distinction between two meanings of one
   word (*discovery*: the visitor's present encounter vs work unshown in its maker's lifetime).
2. **Correction geometry** — a reviewer's stated conviction is set against facts that
   narrow what the reviewer's own word can mean.
3. **Countervoice before arrival** — step 3 of the route: the reviewer's argument must
   "press on the reading you are forming, while that reading is still forming."
4. **Historical non-showing** — two artists who never exhibited in their lifetimes.
5. **Present encounter vs historical visibility** — the two time-frames are the whole hinge.
6. **A single found text as the object of reading** — the article reads a review.

**Reject any candidate whose likely article is:**
surface framing → forgotten/marginalised person → institutional correction → two meanings
of a word.

Also avoid replicating the three existing SOFA benchmarks: **FOX** (mediation selectively
loses reality), **HOUR** (a calculation assigns a property reality never contained),
**MOBILE** (a category becomes materially productive). A fourth story should surface a
mechanism not on that list.

## Candidate 1 — SELECTED

**SOURCE.** RAIB Report 10/2026, *Collision between a tram and two pedestrians at
Staniforth Road, Sheffield, 22 June 2025*. Published July 2026. 10,970 words of prose,
Crown copyright, single self-contained document.

**SUBJECT.** A tram/pedestrian collision at an unbarriered footpath crossing, and the
warning system the crossing's safety rests on.

**WHY CRIPMINDS.** The report contains one sentence that is the whole story if you read it
with the right instrument: *"Safe use of the crossing is reliant on pedestrians correctly
responding to the signage and markings, and observing **or** hearing approaching trams."*
That `or` presents two channels as interchangeable. They are not. Looking depends on a
sightline; hearing depends on a person deciding, in that moment, to press a button. The
report documents the discretion in detail and never names what it does to the `or`.

**LIKELY PERCEPTUAL INSTRUMENT.** A deafness-derived way of perceiving does not treat
sound as an ambient guarantee of the world. It treats hearing as a *channel* — something
that can be off, absent, ambiguous, or simply not aimed at you — and it knows that
**not hearing something is not evidence that it isn't there.** Applied here, that
instrument separates two things the report keeps joined: a signal that is *emitted* by a
machine, and a signal that is *authored* by a person who may or may not author it.

**WHAT DOMINANT FRAMING MISSES.** The dominant framing is attention: the pedestrians did
not look, the driver probably did not sound the bell, the signage did not alert them. All
true, all in the report. What it misses is that the crossing's safety case is an
*inference structure* — it asks the person crossing to treat silence as information. The
report's own five prior occurrences show the channel failing in both directions: twice no
warning was given (2016 Woodbourn Road, fatal; 2025 Staniforth Road), and twice a warning
*was* given and still did not work (2008 Morden Hall Park, fatal, headphones; 2018
Saughton, fatal, bell sounded repeatedly but not loud enough in time). Every remedy across
twenty years has been aimed at the act of warning, never at the status of the channel.

**MATERIAL AVAILABLE.** Exceptionally dense. Times to the second and minute (driver booked
on 09:46; collision ~16:14; pedestrians turned right ~1 second before impact; horn and
hazard brake less than a second before; tram stopped ~4 seconds after braking, ~12 metres
past the point of collision). Speeds (17 mph / 27 km/h at impact; 30 mph line speed; 15 mph
restriction). Distances (audible warning board ~30 m before the crossing; 45 m fence, of
which the final 5 m segregates pedestrians; one pedestrian pushed ~6 m under the tram).
Physical surfaces (brick paving on the crossing, ballasted track south, raised granite
cobbles north — which possibly worsened one pedestrian's injuries; tactile paving before
the crossing; 'LOOK BOTH WAYS' painted on the pavement and on three signs). Named
standards quoted verbatim (GMRT 2484 issue 2 (2007), superseded 2026 by GMRT 2131;
LRSSB-LRG 5.0 (2022); SYFTL's rule requiring an *"appropriate audible warning"* of
*"adequate length"*; training document MEI-CAT Phase Two). Recorder evidence (OTDR records
bell and horn **on the same channel**; no bell recorded at the warning board; horn recorded
under a second before impact; forward-facing CCTV; in-cab CCTV showing the driver's hand on
the control). Organisations (South Yorkshire Future Trams Ltd, South Yorkshire Mayoral
Combined Authority). Five prior occurrences with dates, places and outcomes. A 2005
accident at this same crossing, after which the audible warning board was installed. A 2017
RAIB recommendation, quoted, which RAIB says could have addressed this accident.

**NATURAL ARTICLE MOTION.** Reconstruction of a short sequence → the discretionary
structure of the signal → accumulation of prior occurrences that fail in both directions →
the twenty-year pattern in the *class* of remedy → a structural fact about the `or`.
Accumulation and recurrence, not correction and distinction.

**HOW IT DIFFERS FROM EDINBURGH.** No word with two meanings. No forgotten person, no
historical non-showing, no institutional correction of a marginalised figure. No
countervoice pressing before arrival — the report does not argue with itself; it agrees
with the article and simply stops short of the inference. Not a reading of a text but a
reconstruction of an event and a rule-set. The time structure is recurrence across twenty
years rather than present-encounter vs historical-visibility. The arrival is structural
(what a channel can carry), not semantic (what a word can mean).

**GROUNDING RISK.** Low. Everything is stated precisely and the boundaries are crisp. The
real risks are named and bounded in `GROUNDING-BOUNDARIES.md`: attributing blame or state
of mind to the driver, characterising two injured minors, importing anything from press
coverage or the five earlier reports, and inventing acoustic figures the report does not give.

**TEST VALUE.** High. It pressures Article Form on exactly the axis Edinburgh did not:
sequence, accumulation, physical fact, and an arrival that is a property of a system rather
than of a word. It also tests whether the architecture can keep a disability-derived
instrument *causally necessary but narratively optional* on material where no disabled
person appears at all.

## Candidate 2 — NOT SELECTED (reserve)

**SOURCE.** Koenecke et al., *Careless Whisper: Speech-to-Text Hallucination Harms*
(FAccT 2024, Cornell / U. Washington / NYU / U. Virginia), plus AP's October 2024
reporting on Whisper in hospital transcription.

**SUBJECT.** An automatic speech-to-text model that fabricates sentences that were never
spoken, at a higher rate on speech containing long pauses.

**WHY CRIPMINDS / INSTRUMENT.** A speech-disability-derived instrument treats the pause as
part of the utterance rather than as a gap between utterances. That instrument makes
visible that the model has no representation for *nothing was said*: it is a continuation
engine, so silence is not read as absence but as an unfinished sentence, and completed.

**WHAT DOMINANT FRAMING MISSES.** The framing is accuracy — the model "makes mistakes."
The mechanism is not mishearing but over-hearing: the failure is generative, not lossy.

**MATERIAL.** Strong: methodology, corpus (AphasiaBank), hallucination rates, verbatim
fabricated sentences, 40+ researchers, named deployments (Nabla; 85+ health organisations,
45,000 clinicians).

**NATURAL MOTION.** Accumulation of instances → a structural fact about the gap.

**HOW IT DIFFERS FROM EDINBURGH.** Substantially — no semantic distinction, no correction
geometry. **But** its mechanism sits close to **FOX**: a mediation system whose fidelity
varies with a property of what it records. The polarity is inverted (it adds rather than
loses) but the shape is adjacent.

**GROUNDING RISK.** Moderate-high. Secondary reports disagree on the headline rate (1% vs
1.4%), so number fidelity would be doing more work than Form fidelity.

**TEST VALUE.** Good, but confounded: the evidence literally is *speakers with aphasia got
more hallucinations*, which pulls hard toward "AI harms disabled people" — a reject
category. A transfer failure would be hard to attribute between Form and source pull.

**REJECTED BECAUSE:** mechanism adjacency to FOX, plus reject-category pull that would
confound the transfer result.

## Candidate 3 — NOT SELECTED (reserve)

**SOURCE.** RAIB Report 09/2026, *Dangerous occurrence involving a portable access ramp at
Norwood Junction station*, published 22 June 2026.

**SUBJECT.** A train departed with a portable boarding ramp still attached to it.

**WHY CRIPMINDS / INSTRUMENT.** A mobility-derived instrument distinguishes infrastructure
that *persists* from infrastructure that must be *summoned* each time. A ramp is the only
part of the station that has to be remembered.

**WHAT DOMINANT FRAMING MISSES.** The framing is human error (wrong ramp type, staff
unaware, driver possibly on a phone). The mechanism could be that the dispatch procedure
is built around two classes of object — things belonging to the train and things belonging
to the platform — and the ramp is a third that belongs to both and to neither, so no step
owns it.

**MATERIAL.** Good — RAIB prose density, no injuries.

**HOW IT DIFFERS FROM EDINBURGH.** Substantially: procedural, object-centred, no semantic
arrival.

**GROUNDING RISK.** Low.

**TEST VALUE.** Moderate.

**REJECTED BECAUSE:** the subject *is* an access service, so the article would be filed as
an accessibility story almost regardless of what it discovers. The lens would become the
topic rather than the instrument — failing the "causally necessary but narratively
optional" standard from the wrong direction. Candidate 1 gets the same instrument family
onto material where no disabled person appears at all, which is the stronger test.

## Also screened and dropped before shortlisting

- **HS2 construction noise/vibration monthly monitoring reports** (GOV.UK). Promising
  mechanism (the regime's unit of harm is a threshold event; the body's unit is duration),
  but the April 2026 report was verified as ~85–90% tables and charts with almost no prose.
  It fails the source requirement: the article could not move without invented connective
  tissue.
- **Altadena / Eaton Fire evacuation-alert investigations.** Dense and real, but the
  findings are actively contradictory (a California civil-rights investigation vs the
  Citygate report finding no delay and no bias), 17 deaths are involved, and the disability
  instrument would be strained. Unsuitable as a transfer-test article.
- **The existing project source pool** (`disability_findings.db`, 755 news seeds, 701
  unused). Checked first, as instructed. Date range 2026-04-05 to 2026-06-22 — stale — and
  the seeds hold only RSS `summary` text, not full articles. No candidate in it meets the
  source-density requirement without fresh fetching.
