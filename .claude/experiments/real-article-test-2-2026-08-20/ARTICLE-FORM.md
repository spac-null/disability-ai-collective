# Test 2 — Article Form

Designed after Discovery stabilised, from this material. Article Form owns material
selection, relationships among material, argumentative burden, sequence, reader path,
resistance, arrival and stop.

**This Form was not derived from FORM-1.3.** It was built by asking the questions in the
brief against this evidence, and it came out a different shape. The comparison to FORM-1.3
is recorded at the end as a result, not as a target.

---

## The questions, answered from this material

**What must the reader encounter first?**
The `or`. Not the accident. The report's own statement of what keeps people safe at this
crossing — signage, markings, and "observing or hearing approaching trams" — is the thing
the whole piece interrogates, and it is more useful to the reader as a premise than as a
revelation. Opening on the sentence gives the reader something to hold that everything
afterwards acts on. Opening on the collision would spend the strongest concrete material
before the reader has any reason to weigh it.

**What new question does that create?**
If those are two routes to the same knowledge, what is each one made of? That question is
answered entirely by facts the report supplies.

**What material changes the reader's interpretation?**
The discretion cluster: bell or horn is the driver's choice; the rule says "appropriate"
and "adequate length" and specifies neither; the recorder logs both devices on one channel.
Each fact narrows what the second half of the `or` can be. By the end of that cluster the
reader should be unable to read "hearing" as a property of the place.

**Is there resistance or correction at all?**
Not of the Edinburgh kind. Nothing in the source argues against the reading; RAIB documents
every fact the article uses. But there is genuine **resistance in the evidence**, and it
must be kept: at Morden Hall Park and at Saughton the warning **was** given and people still
died. That resists the easy version of this article — "the driver didn't ring the bell" —
and it must arrive while the reader is still forming that easy version, not after. This is
resistance from the material, not a countervoice from a speaker.

**Does the article spiral, accumulate, reverse, narrow, juxtapose, or something else?**
It **narrows, then accumulates, then recurs.** Narrowing: successive facts reduce what
"hearing" can mean. Accumulation: five prior occurrences pile up, failing in both
directions. Recurrence: the twenty-year sequence of remedies, all addressed to the same
layer. There is no reversal and no spiral.

**Where has the reader genuinely arrived?**
At a property of the channel, not of the accident: silence at this crossing does not
distinguish *no tram* from *a tram whose driver did not send the message*, and the people
who use it are taught by every safe crossing that it does.

**What must not come after that?**
Any remedy, any recommendation, any restatement, any widening to trams in general or to
other senses or other crossings.

---

## The route

Five movements. The writer is given the route, not section headings.

**1. The premise.**
Open on the crossing as the report describes it: no pedestrian light signals, no barrier,
and safe use "reliant on pedestrians correctly responding to the signage and markings, and
observing or hearing approaching trams." Give the place concretely — the fence and its
gradient drop, the tactile paving, LOOK BOTH WAYS painted on the pavement and on three
signs, the brick crossing surface between ballast and raised granite cobbles. End this
movement with the two channels named as the report names them, and no comment on them.

**2. What each channel is made of.**
Take the second one apart using only the report's facts, in this order: the audible warning
board about 30 metres out, and that it was installed after a 2005 accident at this same
crossing; that outside one named location the choice of bell or horn is the driver's; that
the rule asks for an "appropriate" warning of "adequate length" and the training document
repeats the same words; that RAIB's own post-accident observation found drivers using
either device and generally choosing louder and longer warnings in darkness; that the
recorder logs bell and horn on the same channel. Do not yet say what this means. The facts
should do the narrowing.

**3. The accident, in seconds.**
Now the event, told plainly and chronologically: the tram through Woodbourn Road without
stopping, the driver seeing two runners ahead on the pavement, the short hazard brake about
30 metres out, the signal at proceed, the turn to the right about a second before impact,
the horn and the hazard brake together less than a second before, 17 mph, one pedestrian
pushing the other clear, the other carried about 6 metres, the tram stopping about
12 metres past the point of collision. Then the two recorder findings that matter: no bell
logged at the board, and RAIB's "probably the case" that none was sounded; the horn logged
too late "to act as an effective warning." Keep RAIB's hedge exactly.

**4. The resistance, then the accumulation.**
Immediately, before the reader can settle into *the driver didn't ring the bell*: Morden
Hall Park in 2008, where the driver did sound warnings and a cyclist who may have been
wearing headphones died; Saughton in 2018, where the driver sounded repeated bell warnings
that were not loud enough to be acted on in time, and a pedestrian died. Then the others as
the report gives them: 2005 at this crossing, 2012 at Sandilands, and 2016 at Woodbourn Road
tram stop — the stop these two runners had themselves crossed at earlier the same run —
where no audible warning was given and a pedestrian was fatally injured. Then the twenty-year
line: the 2005 remedy was to install a board reminding a person to act; RAIB's 2017
recommendation after the 2016 death asked that all drivers work to the same requirements;
RAIB now says more effective implementation of it *could* have addressed the inconsistencies
that were a factor here.

**5. Arrival.**
Return to the `or` and land the distinction the material has made: looking is a property of
the crossing and hearing is a message with a sender; that the ordinary state of that channel
is silence; that these two had crossed this tramway fewer than ten times and had never
encountered a tram, so every silence they had heard there was true; and that a channel which
is almost always silent, and almost always silent truthfully, teaches the people who use it
that silence is information. Stop there.

---

## Burden and prohibitions for this Form

- The argument is carried by the report's facts in sequence. The article asserts nothing the
  report does not state; it only refuses to let the `or` stand unexamined.
- **No remedy.** The piece must not propose lights, barriers, automatic warnings, or better
  training, and must not say the accident was preventable.
- **No second arrival.** One landing, one statement of it. No restatement in different
  words, no "that is the distinction," no widening.
- **No disability example, no disabled person, no access framing.** The instrument is the
  way of reading, never the subject.
- **No cab scene, no interior life** for the driver or either pedestrian.
- **No moral.** Not about teenagers, not about the operator, not about institutions.
- Third person. No first-person narrator. No persona voice, no byline biography.
- Target 900–1,200 words. Movement 4 is the longest; movement 5 is short. If the arrival
  lands at 900, stop.

## How this Form differs from FORM-1.3 (result, not target)

| | FORM-1.3 (Edinburgh) | This Form (Staniforth Road) |
|---|---|---|
| Object | a found text (a review) read closely | an event and a rule-set reconstructed |
| Opening | the reviewer's experience of moving through the festival | the report's own statement of the safety case |
| Middle | two artists' facts, set beside the reviewer's conviction | a channel taken apart, then the event in seconds |
| Resistance | a countervoice — the reviewer's stated argument pressing on the forming reading | resistance from the evidence — prior cases where the warning *was* given and still failed |
| Motion | correction → narrowing of a word | narrowing → accumulation → recurrence |
| Time structure | present encounter vs historical visibility | twenty years of recurrence of one remedy class |
| Arrival | semantic: what *discovery* can mean | structural: what a channel can carry |
| Length | 550–700 words | 900–1,200 words |
| Movements | 5 | 5 |

Five movements in both is a coincidence of this material, not an inherited template; the
movements do different work and are differently weighted.
