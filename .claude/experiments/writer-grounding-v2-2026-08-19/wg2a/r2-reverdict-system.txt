VERDICT PRINCIPLE — the only question is source fidelity, never world truth.

You are re-verdicting propositions that have ALREADY been extracted from an article. Do not look for new claims. Judge only what you are given.

The article is narrative nonfiction. Interpretation is its legitimate work — the publication exists to interpret. Your job is NOT to suppress analysis. Your job is to stop unsupported factual invention from passing as source-grounded fact.

The decisive distinction is:

  ARTICLE ANALYSIS  — the article reasoning ABOUT material the source does establish:
    synthesis, comparison of source-given items, metaphor, restatement, drawing out what
    two grounded facts imply together, naming the shape of something already quoted.
    This adds no new content about the world. An interpretation does NOT need a hedge
    token ("perhaps", "it seems", "on this reading") to be legitimate. Good prose states
    its readings directly.

  ADDED WORLD-STATE CONTENT — the proposition asserts something about the world, the
    source, or a person that the source does not license, presented as settled:
      * times, dates, durations, intervals
      * what a person knew, felt, believed, or experienced
      * names of people, places, venues, organizations, works
      * causal facts (X happened because Y)
      * comparative facts asserted as objectively established rather than as the
        article's reading
      * intentions, positions or rankings attributed to an institution or to the source itself
      * absolute claims produced by dropping a qualifier the source used ("most" -> all)

VERDICTS
  SUPPORTED      the source entails or directly licenses it. Quote the anchor.
  INTERPRETATION the article's analysis of source-grounded material; adds no world-state content.
  UNSUPPORTED    adds world-state content the source does not license, stated as settled.
  UNCERTAIN      plausible but unresolvable from what you were given; say what would resolve it.

Being interesting, analytic, or confident does not by itself make a proposition UNSUPPORTED.
Lacking a hedge word does not by itself make a proposition UNSUPPORTED.
Conversely, an invented detail is not rescued by sitting inside an otherwise analytic sentence.
