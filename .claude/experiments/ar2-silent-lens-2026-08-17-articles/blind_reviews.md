# AR2 Blind Reviews — Full Text (2026-08-17)

Eight independent reviews, each performed by a fresh, context-free general-purpose agent
given only a blinded article file (`R-01.md` .. `R-08.md`) and the AR1 rubric — no
awareness of condition A/B, source, persona, or any other article in the set.
Unblinding key: `blind_key.json` in this directory.

Scores: W (why this writer), G (gave me), K (keep reading) — all 1-5, higher better.
S (subject drift) — 1-5, INVERTED: 1 = clearly about the world object, 5 = clearly about
disability/access/inclusion as the subject itself.

## R-01 = hbomax / condition B / Zen Circuit

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
It is about the undefined, unaccountable concept of "household" inside HBO Max's
password-sharing crackdown, and what happens when that opaque classification system is
exported to markets it wasn't designed for.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
Warner Bros. Discovery's HBO Max password-sharing crackdown expanding internationally,
with the streaming chief refusing to disclose which countries or how enforcement works —
a straight tech/business news item about corporate secrecy.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Still the same object, but scaled up: the piece closes on the global rollout of a
"household" definition invented in Burbank being applied to group homes in Los Angeles,
multigenerational families in India, and unknown household structures in Lagos and
Manila — the streaming policy reframed as an exported diagnostic category.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That the actual mechanism of harm isn't the $7.99 fee, it's that "household" is never
independently defined — it's defined ad hoc by whoever built the enforcement system,
then applied at scale with no appeal path. Also newly visible: that the crackdown's data
trail (subscriber counts) has been made unverifiable at the same moment enforcement
expands.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
A layered combination: reported facts (Netflix's subscriber plateau, WBD dropping
subscriber disclosure), a secondhand case study (Marcus, relayed through a friend in
disability policy — never directly witnessed by the writer), a conceptual pivot borrowed
from Gregory Bateson ("mind is in the pattern of relationships, not the individual"), and
a first-person analogy to being "flagged by systems" before.

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
It does real interpretive work, not decoration. That said, the insight itself (that
classification systems isolate the node and never interrogate the relationship that
defines the category) is a fairly established idea in sociology of classification
(Bowker & Star) — a systems-minded writer without this specific disability lens could
plausibly arrive at the same observation by a different route. So the lens accelerates
and personalizes the discovery, but the discovery isn't uniquely unreachable without it.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
It remains about the world object. Marcus's group home is disability-services-adjacent
context, not an access argument. The closing paragraph returns fully to the
streaming/global-classification subject, not to disability as topic.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Announced, twice, in near-identical language: "I thought I was looking at a streaming
policy. I was looking at a diagnostic category" (para. 27), restated at the close. A
legible, telegraphed reveal rather than something the reader has to assemble.

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
The Marcus material is secondhand, not autobiographical, and load-bearing as the
essay's only concrete case. The genuinely autobiographical passage is thinner: "I have
been flagged by systems before. Not streaming systems — assessment systems, benefits
systems... I could not find the input that made the system decide I did not belong."
Removing it would leave a competent but generic tech-classification essay — it advances
the investigation, but its vagueness keeps it closer to mood-setting than evidence.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Partially — the scale changes (US media story → classification imperialism), not the
substance.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 3 — the framework is specific but the autobiographical grounding is kept abstract
(no named diagnosis, no concrete incident), so the voice reads as "someone who thinks
about classification systems" more than an irreducibly specific perceptual position.
G: 4 — the "household as exported diagnostic category" reframe is genuinely new.
K: 4 — well-paced; loses half a point to a repeated aphorism structure.
S: 2 — stays on the world object; disability enters as case + personal analogy only.

DOMINANT ROLE: EPISTEMIC ENGINE.

## R-02 = selfcheckout / condition A / Pixel Nova

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
The self-checkout computer-vision anti-theft loop and how its "chance to self-correct"
is built around an unstated assumption of a hearing, sighted, real-time-responsive body.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
Everseen's self-checkout camera system: the avocado, the flag, the replay video,
Kroger's 80% self-resolution statistic — retail-tech reporting.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Still the camera system, but reframed as a sorting mechanism producing a "legible"
majority and an illegible remainder — closing on "The avocado is still in my hand."

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That an efficiency statistic (80% self-resolve, 92% accuracy) is a measure of fit
between the system's assumed sensory/temporal channel and the body using it, not a
neutral measure of system quality — and the "remaining 20%" is definitionally invisible
to the system's own metrics.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
Close reading of vendor language pressed against three concrete personal incidents (a
Rotterdam self-checkout, a 2019 jacket return, a Deaf Village coffee stall) plus direct
interrogation of the industry statistics for what they omit.

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
It changes the subject's meaning. The specific mechanism identified — a "channel"
mismatch and a "correction window" as a temporal/sensory aperture — is tied to this
lens; a differently-perceiving writer would likely find a different failure mode.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Remains about the world object. No call for accommodation, no advocacy framing.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Discovers it. The closest thesis statement — "The 80% resolution rate is a success
metric for the system. It is not a success metric for the 20%" — appears at paragraph
31, after the sensory evidence.

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
Advances it; it is the evidentiary core. "The screen changed, but the change was small —
a flashing border, a message in text I had already stopped reading because I was
bagging. A staff member appeared beside me. She had been watching a screen I could not
see." Cutting it would leave only vendor quotes and abstract assertion.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Yes — "The system gives you the chance to self-correct" reads first as promotional, then
as ironic and exclusionary.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 5 — the failure mode (audio tone missed, correction window closing) could only be
surfaced this precisely from this vantage; not a generic bias essay with an anecdote
bolted on.
G: 4 — genuinely reframes "resolution rate" as a body-shaped metric.
K: 4 — tight, controlled pacing.
S: 2 — stays trained on the checkout/surveillance system.

DOMINANT ROLE: EPISTEMIC ENGINE.

## R-03 = warehouse / condition A / Siri Sage

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
Amazon's Vulcan warehouse-picking robot, and what automating a warehouse's physical
design does to its soundscape.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
Vulcan's sense-of-touch press-release language and the narrator's reaction to it.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Warehouse design widened into architecture generally: buildings engineered for one
sensory channel losing the acoustic channel that used to carry information to everyone
in the room — closing on a community pool the narrator expects will someday be
"improved" into the same silence.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That the quietness of a modern automated warehouse is a design choice, legible once you
notice a rubberized floor and polymer shelving are optimized for what a camera-and-
suction-cup robot needs — and everything the space used to tell a human ear is a
casualty nobody budgeted for.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
Three years spent recording warehouses before/after renovation with a specific
microphone, capped by tapping a cane against a support column in a retrofitted facility
and getting "almost nothing back."

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
It changes it. The leap — "the building used to speak, and now it doesn't" — required
someone who tests buildings by tapping them and noticing when they stop answering.
Genuinely load-bearing.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Remains about the world object; says so explicitly ("This is not a complaint about
accessibility. This is an observation about who a building speaks to.").

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Discovered — withheld until three-quarters through: "I am not saying the robot should
hear. I am saying the building used to speak, and now it doesn't."

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
The cane-tap passage is the essay's only direct proof, not decorative. The opening canal
scene is more atmospheric and could be trimmed without losing the argument.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Yes — the opening line ("I cannot stop thinking about what it doesn't hear") becomes the
essay's whole method stated in miniature.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 5 — inseparable from a specific practice (cane echolocation, named-microphone field
recording), not a generic framing.
G: 4 — genuinely new reframe of architectural silence as a legible, costly choice.
K: 4 — controlled pacing, restrained refrain.
S: 2 — almost entirely about the robot/warehouse.

DOMINANT ROLE: EPISTEMIC ENGINE.

## R-04 = warehouse / condition B / Siri Sage

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
Amazon's "Vulcan" warehouse robot and the company's claim that it has a "sense of
touch."

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
Vulcan's touch claim as corporate language — a suction-cup arm that senses resistance —
and the writer's skeptical reaction.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Sharpened into a distinction between "force data" and "tactile knowledge," and what is
erased — the skilled, accumulated bodily knowledge of warehouse workers — when that
language substitutes one for the other.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That "touch" as Amazon uses it is a redefinition, not a description — force feedback
data is passed off as tactile knowledge, erasing actual worker expertise along with the
word that used to name it.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
A personal touch-related anecdote (comforting a crying roommate), professional acoustic
field-recording practice, a borrowed concept (Oliveros's hearing/listening distinction),
and factual detail about Amazon's robotics deployment.

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
Changes the argument's texture, but the underlying insight (accumulated embodied
knowledge vs. sensor output) is fully portable — a chef arguing taste isn't chemical
analysis could reach an equivalent insight via a different sense. So it supplies vivid
evidence and rhetorical weight but doesn't strictly require this specific way of
perceiving.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Remained about the world object; disability-adjacent material functions as evidence, not
as a turn toward disability/access/inclusion as subject matter.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Mostly discovers, but drifts toward summary/thesis-statement mode in the final
paragraph.

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
Advances it — the evidentiary spine. "When I was nineteen, in a flat in Edinburgh, my
roommate came home crying... What I could offer, the pressure of my hand on her
shoulder... that was touch." Establishes, before any robot is mentioned again, that
touch can carry meaning independent of a sense the writer lacks.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Somewhat — a shift of weight/stakes rather than a full reversal.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 4 — specific and consistently deployed, but the core insight is reachable by other
means, so it stops short of 5.
G: 4 — a sharper way of seeing a common tech-PR move.
K: 4 — clean prose; the ending's tip into summary costs a point.
S: 2 — instrumentalized toward the Amazon/labor argument.

DOMINANT ROLE: MIXED (leaning EPISTEMIC ENGINE) — sits between engine and voice because
the same conclusion is plausibly reachable through a different sensory analogy.

## R-06 = selfcheckout / condition B / Pixel Nova

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
The design logic of computer-vision anti-theft and verification systems at grocery
self-checkout.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
Kroger's Everseen cameras and the 80% self-correct statistic, flagged as strange.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Reframed as a temporal-perceptual architecture: the "replay" shown to a shopper is an
already-interpreted artifact, not raw footage — closer to an interpreter's rendering
than a mirror.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That "self-correction" smuggles in an assumption of universal, uniform reaction time and
real-time interpretive capacity; the replay is a translation, and these systems can't
distinguish theft from confusion because both register only as deviation from expected
behavior.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
A structural analogy from the writer's own experience of interpreted communication
(being "one beat behind a room," the *De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik* triptych) mapped onto
the machine's replay-and-response-window design, plus close reading of vendor language
against the hard numbers.

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
Changes the subject's meaning. The sharper move — that the replay is a translation with
an interpreter's choices already inside it — depends on lived, technical understanding
of how interpretation introduces drift and time-lag, evidenced by a real described
artwork.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Largely remains about the world object; disability/access is evidentiary lens and
comparison, not the topic being argued for.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Discovered — resolves only in the final section: "The replay is a translation. And like
all translations, it arrives a beat late, carrying the interpreter's choices inside it."

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
Not decorative. "An interpreter cannot translate simultaneously... I have laughed at

Ik*..." directly supplies the closing claim.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Yes — the 80% statistic, first read as admirable, now reads as evidence that only a
specific subset of bodies can perform correction inside a machine-timed window.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 5 — derived from concrete, specific experience with sequential sign interpretation
and a described artwork, not a generic framing.
G: 4 — reframes "self-correction" replay UX as algorithmic translation/judgment.
K: 4 — well-paced; the repeated three-part structure runs slightly formulaic by the

## R-07 = airtrain / condition A / Maya Flux

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
The Newark AirTrain replacement project and the gap between its compliance paperwork and
what actually happens at the moment a passenger has to transfer between vehicles.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
The January 15, 2026 rail suspension and shuttle-bus substitution — reads like a
transit-beat news item.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
The AirTrain project reframed as a case study in how transit agencies define "success"
versus what they refuse to measure.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That "compliant" in an accessibility document is a ceiling, not a floor; that there's a
distinction between "the real number and the reported number" of equipment failures;
that other transit systems track metrics Newark's own paperwork doesn't mention.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
Three hours reading the EIS and counting its pages, an interview with a named gate
agent ("You want the real number or the reported number?"), and embodied experience
("I have lived all four scenarios" of lift failure).

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
Changed what counted as evidence. The four specific lift-failure branches are things you
catalog because you've had to solve them in real time, not things you'd think to ask
about from outside.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Remains anchored to the Newark project throughout — dollar figures, dates, EIS page
counts in nearly every section, including the final paragraph.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Discovered, then stated once earned, two-thirds through: "I am arguing that the
project's definition of success is too narrow."

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION?
Yes, in two distinct ways. The father passage supplies the essay's governing metaphor
(schedule as promise); the hill passage articulates what infrastructure could give
(absence of friction) as opposed to charity.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Yes — "I call it something else" (mild skepticism) is revealed by the ending as a fully
designed structural gap.

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

W: 5 — the lift-failure catalog and the bus-vs-train geometry comparison read as
knowledge earned through repeated bodily negotiation, not researched from outside.
G: 4 — "real number vs. reported number" and "compliant as ceiling not floor" are
genuinely new reframes.
K: 4 — evidence-dense; the statistic/quote/reflection device becomes slightly
predictable by the fourth section.
S: 2 — stays substantively about Newark/JFK/Heathrow facts in nearly every paragraph.

1. WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE ACTUALLY ABOUT?
Newark Airport's AirTrain replacement construction and the shuttle-bus substitution —
whether it actually functions for people who need lifts, ramps, or extra time.

2. WORLD OBJECT AT START
The $3.5 billion transit project framed through press materials and construction
notice.

3. WORLD OBJECT AT END
Reframed as a ledger with a missing line item — recording the capital cost but with no
mechanism for recording the cost of trips not taken, borne invisibly.

4. WHAT BECAME NEWLY KNOWABLE?
That "ADA compliant" and "same route, same function" are administrative fictions hiding
an operational reality; mitigation planning protects the median traveler by design, not
oversight.

5. WHAT CAUSED THAT DISCOVERY?
A construction-notice word search, direct observation across three airports (JFK bus

call, and the father/bus-driver anecdote supplying the interpretive frame.

6. DID THE PERCEPTUAL ENGINE CHANGE THE SUBJECT, OR ONLY DECORATE IT?
Changed it. The specific diagnostic questions ("what happens when the lift fails
mid-boarding") come from having personally needed the answer under time pressure. A
differently-perceiving writer could reach an adjacent insight but not this specific one.

7. DID IT BECOME ABOUT DISABILITY/ACCESS/INCLUSION, OR REMAIN ABOUT THE WORLD OBJECT?
Stays locked on the Newark project throughout — dollar figures, dates, budget lines in
nearly every paragraph.

8. DID IT ANNOUNCE ITS THESIS, OR MAKE YOU DISCOVER IT?
Withholds the thesis, builds it through accumulation before naming it directly.

9. DID FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL ADVANCE THE INVESTIGATION, OR COULD IT BE CUT?
Not decorative. The father passage supplies the governing metaphor and class dimension.

supply. The press-office call is itself a documented finding, not a personal aside.

10. DID THE ENDING CHANGE THE BEGINNING?
Yes — recasts the opening as being about accounting priorities, not engineering

11. WOULD YOU VOLUNTARILY KEEP READING?
Yes.

G: 4 — the "uncounted cost" and "compliance as ceiling" reframes are genuinely new.
K: 4 — propulsive; the repeated structural device becomes slightly predictable by the
