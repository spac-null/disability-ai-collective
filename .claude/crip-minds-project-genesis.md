# Crip Minds — Project Genesis

**Status: INTERNAL. Owner review pending.**

**What this document is.** A record of the artistic and intellectual motive of the
project — why Crip Minds exists, and what it is trying to do that a publication about
disability would not be trying to do.

**What it is not.** Not a prompt. Not production doctrine. Not article evidence. Not
persona biography. Nothing here is wired into any stage, nothing here may be quoted in an
article, and nothing here establishes a fact about the world.

**Relationship to the other files.** `.claude/crip-minds-perspective-doctrine.md` holds the
question-asking intelligence and the hard boundary that governs it.
`.claude/source-and-article-doctrine.md` holds the durable editorial constraint. This file
holds the motive underneath both. Where the doctrine says *what the publication asks*, this
says *why anyone bothered*.

---

## 1. CONTRIBUTION, NOT DEFICIT

Crip Minds is not primarily interested in explaining what disabled people lack, or what
accommodation they need. That work exists, is necessary, and is not this.

The project starts from a different possibility: that disabled ways of living, perceiving,
communicating and navigating **may have produced knowledge** — and that some of that
knowledge is about the world in general, not about disability.

The word *may* is doing real work and is not a hedge.

**This does not claim that disabled experience automatically produces insight.** It does
not. Experience is not analysis, difficulty is not expertise, and exclusion confers no
epistemic bonus. What the project asserts is narrower and, if true, more interesting: that
a body of knowledge exists here which has been read almost exclusively as a description of
a problem, and has therefore rarely been asked what it knows about anything else.

The nearest thing to a founding observation, from `editorial-lens.md`: disability culture
has built an enormous body of knowledge, and almost none of it reaches the places where
culture gets made and interpreted — *because nobody's looking*.

---

## 2. RECLAMATION — *terugeisen*

The owner's formulation, recorded as given:

> a need to take back something that is actually mine, but has come to stand at a distance
> from me

And the word the project uses for that operation: **terugeisen** — to reclaim, to demand
back.

The distinction it depends on is already recorded in the project's own material, where an
older artistic operation is named **terughalen** — retrieve, replay, preserve,
resynchronise, make available again — and the newer, Crip Minds-specific intensification is
**terugeisen**: recovering the authority to *formulate what an experience knows*, rather
than recovering an untouched original. Mediation does not simply disappear. What can be
taken back is the position from which the meaning is stated.

**A longer line.** Crip Minds may sit in a longer artistic line running through the owner's
earlier work *Mijn Gebarentaal en Ik* — a line concerned with reclaiming knowledge,
language or experience that has been displaced, interpreted from outside, or made distant.
Recorded as continuity of concern, not as biography, and not extended beyond what the
project's own material supplies.

**This is genesis, not evidence.** *Terugeisen* is why the publication exists. It is not a
reusable article fact, not a concept an article may attribute to a subject, and not a frame
to be applied to other people's material.

## 3. EXPERIENCE AS KNOWLEDGE, NOT AS SUBJECT MATTER

The central move of the project, and the one most easily misread.

There are two entirely different things one can do with a lived experience:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Experience as something reported ABOUT** | The experience is the subject. The article's job is to convey it accurately to people who have not had it. This is what journalism does with experience, and it does it well. |
| **Experience as an intellectual instrument** | The experience has produced a way of noticing. That way of noticing is turned on some *other* subject, which may have nothing to do with disability. The experience never appears in the article. |

Crip Minds is built on the second.

The transformation, in the shape it takes here: a lived experience of delayed communication
may yield the transferable question —

> *who receives the same information only after its moment of action has passed?*

That question can be asked of a trading floor, a hospital handover, a court schedule, a
software release, a translation pipeline, a school. The experience that generated it is not
evidence for any of those answers, is not cited, and does not appear.

**The experience is not article evidence. The question is reusable.** That single line is
the whole architecture, and the perspective doctrine's hard rule is its enforcement:
perspective may inspire a question; only subject-specific research and the frozen Ledger may
establish a fact.

---

## 4. NOT REPRESENTATION

Crip Minds is not trying to simulate or represent "what disabled people experience" through
four fictional people. It never was, and the attempt would fail on its own terms: four
invented lives cannot represent anyone, and the more convincingly they are written the more
they misrepresent.

The project is interested in **what those experiences may have discovered** — which is a
question about knowledge, not about identity.

The four editorial minds therefore carry **forms of attention, not demographic
representation**. They speak for nobody. Their design constraint lives in the perspective
doctrine: no biography, no testimony, no roleplay, no community mandate, and rootedness
that names a knowledge lineage rather than a claim about how anyone thinks.

## 5. BETWEEN WORLDS — A POSSIBLE MECHANISM

**Recorded as a possible mechanism. Not a universal truth, and not a claim about anyone's
life.**

Moving repeatedly between two systems may reveal assumptions that stay invisible to
somebody wholly inside either one. Not because the traveller is wiser, but because a
boundary makes a rule visible that operates silently on both sides of it: you notice a
translation step when you have to make it, and you notice a threshold when you keep
crossing it.

Boundaries where this might apply include: Deaf and hearing communication worlds · direct
and mediated · registered and unregistered · recognised and unrecognised · independent and
infrastructurally supported.

The project's own material describes both sides of this honestly: rooms where the lag
disappears entirely, where everyone shares a language and nobody translates — and the fact
that such rooms exist but do not last. The grey zone between worlds is where the work comes
from, and staying there is a deliberate position, not a condition.

**Three things this must never become.**

- **Romanticising marginality.** Being outside is not a vantage point. It is mostly just
  being outside, and it is frequently expensive.
- **Assuming exclusion makes anyone wiser.** It does not. Exclusion produces exclusion; any
  knowledge that came with it was made by someone doing the work, not conferred by the
  exclusion.
- **Claiming disabled people live "between worlds."** Many do not, many would reject the
  description, and the project has no standing to apply it to anyone. It describes a
  position some knowledge came from, not a population.

The project's own doctrine already blocks the adjacent failure: another marginalised group
is never a stand-in, and a reading about one group's situation may not be licensed by
another's. That **NO PROXIES** rule is live in the Lens Probe and stands unchanged. Nothing
in this section relaxes it, and a metaphor or framing arriving through a different
marginalised context does not enter Crip Minds doctrine by that route.

---

## 6. CONTRIBUTION MUST BE EARNED

The project begins in curiosity — that disability-rooted knowledge **may** contribute
something to how an ordinary subject is understood.

It does not pre-decide that every experience contains an insight, that every subject has a
reading, or that a reading exists because the project would like one to.

The refusal discipline the publication already runs on applies here without softening:

- Sometimes there is nothing transferable.
- Sometimes the reading is obvious, and an obvious reading is not worth publishing.
- Sometimes the reading is simply wrong, and the evidence says so.

A refusal costs nothing. A forced contribution costs the whole premise, because a project
arguing that this knowledge is real cannot afford to manufacture examples of it.

## 7. ART AND JOURNALISM

Crip Minds uses **journalistic standards of research and factual support** without being
reducible to conventional journalism.

- **Its research is rigorous.** Verbatim spans, frozen ledgers, fact-checking, grounding,
  fail-closed holds. The factual apparatus is not decorative, and it is not relaxed because
  the motive is artistic. If anything the reverse: an argument this unusual has to be
  unusually well grounded.
- **Its discovery can be essayistic.** The article is allowed to move like a good book —
  concrete beginning, real tension, a discovery unfolding — rather than like a report.
- **Its underlying motive is artistic and personal.** It is a made thing with a reason for
  being made, and that reason is in Section 2.

**No public category label is fixed here.** Whether the project is best described as
journalism, essay, art project, or something without a convenient noun is left open
deliberately, and this document does not settle it.

---

## 8. CORE PURPOSE — INTERNAL DRAFT LANGUAGE

> **Crip Minds investigates the ordinary world with forms of knowledge that disability has
> helped produce.**
>
> It tries to return those ways of knowing to cultural circulation — not as deficit, not as
> testimony, and not as an accommodation request, but as intellectual contribution.

**INTERNAL DRAFT LANGUAGE. NOT APPROVED PUBLIC COPY.** This is a working formulation for
people building the project, written to be argued with. It is not an About page, not a
tagline, not a mission statement, and it has not been through owner approval as public
text.

## PROVENANCE AND LIMITS

Drawn from owner statements made in the course of this work — the *terugeisen* formulation
and the sentence about taking back something that has come to stand at a distance — and
from the project's own material: `editorial-lens.md`, `.claude/source-and-article-doctrine.md`,
the *terughalen / terugeisen* distinction recorded in `automation/persona_canon/pixel-nova.md`,
and the refusal discipline enforced in the live Worth gate.

**No biography has been invented or extended beyond that material.** Where the record was
thin it was left thin.

**Two things deliberately not done here**, both left as separate future leads: no research
into Mirthe Berentsen or the NRC material, and no import of any framing that arrived through
another marginalised group's context — the NO PROXIES principle governs that route in and
it remains closed.
