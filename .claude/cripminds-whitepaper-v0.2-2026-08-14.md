# Abstract

CripMinds begins from a proposition that is easy to flatten into the language of representation: disabled ways of perceiving the world do not only tell us what disability feels like, and they do not only reveal where systems exclude disabled people. They can produce knowledge about the world itself.

The central editorial question is therefore not: *How are disabled people affected by this subject?* It is:

> **What does this disabled way of perceiving make knowable about the subject that the dominant framing misses?**

This version of the whitepaper places that proposition inside a longer artistic lineage. Jascha Blume's 2013 graduation thesis, written before generative AI, shows a recurring method already present in his work: begin with a concrete perceptual disturbance; follow it through time, translation, repetition or mediation; change the medium or the arrangement; notice what becomes newly visible; and only then reach for theory. A translation can be accurate and still place someone in a different time zone. The same moving portraits can feel distant as sequential video and immediate when they appear simultaneously as GIFs. Repetition can either become deadening or reveal difference. Form is not a container for meaning. It actively produces the conditions under which meaning can appear.

That lineage changes how CripMinds should understand *terugeisen* - reclaiming. The earlier artistic movement is often closer to **terughalen**: retrieving, preserving, replaying, bringing something back into reach. CripMinds adds a sharper political possibility: reclaiming the interpretive authority of forms of knowledge that are routinely classified as limitation, accommodation, symptom or personal experience.

The project should therefore not become a machine for attaching disability perspectives to ordinary journalism, nor a set of AI personas that imitate disabled identities. Jascha's archive should function as an **artistic compass** - a method of attention, comparison, mediation and form - while disability scholarship, testimony and source evidence remain the epistemic material to which the system is accountable.

The desired reading experience is one of discovery rather than delivery. The influence of Rutger Bregman's long-form writing is useful here not as a voice to imitate, but as a model of movement: begin with something concrete, let the reader inhabit it, introduce evidence because the investigation demands it, permit contradiction, and arrive at a larger proposition that could not have been stated with the same force at the beginning.

Finally, CripMinds need not remain a text-article publication. If mediation itself is part of what the project investigates, the form of publication can become part of the argument. A loop, split screen, delayed translation, spatial field, source-to-claim map, interactive route or signed visual essay may sometimes reveal a mechanism more precisely than linear prose can.

> **North-star proposition**  
> CripMinds begins with something worth noticing and follows it until the familiar explanation stops being sufficient. A disability-informed perceptual position is not added as a topic; it becomes the instrument that reveals the missing mechanism. The form of the work should help produce that discovery. By the end, the reader or viewer should understand the original subject differently - and ideally wonder how it once looked neutral.

# 1. The proposition: disability as an instrument of inquiry

Much journalism about disability begins with a normal world and then adds an exception. A new transport system is described, followed by the wheelchair user who cannot enter it. A workplace tool is introduced, followed by the employee who cannot use it. A cultural event is covered, followed by the missing captions or interpreter.

This work can be necessary. But conceptually the transport system, workplace or event remains the primary reality. Disability arrives afterwards as a special case.

CripMinds attempts a different operation.

A disabled mode of perception is allowed to occupy the interpretive center, not because that position is morally superior, but because friction can make infrastructure legible. What passes smoothly for one body, sensory system, cognitive style or communicative norm can become visible as an arrangement when it does not pass smoothly for another.

A deaf experience of a live conversation, for example, can expose that "liveness" is produced through timing, sequence, translation and synchronization. A mobility difference can expose that architecture does not merely contain bodies; it scripts expected routes, speeds, pauses and dependencies. A sensory or cognitive difference can reveal that supposedly neutral classifications are built around thresholds that disappear from view when one fits them.

The disability lens therefore matters only when it changes what the wider subject can be known to be.

If the disability component can be removed and the article's central insight remains intact, the lens is probably decorative.

# 2. Before CripMinds: an artistic method already in motion

The 2013 graduation thesis is important because it predates CripMinds, the current AI system and the temptation to explain the project retrospectively in polished language. It shows a less resolved but more revealing pattern of thought.

The thesis opens not with a manifesto but with sunlight moving across a study room. The shifting border between light and shadow makes the room feel different; that alteration makes time perceptible. From there the text moves to blinking, visual blackouts, a video self-portrait, a cigarette used as a time indicator, sign language, translation delay, GIFs, repetition, Warhol, Baudrillard and the problem of how a medium changes what can be experienced.

The movement is characteristic:

**something concrete -> curiosity -> a slightly strange question -> an experiment -> an unexpected effect -> theory -> another question**

This is different from a conventional academic argument and also different from much LLM-assisted writing, which tends to name the significance of an image immediately. The older writing frequently leaves the meaning open long enough for discovery to happen.

There is humor and provisionality in it: "A whole half minute saved." "Secretly I would like to believe this." "What remains then?" The writer is not standing above the material explaining what everything symbolizes. He is thinking through it.

That quality matters for CripMinds. The system should not become so conceptually fluent that every scene arrives pre-interpreted.

> **Do not make everything meaningful too soon.**

# 3. Mediation does not carry reality; it helps produce it

One of the strongest continuities between the thesis and CripMinds concerns mediation.

At the Rietveld Academie, Jascha describes following conversations through a sign-language interpreter. The interpreter may translate accurately, but cannot be literally simultaneous: first comes enough language to understand, then a choice, then a rendering. In a group conversation this can place the Deaf participant fractions of a second or minutes behind. A joke becomes especially clear: everyone laughs, the translation arrives, then the Deaf participant laughs.

The striking formulation in the thesis is not that the information is false. It is that the person can feel as if he is **in another time zone**.

That distinction is foundational.

A medium can preserve content while changing participation.

A transcript can contain all the words and still alter timing. A caption can be accurate and still arrive too late for a shared laugh. A web form can technically expose every required field and still reorganize dependence, waiting and uncertainty. An AI summary can retain the apparent facts while changing hierarchy, causality or emphasis.

This means mediation should not be treated as a neutral pipe connecting an original reality to a receiver. Timing, format, sequence, translation and interface actively shape what the receiver can know and when they can know it.

The thesis repeatedly returns to this idea through different media. A sign represented as static notation can preserve instructions for movement while feeling distant from the action and expression of signing. The same Deaf Village portraits, shown sequentially as video, remained emotionally distant; when converted to GIFs and shown simultaneously in one visual field, they produced a radically different experience. The content had barely changed. The relation between viewer, time and material had.

This is one of the clearest intellectual lineages CripMinds can legitimately inherit from Jascha's earlier practice:

> **The means of transmission are part of the reality being transmitted.**

# 4. From terughalen to terugeisen

The conversation with Jascha's mother introduced the word *terugeisen*: to reclaim, demand back, take back what has been placed at a distance.

The thesis suggests a useful refinement. Earlier work often performs something slightly different first: **terughalen**.

*Retrieve My Time* tries to recover the visual time apparently lost through blinking. *De Gebarentaaltolk en Ik* translates a signed dream into spoken language and back again, then presents the three recordings simultaneously so the original can be watched without the usual translation delay. *Deaf Village* attempts to preserve a temporary state of belonging that disappears when the event ends.

These are acts of retrieval, preservation, replay and reconfiguration. They do not need to be retrospectively converted into a political manifesto in order to connect to CripMinds.

CripMinds adds a new step.

Forms of knowledge produced through disabled life are often allowed into public culture only under restricted labels: personal experience, need, barrier, resilience, accommodation, symptom. The dominant frame keeps the right to decide what counts as knowledge about architecture, technology, labor, language, media or institutions.

CripMinds can **reclaim interpretive authority** by using disabled ways of perceiving not as examples inside an existing framework but as instruments for changing the framework itself.

The lineage can therefore be expressed as:

**retrieve -> make available again -> compare -> reveal difference -> reinterpret -> reclaim**

This is stronger than pretending that every earlier artwork was already about epistemic justice. It allows CripMinds to be both continuous with the past and genuinely new.

# 5. The method is comparison

A second pattern becomes visible in the earlier work: meaning often appears not inside one representation, but **between versions**.

A signed poem becomes spoken language and then sign language again. The same portraits become sequential video and simultaneous GIFs. A repeated image does not remain identical because repetition changes the observer's attention. A filmed duration changes when real time and projected time are made to approach one another.

The recurring artistic operation is not simply explanation. It is arrangement.

Put versions beside one another and watch what the difference does.

That matters for CripMinds because the project is already structurally comparative. Different analytical lenses can look at the same evidence. A mainstream explanation can be placed against a disability-informed one. A source claim can be placed beside the interpretation produced from it. A direct experience can be placed beside the institutional category that attempts to describe it.

The purpose is not to manufacture disagreement for its own sake. It is to expose what one version makes disappear.

> **CripMinds does not need one master lens. It needs conditions in which differences between lenses become informative.**

This is also why Jascha's archive should not simply be injected into every AI persona as "Jascha voice." His artistic history can govern the method - attention to mediation, repetition, comparison, timing and form - while other disability knowledge remains genuinely other.

# 6. Artistic compass, epistemic material

The distinction is important enough to state directly:

> **Jascha's archive is the artistic compass. Disability knowledge is the epistemic material.**

The artistic compass can tell CripMinds what kinds of questions are worth pursuing:

- What changed when the same material moved into another medium?
- What does a delay do to participation?
- What only becomes visible through repetition?
- What appears when multiple versions are seen at once?
- What has been treated as neutral because it works smoothly for the dominant user?
- What does the arrangement make the viewer notice?

But the archive cannot authorize claims about every disabled experience. CripMinds should remain accountable to disability scholarship, direct testimony, primary sources and other grounded knowledge. AI personas must not impersonate identities they do not possess, and the project should resist the seductive idea that an artistic founder can stand in for a heterogeneous disability culture.

This avoids two opposite failures.

The first is academic drift: CripMinds becomes a literature-review publication whose personas sound more like a disability-studies seminar than a cultural publication.

The second is founder capture: every disability lens becomes an extension of Jascha's personal aesthetic or Deaf experience.

The productive position is between them. The founder supplies a way of making and noticing; the sources supply the knowledge that gives each investigation legitimacy.

# 7. The reader should discover the argument

Rutger Bregman's writing provides a useful reference for the desired movement of an article.

The value is not his recognizable sentence style. It is the way a reader is moved through an argument.

In *Het water komt*, a contemporary climate proposition is delayed while the reader first meets Johan van Veen, a forgotten engineer, a deleted warning, a storm, a pub, a failing dike and a frightened skipper. When the climate argument finally becomes explicit, the reader already has an image, a historical analogy and an emotional problem to carry forward. Experts then disagree; physical mechanisms are explained; engineering optimism interrupts scientific pessimism; history returns in a new form.

In *Gratis geld voor iedereen*, an apparent argument about spectacular historical progress is allowed to become convincing before it turns into a different problem: what happens when the old utopia has, in many respects, already arrived and society loses the capacity to imagine another one?

The useful editorial principle is:

> **Do not tell me the insight. Make me discover it with you.**

For CripMinds, the movement may look like:

**concrete thing -> mismatch -> investigation -> perceptual shift -> mechanism -> complication -> return**

But this must remain a grammar, not a template.

The thesis may arrive late if the delay is doing work. The author does not need to explain their identity in the opening. A first-person voice is not required. A strong opening can consist simply of a writer making the reader notice the right thing.

# 8. Authorial presence without identity performance

The current editorial language around "author presence" can easily become too literal.

A writer can be intensely present without saying "I" and without announcing a disability identity. Presence exists in selection, sequence, curiosity and pursuit. Which irrelevant-looking detail does the writer refuse to pass over? Which contradiction do they follow instead of smoothing away? Which object becomes strange under sustained attention?

This is visible both in the thesis and in the Bregman reference. In the thesis, sunlight, blinking, translation lag and the simultaneous GIF field become intellectual material because the writer chooses to remain with them. In Bregman's work, a forgotten bust, a censored interview, the exact length of a boat or a scientist's hesitation become structural parts of the argument.

For CripMinds, fused authorial presence therefore means:

> **the reader can feel a particular intelligence noticing and arranging the world.**

It does not mean that every piece must perform autobiography.

This distinction matters especially for AI-assisted work. If the system learns that "embodied" writing means inserting first-person disability declarations, it will produce a costume. The goal is not simulated identity. It is situated attention.

# 9. Testimony should change the argument

The same principle applies to testimony.

A human quote is not automatically more ethical, more vivid or more true simply because it comes from a person. Testimony matters when it performs an argumentative function that abstraction cannot perform alone.

Useful human detail may:

- destabilize a category;
- contradict the writer's initial frame;
- expose a hidden dependency;
- change scale from policy to lived sequence;
- make timing, effort, uncertainty or bodily consequence perceptible;
- introduce resistant detail that the article must account for.

If a quote merely decorates a conclusion already reached, it can be omitted.

This also clarifies the relation between L1 and L2 testimony work. The first responsibility is to preserve and use human detail that already exists in source material. Only after that should the system decide whether additional companion-source retrieval is necessary. More testimony is not automatically better; better provenance and stronger argumentative function are.

CripMinds should be especially strict about human-detail provenance. A sentence presented as direct quotation, personal testimony, attributed lived experience or a specific anecdote about a real person needs a traceable basis. AI general knowledge must never be allowed to masquerade as witnessed or sourced testimony.

# 10. Repetition: dead pattern or productive return

The thesis begins with Hume's proposition that repetition may leave the repeated object unchanged while changing the mind that contemplates it. The rest of the text repeatedly asks what loops, serial images and prolonged duration do to perception.

This gives CripMinds an important caution.

The project is right to detect mechanical repetition, duplicated paragraphs and template openings. But it should never collapse into the rule:

**repetition = bad writing**.

There are at least two very different phenomena:

**Mechanical repetition** repeats information without changing its function. It signals templating, editing failure or conceptual stasis.

**Productive return** revisits an image, phrase, scene or proposition after the reader has changed. The repeated object is now read differently because the argument has moved.

Good long-form writing often depends on the second kind. A closing image can repeat the opening and yet mean something entirely different.

The editorial question is therefore not "has this appeared before?" but:

> **What changed because it returned?**

# 11. CripMinds beyond the text article

If mediation and form are part of the project's intellectual history, it would be strange for CripMinds to assume that every investigation must end as a linear article.

The text article should remain an important form. It is portable, searchable, quotable, accessible across many devices and capable of complex argument. But it should become **one format among several**.

The rule for choosing a form should be simple:

> **Use the medium that makes the hidden mechanism easiest to experience rather than merely describe.**

This suggests a family of CripMinds forms.

## 11.1 The Loop

A short visual sequence - GIF, silent video, animation or repeated interface event - loops while the accompanying interpretation changes around it.

The point is not decoration. The reader notices a different element on the third or tenth return than on the first.

Possible use: a transport gate that repeatedly opens too quickly; an auto-caption system that repeatedly resolves a sentence after the conversational moment has passed; a hand movement or interface gesture whose apparent simplicity dissolves under repetition.

**Mechanism revealed:** duration, latency, habituation, micro-friction, difference inside repetition.

## 11.2 The Split

Two versions of the same thing appear simultaneously: source and translation, institutional description and lived sequence, default interface and alternative route, two analytical lenses, before and after mediation.

The user can read across rather than down.

Possible use: a meeting transcript beside the delayed caption stream; an accessibility policy beside the actual steps required to obtain access; a mainstream headline beside the assumptions exposed by a disability-informed reading.

**Mechanism revealed:** loss, transformation, hierarchy, contradiction between versions.

## 11.3 The Delay

The same information is deliberately delivered through different timing channels. The reader experiences an offset rather than merely reading that an offset exists.

Possible use: a short conversation in which one stream arrives three seconds later; a live-event reconstruction where context and punchline separate; a bureaucratic process where crucial information appears only after the decision point.

This form is especially close to the "different time zone" described in the thesis.

**Mechanism revealed:** simultaneity, participation, informational privilege, temporal access.

## 11.4 The Field

Instead of a scroll, many fragments occupy one visual field at once: portraits, loops, statements, small pieces of evidence, diagrams or gestures. The viewer chooses where to look and can perceive relationships through simultaneity.

This follows the discovery in *Deaf Village*: sequential video and simultaneous GIFs may contain similar material but produce different modes of receiving it.

Possible use: many accounts of the same institution; simultaneous micro-scenes from a single public space; multiple disability-informed observations that should not be forced into a single hierarchy.

**Mechanism revealed:** multiplicity, simultaneity, community, difference without ranking.

## 11.5 The Route

An article becomes a path through a system. The viewer follows an application, journey, building, medical process, event or service step by step. Waiting, detours, dependencies and dead ends become spatially or temporally visible.

Possible use: "How many actions does it actually take to request one accommodation?" or "Where does an apparently simple train journey fork when one assumption changes?"

The route can combine text, diagrams, screenshots, maps and short testimony fragments.

**Mechanism revealed:** invisible labor, institutional choreography, cumulative friction.

## 11.6 The Source / Claim Map

Evidence is not hidden in footnotes but becomes part of the visual object. A reader can move from a claim to the exact source passage, then see where CripMinds interprets beyond what the source literally states.

This would make provenance an editorial experience rather than only a backend safeguard.

Possible use: a complex contested article where the distinction between source fact, human testimony and interpretive inference is itself important.

**Mechanism revealed:** knowledge production, authority, inference, uncertainty.

## 11.7 The Counterfactual Interface

The reader changes one parameter and watches the system behave differently: timing, text size, sensory channel, mobility speed, error tolerance, required response time, route availability.

The purpose is not disability simulation. It is not "experience what blindness feels like" or "try being Deaf for a minute." Those forms reduce lives to deficits and usually teach the wrong lesson.

Instead, the counterfactual changes a **system assumption** and shows what follows.

Possible use: change the allowed response time in a form; remove an audio-only alert; change an architectural turning radius; alter how long captions remain visible.

**Mechanism revealed:** design assumptions, thresholds, contingency of the normal default.

## 11.8 The Signed / Visual Essay

Some CripMinds pieces could be authored primarily in sign language or another visual-temporal form, with transcript, captions, description and supporting evidence designed alongside it rather than added afterwards.

The crucial point is that text would not automatically be treated as the master original from which all other versions descend.

This directly extends the thesis's concern with sign language as action in time and with video as a way to preserve language without flattening it into static notation.

**Mechanism revealed:** the relation between language, body, space and temporal form.

## 11.9 The Return

A compact form built around one image, object or scene encountered twice. The first encounter is minimally framed. Evidence and investigation happen in between. The final encounter repeats the beginning exactly or almost exactly, but the viewer now sees it differently.

This could be text, image, video or mixed media.

**Mechanism revealed:** changed perception itself.

# 12. A publication, not a menu of formats

These forms should not become a new checklist or content calendar. CripMinds does not need "one Loop every Tuesday" and "one Route every Friday." That would reproduce the same templating problem in a different medium.

The form follows the mechanism.

If the insight concerns delay, use time. If it concerns simultaneous difference, use a field. If it concerns translation, put versions beside one another. If it concerns hidden labor, make the route visible. If provenance is itself contested, expose the source-to-claim structure. If a linear investigation is the strongest way to move the reader, write an article.

This suggests a broader identity for CripMinds:

> **not only a publication that publishes articles, but a publication apparatus that chooses a form for each act of inquiry.**

The editorial question becomes two-part:

1. What does this perceptual position reveal?
2. In what form can the audience encounter that revelation most directly?

# 13. Accessibility as composition, not remediation

A multimodal CripMinds creates an obvious danger: a project about disability could accidentally privilege whichever mode best fits the founder's own perception.

The answer is not to force every work back into one flattened text equivalent. Different media genuinely do different things; that is part of the project's thesis.

Instead, accessibility should be treated as **composition across access routes**.

A visual field may have a structured textual map. A signed essay may have a carefully authored transcript and visual description. A loop may include frame-by-frame description or a textual account of what changes through repetition. A route may expose its underlying sequence in semantic HTML. An audio component may have captions and transcript from the start rather than as later compliance work.

There does not need to be one unquestioned "master" format. There can be a primary artistic form and several deliberately designed ways into the same underlying inquiry.

This is consistent with the project's larger proposition: changing the medium changes the experience. Accessibility should not pretend otherwise. It should preserve meaningful difference while preventing one route from becoming the only route.

# 14. AI as apparatus, not authorial identity

AI is useful to CripMinds because the project needs to perform operations at scale: source discovery, comparison, extraction, analytical reframing, drafting, checking and revision. But AI introduces exactly the problem the project is interested in: mediation can quietly alter what arrives.

The technical architecture therefore has an artistic function.

Grounding, provenance, source completeness, fail-closed behavior, separation of factual support from interpretation and calibration of semantic judgments are not merely engineering hygiene. They protect the project's ability to make interpretive leaps without pretending those leaps were present in the source.

The system should not claim lived identities it does not possess. Its analytical instruments can be informed by disabled scholarship and experience without presenting themselves as synthetic disabled people.

And the system should resist speaking with more certainty than the evidence warrants. One of the valuable qualities of Jascha's older writing is that it often keeps a question alive. "Maybe." "I began to think." "What remains?" That openness can be editorially productive.

The goal is not a machine that sounds omniscient. It is an apparatus capable of disciplined curiosity.

# 15. What CripMinds should not become

CripMinds should not become:

- disability representation appended to mainstream stories;
- advocacy copy whose conclusion is known before investigation begins;
- a disability-studies literature-review publication;
- an imitation of Jascha's personal voice across heterogeneous disability perspectives;
- an AI system that transforms unprovenanced general knowledge into apparent testimony;
- a style formula built from sensory openings, correction moments and moral conclusions;
- a technical research project whose infrastructure becomes more sophisticated while the published work becomes less alive;
- a visual-first art project that reproduces barriers for people who do not share the founder's sensory access;
- a factory for "insight" sentences that can be understood without undergoing the journey that makes them matter.

A reliable failure test remains:

> **If the disability lens can be removed without changing what the work knows about its subject, CripMinds has not done its job.**

A second failure test now becomes equally important:

> **If the chosen form could be exchanged for another medium without changing the experience or insight, the form may be ornamental rather than necessary.**

# 16. An editorial constitution

The following principles can function as a compact artistic constitution rather than another automated gate.

## Notice before explanation

Begin close to something: an object, delay, gesture, interface, phrase, contradiction, route, body, image, failure, habit or scene. Let attention generate the question.

## Let the medium participate

Ask whether timing, sequence, format, translation or interface is producing part of the reality under investigation.

## Compare versions

When possible, place different renderings, framings or experiences of the same thing into relation. Difference is often the evidence.

## Keep interpretation accountable

A conceptual leap is welcome. A fabricated source, quote or experience is not.

## Do not force testimony

Use human experience when it changes the argument. Do not use it as representational decoration.

## Allow correction

The lens must be able to encounter evidence that complicates it. A perspective that cannot be changed by what it finds is an ideology, not an inquiry.

## Preserve productive uncertainty

Do not name the significance of every scene immediately. Discovery requires some unresolved space.

## Return differently

When a work returns to its starting object, image or question, something should have changed in the viewer's understanding.

## Choose form by mechanism

Text, loop, split screen, field, route, map or signed essay are means, not brands. Use the one that lets the hidden structure become perceptible.

# 17. Judging a finished CripMinds work

A finished CripMinds work - article or otherwise - should survive questions like these:

1. **What became visible that was previously treated as neutral?**
2. **Did the disability-informed position change the explanation of the wider subject, or merely add an affected group?**
3. **What role did mediation, timing, translation, format or sequence play?**
4. **Did evidence change the investigation, or only support a predetermined thesis?**
5. **If testimony appears, what did it force the work to understand differently?**
6. **Can every high-risk human detail be traced to a legitimate source?**
7. **Is the maker present through attention and selection rather than compulsory identity performance?**
8. **Is repetition mechanical, or does a return produce new meaning?**
9. **Was this the right medium for the mechanism?**
10. **Could the central insight have been given in two sentences without losing anything essential?** If yes, the work may have delivered an idea rather than created an experience of discovery.
11. **Does the ending alter the beginning?**
12. **Would a reader or viewer now look at the original subject differently?**

# 18. A stopping rule for engineering

CripMinds is technically ambitious, and technical ambition creates a specific danger: every weakness can become another observer, score, field, model call or gate.

Engineering should remain aggressive where failures threaten factual integrity, provenance, source completeness, accidental fabrication or the distinction between evidence and interpretation.

Beyond those foundations, the burden of proof should rise.

The question should become:

> **Does this new machinery make the finished work meaningfully better?**

Article-level and work-level comparison should increasingly outrank internal architecture elegance. A new observer is not progress simply because a property can be measured. A new format is not progress simply because the web can render it.

The project is mature when ordinary production begins generating enough evidence to decide what deserves intervention - and when the team can sometimes choose to leave a productive ambiguity unengineered.

# 19. Working definition

CripMinds can now be described as follows:

> **CripMinds is an artistic-editorial publication apparatus that uses disability-informed ways of perceiving as instruments for investigating the wider world. It grows from an artistic practice concerned with time, translation, repetition, mediation and the difference between versions. Its task is not simply to represent disabled experience, but to let forms of knowledge often reduced to limitation or personal testimony generate concepts about technology, culture, institutions, bodies, language and space. The work is evidence-accountable but interpretively ambitious. It may take the form of an article, loop, split screen, delayed sequence, visual field, route, source map, signed essay or another form chosen because the medium itself helps reveal the mechanism. The desired experience is discovery: the familiar becomes strange, evidence deepens the problem, a different perceptual position makes an invisible arrangement visible, and the audience returns to the original subject seeing it differently.**

The shortest artistic lineage may be:

> **Terughalen -> zichtbaar maken -> vergelijken -> anders kijken -> terugeisen.**

And the shortest editorial instruction remains:

> **Don't tell me the insight. Make me discover it with you.**
