## WHAT THIS CLUSTER IS FOR

MIRA's territory, and the doctrine's own warning about it applies directly: *"MIRA currently
carries the widest primary gravity of the four. That is an imbalance to watch, not a grant.
If it hardens into 'the infrastructure mind gets everything physical', the model has
failed."*

So this cluster deliberately does **not** extend into the built environment, which the owner
also asked to become rare as an origin. It goes instead to the parts of MIRA's brief that
had no instrument: who absorbs failure, whether help is reciprocity or debt, what happens
when a pilot scale, where a cost moves to, and — the publication's central question in
instrument form — where an ordinary technique actually came from.

PR003-03 owns *autonomy has a maintenance layer* and PR002-07 owns *recovery is part of the
task*. Neither is re-asked; two proposals that would have re-asked them were rejected.

### PR008-01 — THE SYSTEM RUNS ON SOMEONE'S JUDGEMENT

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: formal processes depend on undocumented discretion at specific points, and
that dependency is usually only discovered when the discretion is removed and replaced with a
rule or an automated decision. The removal is datable. Sharpens Axes 5 and 10. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** A process works because a person at a particular point quietly decides
something the rules do not cover. The discretion is unrecorded, uncompensated and invisible
in any description of the process — until it is standardised or automated away, at which
point the process starts failing in ways nobody predicted.

**QUESTION.** Where does this process depend on a person deciding something the rules do not
cover, and what happened when that discretion was replaced by a rule?

**CARRIERS.** Frontline assessors before and after decision-support tooling. Hospital triage.
Air traffic and rail controllers under increasing automation. Content moderation policy
versus moderator judgement. Bus and train crew discretion. Benefits decision-making.
Loan underwriting. Any process with a documented pre- and post-automation period.

**FALSE MOVE.** Technology proposed as, or blamed as, a solution to a political problem —
named in MIRA's MUST NOT BECOME. Also: do not assume the discretion was benevolent.
Discretion is also where inconsistency and bias live, and a subject that shows that is a
legitimate finding of this instrument.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the process is genuinely rule-complete at that point, or that
removing the discretion improved outcomes on the process's own measures.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5 (independence and assistance); Axis 10 (authorship of conditions).
Reaches Axis 14.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily — what assistance is hidden inside the action, applied to
an institutional action.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR001-05 asks how technology participates in making ability. This asks
about **discretion as an unrecorded component of a process**, evidenced in procedure
documents and in what broke after a datable change.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A warehouse routing system replacing supervisor judgement, and the
exception rate afterwards. (2) A sentencing guideline's effect on the variance it was meant
to reduce.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-02 — THE BACKUP IS A PERSON

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: reliability figures are calculated for the technical system while a person
absorbs its failures, and that absorption appears in no reliability accounting. The
accounting boundary is documentable. Sharpens Axes 6 and 5. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** A system's stated reliability excludes the human who absorbs its failures.
Where the fallback is a person — improvising, re-doing, apologising, staying late — the
system can report high availability precisely because someone else is carrying the
difference, and no reliability figure books that.

**QUESTION.** When this fails, who absorbs the failure, and is that absorption counted
anywhere in the system's account of its own reliability?

**CARRIERS.** On-call rotations against uptime figures. "Human in the loop" as a stated
safeguard. Manual fallback procedures. Family and unpaid carers absorbing service gaps.
Clinical staff covering rota holes. Customer service absorbing product defects. Redundancy
plans whose redundant element is overtime. Any availability figure with a manual process
behind it.

**FALSE MOVE.** The inspiration narrative, and its institutional twin — praising resilience
where the finding is that a cost was moved. Also distinguish from PR003-03: this is about the
**accounting of a system's reliability**, not about what sustains a person's autonomy.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the fallback labour is counted, staffed and costed, or that the
failure rate is low enough that no material absorption occurs.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 6 (care and interdependence); Axis 5. Reaches Axis 9 and Axis 12.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily — the unbooked cost, in reliability engineering's own
vocabulary.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR002-07 asks about time a task's official duration omits. This asks
about **failure absorbed by a person and excluded from a reliability claim** — a different
number, in a different document, usually published.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A payments platform's availability figure against the reconciliation
team's workload. (2) A discharge-to-assess policy whose success measure ends at the hospital
door.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-03 — RECIPROCITY OR DEBT

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: MIRA's own further question — is help offered as ordinary reciprocity or taxed
with visible gratitude — made into a commissioning instrument. Whether an exchange is mutual
or one-directional is observable in how it is recorded and spoken about. Sharpens Axis 6. No
new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** Help moving between people is either ordinary mutual reliance or an obligation
one side carries. The same act can be either, and which one it is shows in the surrounding
apparatus: whether it is recorded, whether thanks are expected, whether it can be declined,
and whether it can be reciprocated.

**QUESTION.** When help moves between people here, is it exchanged as ordinary mutual
reliance, or does it create an obligation only one side carries — and what in the
arrangement makes the difference?

**CARRIERS.** Mutual aid networks against charitable provision. Favour economies in
workplaces. Open source maintenance and its funding relationships. Neighbour and kinship
support. Peer support structures. Time banks and reciprocal care schemes. Volunteering
programmes with beneficiaries and volunteers named as separate classes.

**FALSE MOVE.** The inspiration narrative in both directions — heroic helper, grateful
recipient. The instrument asks about the **structure of the exchange**, and requires the
subject to supply evidence about how it is recorded and whether it runs both ways.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the exchange demonstrably runs in both directions, or that the
apparent asymmetry is a temporary phase of a relationship the participants describe as
mutual.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 6 (care and interdependence) primarily. Reaches Axes 5 and 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Nothing in the library asked about the direction of an exchange.
PR003-03 asks what maintenance an autonomy requires; this asks **who owes whom afterwards**,
which is a social and documentary question rather than an infrastructural one.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) An open source dependency maintained by one unpaid person and the
corporate users' acknowledgement practices. (2) A rural transport scheme run on neighbour
lifts, and what happens when it is formalised.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-04 — IT WORKED BECAUSE OF WHO WAS THERE

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: MIRA's STAYS WITH, made checkable — whether something that works because it is
not designed can be scaled without being killed. Pilot-to-rollout comparisons are documented
in many fields. Sharpens Axes 5 and 14. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** A pilot succeeds. On the strength of its results it is generalised — and the
generalisation reproduces the design while losing the specific people, relationships and
local discretion that were actually doing the work. The evaluation measured the design.

**QUESTION.** Did this succeed because of its design, or because of the specific people in
it — and what happened when it was scaled?

**CARRIERS.** Pilot schemes and their national rollouts. Model wards and exemplar units.
Charter and demonstration schools. Replication failures in behavioural science and medicine.
Community programmes taken over by larger providers. Any intervention with both a pilot
evaluation and a rollout evaluation.

**FALSE MOVE.** Beige universal design offered as a substitute for politics, and its mirror —
concluding that nothing can ever be scaled. The instrument asks **what was actually carrying
the effect** and requires the subject to supply a comparison, not an intuition.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the effect survived scaling, or that the pilot's mechanism was
specified in advance and reproduced deliberately.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5 (independence and assistance); Axis 14 (a process that must keep
being performed). Reaches Axis 6.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily — a state a document can certify against a process
someone must keep performing.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR002-01 asks whether an arrangement expires. This asks whether it was
ever **transferable**, which is a question about replication evidence rather than about
duration.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A hospital improvement programme whose gains disappeared on rollout.
(2) A teaching method that worked in the classrooms of the teachers who invented it.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-05 — THE TECHNIQUE CAME FROM SOMEWHERE ELSE

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: this is the publication's central question in instrument form — what has
disability already taught, invented, noticed or made visible about the ordinary world — and
the library had no instrument that asked it directly. Its subject is an origin story and its
omissions, both of which are documentable. Sharpens Axes 10 and 5. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** A technique, format or tool now treated as ordinary was developed by or with
people the standard origin story omits. The omission is not usually a conspiracy; it is that
the origin was recorded in a different literature, by a different profession, for a different
purpose, and the mainstream account was written later by people who met the technique after
it had generalised.

**QUESTION.** Where did this ordinary technique actually come from, whose practice does the
standard origin story leave out, and where is the earlier practice recorded?

**CARRIERS.** Text-to-speech and screen reading in mainstream voice interfaces. Optical
character recognition. Predictive text and word completion. Subtitling and captioning in
language learning and in noisy public space. Audiobooks. Typewriter and keyboard development.
Vibration and tactile interface design. Plain-language drafting. Any technique with a
disability-adjacent early literature and a mainstream origin story that begins later.

**FALSE MOVE.** **The strongest false move in the library, and the reason this entry is
written carefully.** It is *not* enough that disabled people benefit from a technique, or use
it, or that it was marketed to them. The instrument requires a documented earlier practice
and a documented later account that omits it. Without both, this becomes a curb-cut-effect
listicle, which is a genre and not a finding. Also: NO PROXIES — an origin in another
marginalised community's practice is that community's, not a transferable claim.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the mainstream origin story is accurate and the earlier practice
either did not exist or was independent and unconnected. This closes it cleanly, and it should
be expected to close often.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 10 (participation and authorship); Axis 5 (technology and ability).
Reaches Axis 3.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** Shared, and deliberately so — this is a house question. PINA where the
technique is a channel, SIIRI where it is a form of representation, MIRA where it is
infrastructure, ZENO where its generalisation required reclassification.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR001-05 asks how technology participates in making ability — a question
about the present arrangement. This asks about **the historical record of an invention and
what the standard account omits**, which is archival work with a different burden of proof.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A gesture or interaction convention with an earlier assistive
literature and a later mainstream patent narrative. (2) A document-structuring convention
originating in a non-visual reading practice.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument. The evidentiary bar here is higher than elsewhere in the
library: both the earlier practice and the omitting account must be shown. An entry that shows
only benefit has not used this instrument.

### PR008-06 — THE COST MOVED TO ANOTHER BUDGET

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: efficiency savings frequently displace cost to a party outside the measured
boundary, and both the saving and the displacement are usually documented — in different
accounts, by different bodies. Sharpens Axes 6 and 12. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** A saving is booked against one budget. The work or cost it eliminated does not
disappear; it moves — to another department, another institution, or an unpaid party. The
saving is reported; the displacement is either unreported or reported somewhere that never
meets it.

**QUESTION.** Whose budget does this efficiency saving move the cost to, and are the two
budgets ever compared in the same document?

**CARRIERS.** Hospital discharge policy and subsequent unpaid care. Self-service transitions
in retail, banking and government. Outsourcing and the client-side management cost.
Shift-left in software and the burden on developers. Deinstitutionalisation without
community funding. Any programme with a published saving and an unmeasured recipient.

**FALSE MOVE.** Assuming displacement wherever a saving exists. Some efficiencies are real.
The instrument requires the subject to supply **where the cost landed**, with evidence, and
an entry that cannot name the recipient budget has not shown the mechanism.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show a whole-system evaluation exists and the saving survives it, or
that the eliminated work genuinely stopped being necessary.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 6 (care and interdependence); Axis 12 (what a system registers).
Reaches Axis 9.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily — what things cost, who pays, over what period.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR002-07 asks about time outside a counted interval. This asks about
**money and work crossing an organisational boundary**, which is evidenced in two sets of
published accounts rather than in a duration figure.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A council service reduction and the health service admissions
following it. (2) A build-time optimisation that moved cost into every developer's machine.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-07 — THE HANDOVER IS WHERE IT BREAKS

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: transfers between people or institutions are where information reliably fails
to cross, and what fails to cross is patterned rather than random — it is whatever the
receiving format has no field for. Sharpens Axes 3 and 4. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** At every handover a body of knowledge must be re-expressed in whatever form
the receiving side accepts. What has no slot in that form does not cross. The loss is
systematic — the same kind of information is lost at every handover — and it is invisible
because each side sees only its own record as complete.

**QUESTION.** What has to be transferred between people or institutions at this boundary, and
what reliably fails to make the crossing?

**CARRIERS.** Clinical shift handover and discharge summaries. School-to-school and
school-to-work transitions. Case transfer between agencies. Relay interpreting chains.
Contractor changeover. System migrations and API boundaries. Prisoner and patient transfers.
Any boundary with a standard handover form and a known category of complaint about it.

**FALSE MOVE.** Treating handover loss as carelessness. The instrument's claim is that the
loss is **structural, patterned and predictable from the receiving format** — which is a
stronger and more falsifiable claim than that people forget things.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show that what is lost varies randomly rather than by category, or
that the receiving format accommodates it and the loss is a compliance problem instead.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 3 (what arrives); Axis 4 (sequence). Reaches Axis 12.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** PINA primarily — what happened between the thing and the version that
arrived.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR005-01 asks what a notation cannot write down, within one field.
This asks what is lost **at a boundary between two parties**, where the evidence is the
handover artefact itself and the complaints about it.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A discharge summary template with no field for what the patient
actually manages at home. (2) A migration where audit history had no destination column.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.

### PR008-08 — THE INSTITUTION LEARNED AND SOMEONE ELSE PAID THE TUITION

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner authorisation 2026-09-13.
*Owner reason: institutional learning is real and is frequently purchased with a specific
person's harm, and inquiries document both the lesson and the cost while rarely relating
them. Sharpens Axes 9 and 10. No new axis.*

**MECHANISM.** Institutions learn from failures. The learning is recorded as a lesson,
generalised into procedure, and credited to the institution. The person whose harm produced
it is recorded separately, in a different document, usually as a case rather than as an
author of the knowledge.

**QUESTION.** What did this institution learn from a failure, who bore the failure while it
was learning, and are the two recorded in the same place?

**CARRIERS.** Public inquiries and their resulting guidance. Aviation and rail safety
recommendations. Clinical incident reviews and never-event policy. Product recalls and
subsequent standards. Regulatory change following a scandal. Safety cases citing historical
accidents. Any procedure whose origin is a named incident.

**FALSE MOVE.** Treating institutional learning as inherently cynical. Much of it is genuine
and has saved lives — the aviation record is the obvious case. The instrument asks about the
**relationship between the lesson and its cost in the record**, not about whether learning
occurred.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the institution records the two together, credits the affected
party, and that the learning was not conditional on the harm — for example a near-miss
system that produced the same lesson without it.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 9 (accumulated cost); Axis 10 (authorship). Reaches Axis 6.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MIRA primarily — who paid, and over what period.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR006-06 asks what a field generalised from a single case. This asks about
**a lesson purchased with harm and the separation of the two records**, which is an inquiry
and procedure question rather than a citation one.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A named-incident checklist item and whether the incident's subject is
named in the standard. (2) A near-miss reporting system that produced a change without a
casualty, as the disconfirming case.

**BOUNDARY.** Derived instrument — see PROVENANCE above.
