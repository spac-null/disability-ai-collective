## ENTRIES

### PR002-01 — ACCESS CAN EXPIRE

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner review 2026-09-08.
*Owner reason: the mechanism is precise and falsifiable — eventual availability is not
equivalent to availability while an action can still be changed. It sharpens owner M3 without
reducing temporal access to "lag". Preserved on approval: the consequence of the delay must be
demonstrated, not every delay is exclusion, S6 remains abstract-level only, and **this
approval does not adopt S6's broader theory wholesale**.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S6 (abstract-level): the paper shifts attention "from waiting as
experience to waiting as distribution", and states that **access received too late is not
equivalent to access received in time** — a general-theory formulation of exactly this
mechanism, arrived at independently of disability studies. S3 (read) supplies the
interactional version: a student watching the interpreter loses the teacher's gestures on
the model, and watching the model loses the ongoing talk, so information arrives after the
moment it referred to. Owner canon M3 (the three-second lag) already carries the shape.

**MECHANISM.** Information can be fully available eventually and still be inaccessible at
the moment when it could have changed a decision or an interaction. Availability and
timeliness are separate properties, and a system that guarantees the first often reports
itself as having provided the second.

**QUESTION.** Was the information available *before* the moment at which it lost its power
to change what happened?

**CARRIERS.** Interpreting delay. Caption latency. Meeting turn-taking and the point at
which an objection is still admissible. A live vote. A trading or auction window. A court
listing. An emergency announcement. A clinical handover. A real-time interface with a
refresh interval.

**FALSE MOVE.** *"Any delay equals exclusion."* The consequence of **this** delay has to be
demonstrated — the specific thing that could no longer be changed once the information
arrived. A delay with no forfeited action is a delay, not a mechanism.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 4 (timing & simultaneity); reaches Axis 3 (mediation).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** PINA primarily; MAYA where the expiry is institutional.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 4 asks whose pace a process assumes. This asks a narrower and more
checkable thing: whether there was a **deadline inside the information itself**, after which
having it changed nothing.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A security advisory published after the exploit window closed —
fully public, entirely too late. (2) A regulator's finding issued after the merger
completed.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show that the late-arriving information was still actionable when
it arrived, or that the decision it was supposed to inform remained open. Either kills it.

**BOUNDARY.** S6 is read at abstract level only and its authors are unknown; the claim is
recorded as the paper's, not as established. S3's version is one Norwegian classroom study
(14 situations, ~55 hours).

### PR002-02 — PACE IS PART OF THE INTERFACE

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner review 2026-09-08. **The strongest empirical
instrument in PR002.**
*Owner reason: the source rate itself materially changes what survives mediation, and the
evidence corrected the intuitive assumption that lag was necessarily the main variable.
Preserved on approval, all four: the CASE/SEE seam, the N=12 limitation, no Deaf
generalisation, and the refusal of "slower is always better". Sharpens Axis 4 and PINA. No new
axis.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S1 (read), an experiment with 12 educational CASE transliterators at three
speaking rates — **88, 109 and 137 words per minute**, all ordinary conversational rates.
Accuracy fell **12 percentage points** from the slow to the fast rate
(F(2,22)=14.3, p<.001, ηp²=.565), with **omissions** the primary driver, rising 10 points.
Mean accuracy across all rates and transliterators was **61%**.

Lag time, by contrast, had **a very weak negative relationship** at phrase level (r=−.178),
**accounting for just 3% of the variance**, and no relationship at all between a
transliterator's average lag and their average accuracy.

**The seam, marked:** S2 (abstract-level, and a *different* system — SEE, not CASE) reports
that intelligibility **decreases sharply below 65% accuracy**. Read together the two
suggest a rate→accuracy→intelligibility chain crossing a threshold; read strictly they are
two studies of two systems and the chain is **an inference, not a finding.**

**MECHANISM.** The rate at which a system emits information is part of its access model, not
a neutral property of delivery. A source moving at its own ordinary pace can degrade what
arrives through a mediating channel — and can do so more than the channel's own delay does.

**QUESTION.** What capacity does this process assume simply by moving at this speed?

**CARRIERS.** A speaking or presentation rate. A timeout. An assumed reading speed. An input
window. A response deadline. A scroll, refresh or sampling rate. A production quota. A
service-level target expressed as a duration.

**FALSE MOVE.** Assuming **slower is always better**. S1 is direct evidence against the
reflex: for these transliterators longer lag was associated with *slightly worse* accuracy,
the opposite of the ASL-interpreter pattern, which the authors attribute to memory cost. The
question is what a given rate assumes, not whether speed is bad.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 4; reaches Axis 3 (what mediation changes) and Axis 2 (perception).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** PINA primarily.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** This is the cluster's real correction to a naive reading of owner canon
**M3**. The intuitive account of mediated access puts the loss in the *lag*. In the one place
it has been measured here, the lag explains 3% of the variance and the **speaker's ordinary
rate** explains a great deal more. The instrument to reach for is the rate, not the delay.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A trading or telemetry feed whose sampling rate silently determines
what a downstream model can ever detect. (2) A courtroom or committee whose speaking pace
determines what a stenographic or translated record actually preserves.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show that varying the rate across its ordinary operating range
leaves fidelity, comprehension or completion unchanged. If pace does not move the outcome,
pace is not part of the interface here.

**BOUNDARY.** N=12, one transliteration system, an educational setting, and the authors state
plainly that this "cannot be considered a large or nationally comprehensive sample". The 65%
threshold comes from a **different** system and must not be quoted against CASE numbers. And
nothing here licenses a claim about deaf people in general: it is a finding about a service.

### PR002-03 — SYNCHRONY IS A RESOURCE

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner review 2026-09-08.
*Owner reason: it identifies a distinct mechanism invisible to per-channel accessibility — two
individually available channels colliding because both demand the same attention at the same
moment. Preserved on approval: this is about an **arrangement**, not about Deaf experience in
general, and sequential, replayable or redundant channels can disconfirm it. Sharpens Axes 2
and 4, and SIIRI/PINA. No new axis.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S3 (read) — Berge & Thomassen, 14 interpreter-mediated lessons, ~55 hours
of three-camera video, Norwegian high school. A deaf student "can only have his or her visual
attention focused on one source at a time" while hearing peers listen and look at once. With
an artifact in use — a model, a machine, a screen — the student must choose sequentially
between the interpreter's signs, the teacher's situated gestures on the artifact, and the
artifact itself: "when she looks at the interpreter's signs she will lose sight of the
teacher's gestures on the model, and when Torill looks at the model, she will lose the
ongoing talk." Deictic reference ("that", "there") fails across the gap. The authors'
conclusion is the entry in one line: **"the interpreter's presence is not enough to ensure
visual access."**

One carrier worth keeping for its concreteness: an interpreter stepped closer to the teacher
to improve the student's sightline, and the teacher, unaware, stepped forward and blocked it.

**MECHANISM.** Two channels can each be accessible separately and become inaccessible
together, when they demand the same attention at the same moment. Provision is counted
channel by channel; the collision is between them, so nobody's accessibility audit sees it.

**QUESTION.** What is this person being asked to perceive simultaneously?

**CARRIERS.** An interpreter beside a presentation. Captions beside on-screen action. A
dashboard beside an alarm. Multiple monitors. A spoken instruction during a manual task. A
multimodal interface. Any deictic reference — "this one", "there" — that assumes the speaker
and the referent are in one field of view.

**FALSE MOVE.** Reducing every multimodal problem to sensory disability. Divided attention is
a general constraint; what this instrument asks about is a system that **provisioned two
channels and counted the job done**.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 4 (simultaneity) and Axis 2 (perception); reaches Axis 3.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** SIIRI on attention allocation and sequential-versus-simultaneous
perception; PINA on turn-taking and temporal equivalence.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Neither Axis 2 nor Axis 4 currently asks about **collision between two
provided channels**. It is a distinct failure from either channel being missing, and it is
invisible to per-channel compliance.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) An aircraft or plant operator given a visual alarm during a
head-down procedure. (2) A live-translated conference where slides and interpretation are on
opposite sides of the room.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the two channels can be attended sequentially with no loss —
because the material is redundant across them, or self-paced, or replayable. Then there is no
collision, only two sources.

**BOUNDARY.** One school, one country, 2016, and a specific classroom genre. Do not
generalise a particular interface result to deaf experience as a whole; S3 is a study of
interpreted lessons with artifacts, not of deafness.

### PR002-04 — WAITING IS AN ALLOCATION

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **held at candidate, owner review 2026-09-08.**
*Owner reason: a strong mechanism, but the primary paper is **ABSTRACT-LEVEL ONLY and its
authors remain UNKNOWN**. Do not promote an elegant theory faster than source access permits.
The disconfirming requirement is kept as the gate: show actual temporal asymmetry or
reordering, not merely the existence of waiting.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S6 (abstract-level). The paper theorises **institutional time inequality**
— disparities in the duration between claim and resolution as a mechanism of stratification —
and **speed capital**, the capacity to convert economic, social and cultural capital into
accelerated institutional outcomes under differentiated temporal governance. Its stated move
is from **waiting as experience to waiting as distribution**, and its empirical contexts are
reported to include immigration adjudication, pretrial detention, **disability benefits
administration**, housing waitlists, eviction proceedings and citizenship by investment.

**MECHANISM.** Institutional waiting distributes time unequally. One actor's speed can be
purchased with another's delay, and the purchase is usually invisible because the two parties
never appear in the same document.

**QUESTION.** Who gets to move immediately here, and whose time absorbs the uncertainty?

**CARRIERS.** A queue policy and its priority rules. An expedited or premium track. An
approval process with different lanes. Triage criteria. An appeal timetable. A procurement
schedule. A support-package assessment. An administrative handoff where the clock stops for
one party and not the other.

**FALSE MOVE.** Assuming **every wait expresses domination**. Queues exist for many reasons,
including capacity and fairness. The reading needs the *asymmetry* shown — a party whose
speed is produced by another party's delay — not the existence of a wait.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 9 (endurance, pace, accumulated cost); reaches Axis 5 (assistance)
and Axis 10 (participation).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA primarily — this is infrastructure and its economics in temporal
form.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 9 asks what a thing costs in duration. This asks **who pays that
duration on whose behalf**, which is a different and more locatable question.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A cloud provider's paid support tier, where one customer's response
time is produced by the queue position of everybody else. (2) A planning authority where a
developer's pre-application service shortens their wait and lengthens the ordinary one.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the fast party's speed is produced by added capacity rather
than by reordering — that nobody's wait grew. Then it is provision, not allocation.

**BOUNDARY.** **Abstract-level only, authors unknown, and not disability scholarship.** It is
used here because it supplies a mechanism disability knowledge can interrogate, per the
instruction — not adopted because it is elegant. Its empirical contexts are as reported in
the abstract and have not been checked in the text.

### PR002-05 — DELAY CHANGES THE STATE IT IS WAITING ON

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner review 2026-09-08.
*Owner reason: it produces a genuinely useful house question — the subject may change while an
institution continues to decide against a frozen earlier representation. Preserved on
approval: the mechanism is the mismatch between a moving subject and a static record;
mortality and deterioration statistics are **not themselves the insight**; and **no claim of
institutional intent is licensed**. Sharpens Axis 9 and connects to Axis 12 / PR001-07. No new
axis.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S9 (abstract-level, government audit): of roughly 9 million disability
applicants who appealed between FY2008 and FY2019, **109,725 died before receiving a final
decision** — about 1.2% — and the annual death rate among applicants awaiting a decision rose
from **0.52% to 0.72%** between FY2011 and FY2018. GAO states plainly that during the wait an
applicant's health or financial situation could deteriorate. S4 (read) supplies the workplace
version: planning horizons that assume a stable body, and a participant's response to
planning for a year ahead — "I might not even be here."

**MECHANISM.** Waiting is not always neutral time. The person, body, resource or situation
may change while the institution waits to decide — and the decision, when it comes, is often
written as though the subject had stayed still.

**QUESTION.** What changes during the waiting period that the eventual decision treats as
though it had remained still?

**CARRIERS.** A clinical trajectory recorded across an assessment interval. Lost income or
accrued debt. An opportunity with its own expiry. A housing or employment position. A device
that fails mid-wait. A decaying sample. A changing environment. A decision document dated
long after the evidence it rests on.

**FALSE MOVE.** Treating mortality or deterioration statistics as the mechanism. The
mechanism is the **mismatch between a moving subject and a static record** — the decision
answering a question about a person who no longer exists in that form. Without that mismatch
shown, it is a sad number.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 9; reaches Axis 12 (what a record can hold — here, a record that
cannot hold change).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA primarily; SIIRI where the frozen record is the point.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** A genuinely new question for the house. Neither Axis 9 nor Axis 12 asks
what the *waiting itself* did to the thing being waited on. It also pairs with PR001-07: the
record fixes a state, and the delay guarantees the fixed state is out of date.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A grant or permit decided against a budget that has since changed.
(2) A safety recall issued against a fleet whose fault has already progressed.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the decision process re-reads current state at the point of
decision rather than the state at claim — or that the subject is genuinely static over the
interval. Either removes the mechanism.

**BOUNDARY.** S9 is a US administrative audit, not theory and not peer-reviewed; the figures
are as reported in its published summary. It evidences that states change during
institutional waits. It does not establish intent, and this instrument must not be used to
assert one.

### PR002-06 — MORE TIME IS NOT ALWAYS DIFFERENT TIME

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **held at candidate, owner review 2026-09-08.**
*Owner reason: the mechanism is promising and S4 supports an episodic, non-smooth temporal
pattern — but the two central crip-time texts, Samuels and Kafer, were **not accessed in
full**, and this entry carries a **high essentialism risk** if stated too broadly. The
constraint stands: disabled time is NOT inherently nonlinear. Research action: obtain and read
Samuels and Kafer before promotion.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S4 (read): crip time is "marked by disruptions and irregularities", against
chrononormativity's expectation of "smooth and linear progress"; a participant describes
cancelling a major event because "sometimes, things just get cancelled because we can't
predict how our bodies will feel". S7 (**not accessed**; fragment via search): "Crip time is
time travel. Disability and illness have the power to extract us from linear, progressive
time with its normative life stages and cast us into a wormhole of backward and forward
acceleration, jerky stops and starts, tedious intervals and abrupt endings." S8 (**not
accessed**; via secondary S10): "rather than bend disabled bodies and minds to meet the
clock, crip time bends the clock to meet disabled bodies and minds."

**MECHANISM.** "Extra time" assumes duration is smoothly exchangeable — that twice the
duration yields twice the usable capacity. Where capacity is episodic, unpredictable,
fatigue-limited or recovery-dependent, duration and usable capacity come apart, and an
accommodation denominated in duration can miss entirely.

**QUESTION.** Is duration actually the scarce resource here, or is usable capacity
distributed unevenly through that duration?

**CARRIERS.** An exam accommodation expressed as a percentage of extra time. A shift pattern.
A deadline extension. A hearing schedule. A treatment interval. A travel itinerary. Any
provision whose only variable is *how long*.

**FALSE MOVE.** **Claiming disabled time is inherently nonlinear.** The subject must show the
temporal pattern — the unpredictability, the episode, the fatigue curve — in its own evidence.
Asserting nonlinearity as a property of a population is the analogy failure in temporal
clothing, and the doctrine already refuses it.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 4 and Axis 9; reaches Axis 8 (capacity).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** ZENO on episodic patterns and what a measure assumes about their shape;
MAYA on provision.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Distinguishes *quantity* of time from *shape* of time. The doctrine
currently has no instrument for a resource that is unevenly distributed **inside** an
interval rather than scarce across it.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) Compute capacity sold as monthly hours where the workload is bursty
and the bottleneck is concurrency, not total. (2) Grid supply where the binding constraint is
peak availability, not annual generation.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show that adding duration does in fact add proportional usable
capacity in this subject. If more time simply works, the instrument does not apply.

**BOUNDARY.** The two most-cited framings here (S7, S8) were **not read in the original**.
S4 is read and carries the mechanism on its own; the crip-time quotations are decoration
until the primaries are obtained. Research action: get Samuels and Kafer properly.

### PR002-07 — RECOVERY IS PART OF THE TASK

**STATUS:** **APPROVED_DURABLE** — owner review 2026-09-08.
*Owner reason: a task's official duration can omit materially necessary preparation and
recovery time — a concrete temporal form of the existing unbooked-cost / affective-ledger
logic. Preserved on approval: not all off-clock preparation is exploitation; the actual
accounting boundary and the material recovery cost must both be shown; and the source is
qualitative and establishes no magnitude. Sharpens Axis 9 and MAYA. No new axis.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S4 (read): disabled workers "must dedicate a significant portion of their
free time to self-care" in order to remain functional; a social-worker participant, knowing
this from her own body, "strictly forbid[s] my staff to do any extra work". The paper frames
productivism as "a mechanism that reduces people to mere resources for productive output",
organising paid work around "self-discipline, efficiency and individual responsibility".

**MECHANISM.** An institution may count only the visible performance interval, while the body
also pays preparation and recovery time outside it. The uncounted time is real, it is
unequally distributed, and it does not appear in any measure of the task's cost.

**QUESTION.** What time does this task consume that its official duration does not count?

**CARRIERS.** A shift pattern beside its recovery requirement. A commute. A medical procedure
with a preparation and recovery protocol. A performance and its preparation. Travel. Care
handover. Sensory recovery after an environment. An on-call arrangement. Any duration figure
whose boundaries were drawn by whoever benefits from them.

**FALSE MOVE.** Treating all uncounted time as exploitation. Some preparation is ordinary and
freely given. The instrument asks where the **accounting boundary** was drawn and who set it,
not whether effort exists outside it.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 9 (endurance and accumulated cost); reaches Axis 6 (care) and Axis 5.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA primarily — the unbooked-cost instrument in temporal form, and a
direct sharpening of PR001's affective-ledger material.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 6 asks who performs unbooked labour. This asks specifically about
**time outside the counted interval**, which is a measurable and documentable version of the
same question.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) An airline duty-time rule that counts flight hours and not the
displacement either side. (2) An incident response measured from page to resolution, ignoring
the days of degraded capacity afterward.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the official duration already includes preparation and recovery
— or that no material recovery cost exists for this task. Either closes it.

**BOUNDARY.** S4 is qualitative, interview-based, in a post-socialist Czech context, and its
participants are self-selected. It evidences the mechanism; it establishes no magnitude.

### PR002-08 — A DEADLINE CAN DEFINE THE PERSON

**STATUS:** **NEEDS_RESEARCH** — **held, owner review 2026-09-08.**
*Owner note: the research protocol worked correctly here, and this entry is **not** to be
upgraded because the hypothesis is attractive. What is needed is actual literature showing a
capacity or classification changing materially as a function of the measurement window.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** Owner discussion, plus indirect support only. S1 shows accuracy is a
function of the rate at which the task is presented — a competence measured inside a chosen
window — but it measures a *service*, not a person, and the authors make no claim about
capacity attribution. S4 documents workplace temporal norms without making the classification
claim. **No source in this set establishes that a capacity appears absent because it was
measured inside a particular time window.**

**MECHANISM (hypothesis).** A capacity may appear absent only because it is measured inside a
particular time window; change the clock and the incapacity changes with it. The window then
gets recorded as a property of the person.

**QUESTION.** Would this still count as inability if the clock were different?

**CARRIERS.** A response-time threshold. A standardised test with a time limit. A
service-level objective. A reaction-time assessment. An employment performance metric. A
clinical assessment with a timed component.

**FALSE MOVE.** Assuming **all capacity differences disappear with more time**. Some do not,
and PR002-06 is the reason: where capacity is episodic, a longer window does not recover it
either. The two entries constrain each other and should be read together.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 8 (cognition & capacity), Axis 11 (classification), Axis 4.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** ZENO — this is the temporal form of PR001-09 and of the classification
axis generally.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** If it survives: the clock as an instrument of classification rather than
of scheduling. That would be a real addition. It has not survived yet.

**TRANSFER TEST.** *(Held until there is a source. Writing transfer tests for an unsupported
mechanism is how a hypothesis starts looking established.)*

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show the capacity is stable across window lengths — the same
assessment at twice the duration classifies the same people the same way.

**BOUNDARY.** Kept as NEEDS_RESEARCH deliberately. It is the most attractive hypothesis in
the starting set and the least supported, which is exactly the combination the protocol
exists to slow down. Research action: timed-assessment and reaction-threshold literature,
and Samuels/Kafer once obtained.

### PR002-09 — THE ACCOMMODATION PRESERVES THE SCHEDULE

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **held at candidate, owner review 2026-09-08. Explicitly NOT
rejected.**
*Owner reason: one of the most interesting hypotheses PR002 **produced** rather than was fed.
But it rests heavily on one workplace/time context and could too easily harden into a
pre-decided critique — "accommodations preserve bad systems". Before promotion, find another
independent domain or source where individual flexibility demonstrably absorbs deviation AND
the default schedule or rule thereby remains structurally unchanged. Its false move is
preserved with emphasis: **this is never an argument to withhold accommodation.***
*Not in the starting hypothesis set. It emerged from S4 and is recorded because the research
produced it.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** S4 (read). The authors caution that positive manifestations of crip time
"were relatively rare in contrast to the pervasive pressures of normative temporalities", and
— the load-bearing observation — that flexibility enabling temporary accommodation **"actually
enables the system to keep running"** by absorbing individual deviations without structural
change.

**MECHANISM.** An accommodation can be the thing that protects the timetable it appears to
bend. By absorbing deviation case by case, it removes exactly the pressure that would
otherwise force the schedule itself to change — so the more effective the accommodation, the
more durable the norm.

**QUESTION.** Does this flexibility change the schedule, or is it what allows the schedule to
stay as it is?

**CARRIERS.** An exceptions process alongside an unchanged default. A case-by-case waiver. A
discretionary extension with no policy revision. An accommodation register. A hardship fund.
Any provision granted individually where the underlying rule is never revisited.

**FALSE MOVE.** Concluding that accommodations are therefore bad or should be withheld. The
source says nothing of the kind, and the inference would be monstrous. The instrument asks
what the flexibility **does to the norm**, not whether the person should have received it.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 14 (access as a process, not a state) and Axis 9; reaches Axis 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA primarily; ZENO on the exception that defines the rule.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 14 asks whether access is a certified state or a performed process.
This adds the reflexive case: a process **whose success is what keeps the state unexamined.**
It is also a genuine sharpener for PR001-03 — the exception route is an institutional
consequence of classification that leaves the classification intact.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A manual workaround that keeps a broken system passing its SLA, and so
prevents it being fixed. (2) An opt-out that absorbs the objectors and preserves the default
for everyone else.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Show that accumulated accommodations did in fact change the rule —
that the exception became the default, or the schedule moved. Then the flexibility was a
route to structural change, not a substitute for it.

**BOUNDARY.** One qualitative paper, one national context. The mechanism is stated by the
authors about workplace temporal norms; its transfer beyond that is untested.

## CRIP TIME IS NOT A PHILOSOPHY OF SLOWNESS

Recorded as a standing constraint on this cluster, because the failure is specific and easy.

The celebratory reading — crip time as freedom from normative scheduling, a wiser relation to
duration — is available in the literature and is **not** what this cluster takes from it. The
sources that were actually read complicate it:

- **S4 (read):** positive manifestations were "relatively rare in contrast to the pervasive
  pressures of normative temporalities"; crip time emerges **"from necessity rather than
  choice"** and remains "closely tied to socio-material conditions". A participant cancels an
  event because bodies cannot be predicted. This is not liberation from the clock; it is a
  cost.
- **S7 (fragment, not accessed):** Samuels' essay is described in its own abstract as
  reflecting on crip time "not only as a form of liberation, but also as a site of loss and
  alienation", and names crip time as **grief time** and **vampire time** — grieving "who we
  were, who we thought we would become". The framing that most resists romanticisation is the
  one this session could not read in full.
- **S10 (secondary):** crip time "doesn't insulate people from material realities".

**The constraint.** Crip time may involve flexibility and freedom from normative scheduling
*and* loss, unpredictability, waiting, exhaustion, exclusion and grief. An instrument here
that only carries the first half has been written wrongly. **Disability temporality is not a
superior philosophy of slowness**, and treating it as one would violate the
contribution-must-be-earned principle in `crip-minds-project-genesis.md` — it would decide in
advance that the experience contains an insight, which is precisely what the project refuses.

## WHAT THIS CLUSTER DOES TO THE OWNER MECHANISMS

**M3 — THE THREE-SECOND LAG. SHARPENED, NOT INVALIDATED** *(owner decision, 2026-09-08)*.
The operative instruction the cluster produces: **do not automatically locate temporal access
loss in lag itself.** Check the **source rate**, the **simultaneity / attention collision**,
and the **action expiry** before deciding what the mechanism is. M3 is not rewritten in
doctrine by this cluster. The intuitive account puts
the loss in the delay. In the one place it has been measured (S1), **lag explains ~3% of the
variance in accuracy while the speaker's ordinary rate moves it 12 points** — and S3
concludes that "the interpreter's presence is not enough to ensure visual access". So M3's
question survives intact, but the instruments beneath it should reach for **rate** (PR002-02)
and **simultaneity** (PR002-03) before reaching for delay. M3 is not weakened; the naive
reading of it is.

**M5 — PERMISSION ARRIVES LAST. SHARPENED** *(owner decision, 2026-09-08)* by waiting as a
possible **allocation** and by delay **changing the subject or state being decided about**.
Not rewritten in doctrine by this cluster. PR002-04 says the lateness may
be an **allocation** — someone else's speed produced by this delay. PR002-05 says the delay
**changes the thing being decided about**, so the permission, when it lands, answers a
question about a person or situation that no longer exists in that form. M5 recorded that
permission came last; these say what lateness *is* and what it *does*.

Neither owner mechanism needs proving and neither has been treated as article evidence.

## META-EDITORIAL

### ME003 — DISCONFIRMING SHAPE

**STATUS:** **OWNER-APPROVED META-EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE** — owner review 2026-09-08.
**Adopted into the perspective-research protocol**, effective for new entries from PR003
onward. See `README.md`.

*Note on vocabulary: this is not a sixth research-entry status. The five statuses are
unchanged and apply to research entries only.*

**DEFINITION, as adopted.** A DISCONFIRMING SHAPE is *a plausible observation or evidence
pattern that would make the proposed mechanism NOT apply to the subject.*

**THE RULE.** If no plausible disconfirming shape can be stated, the hypothesis is probably
too ideological, too vague, or too self-confirming to be a Perspective Library instrument.

**THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS.** FALSE MOVE = how the *editor* may misuse the idea.
DISCONFIRMING SHAPE = what *subject evidence* would show the idea is wrong here. They answer
different questions and neither substitutes for the other.

Every entry in this cluster carries one. It was added as an experiment and earned its place —
it is what moved PR002-08 to NEEDS_RESEARCH and what exposed PR002-04's dependence on showing
an asymmetry rather than a wait.

It is a direct operationalisation of **ME002** (perspective as hypothesis generation): a good
hypothesis *can fail*, and a field that names the failure makes that checkable rather than
aspirational. The test in practice: **if no plausible disconfirming observation can be
written, the question is too ideological or too vague to be an instrument.**

**Adopted into the global README protocol, 2026-09-08**, for new entries from PR003 onward.
**PR001 is not retroactively rewritten**; a PR001 entry gains a disconfirming shape only when
it is substantively revisited on other grounds.
