# Perspective Research 004
## Art, Disability, Aesthetic Knowledge & Institutions

**STATUS: WORKING RESEARCH — NOT PRODUCTION DOCTRINE.**
Nothing here is in force. No entry may be quoted, cited, or used to establish anything about
any subject. **Nothing is promoted; owner review comes afterward.**

**CENTRAL QUESTION.** Not which artists are disabled, not which museums have access
programmes. Rather: **what forms of question-asking intelligence have disability-rooted
artistic, curatorial and institutional practices produced** — about perception, spectatorship,
space, mediation, timing, authorship and institutional behaviour — and **can that knowledge
generate questions about the ordinary world outside art?**

**THE DISTINCTION THIS CLUSTER RUNS ON.** Disability as *subject matter* is not disability as
*method*. An exhibition containing disabled artists does not qualify. An institution providing
captions or ramps does not qualify. A work about disability does not qualify. The mechanism
must change how something is **made, perceived, organised, mediated, explained or operated.**

**ART DOES NOT GRANT NOVELTY.** Where an art concept turns out to be an existing instrument in
gallery language, it is marked DUPLICATE. One entry below is rejected on exactly that ground.

**NO DEVICE, ARTIST OR INSTITUTION BELONGS TO A MIND.** MINDS SHARPENED records mechanism, not
subject.

**FIELD SCHEMA.** Canonical README ten, under the administrative STATUS and SOURCE BASIS
headers. README unmodified.

## ENTRIES

### PR004-01 — WHICH CHANNEL IS TREATED AS THE ORIGINAL

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S2 (read in full).** The Whitney's own account of Kim's practice: she
uses "musical notation, infographics, and language — both in her native American Sign Language
(ASL) and written English"; the work "confronts the systemic marginalization of the Deaf
community and **subordination of access**"; and it is directed at "the politics of sound" and
"the social currency of oral languages". Specific works are described as comparing English
words and phrases "with their **Deaf English** (shorthand translations of ASL) counterparts",
and she repurposes classical dynamics notation (p, pp, ppp) — a hearing discipline's own
apparatus — to make quietness graphic.

**MECHANISM.** A work, institution or system can treat **one channel as the original and the
others as translations of it**, without ever saying so. The hierarchy is not usually stated;
it shows up in which version is authored first, which is corrected against which, and which
one is allowed to be incomplete.

**QUESTION.** Which channel here is being treated as the authoritative original, and what is
positioned as a translation of it?

**CARRIERS.** Which version is produced first and which is derived. Which is corrected against
which. Which is permitted to lag or abbreviate. Which one carries the byline. A caption
generated from audio versus audio described from a caption. A label written to an object
versus an object hung to a label. A screen-reader path derived from a visual layout. Notation
that presumes a performance, or a performance that presumes notation.

**FALSE MOVE.** "Vision or hearing is oppressive." The mechanism needs a **concrete hierarchy
in the actual arrangement** — an order of production or authority — not a stance about senses.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Multiple channels have equal authorial status, or no single channel
determines what counts as the original — for instance both are authored in parallel, and
either can be revised against the other.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 2 (perception) and Axis 3 (mediation); reaches Axis 12.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** SIIRI on what description is derived from; PINA on the order of
production. **Not assigned by the artist's deafness.**

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 2 asks what perceptual **range** a system is defined over. This asks a
different question — of two or more channels a system does provide, **which one is the
source** — and it is answerable from a production workflow rather than from a specification.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) **Software localisation:** an English string file that all other
languages are diffed against, so every other language is structurally a lagging translation.
(2) **Broadcast:** whether subtitles are authored from the script or transcribed from the
finished audio, which decides what happens to anything unscripted.

**BOUNDARY.** S2 is a **museum press release** — an institution's account of an artist, not the
artist's own writing. Kim's own statements were not obtained. Do not attribute the mechanism
to her as a claim she makes; it is our reading of what the described works do.

### PR004-02 — THE MEDIATOR ASSIGNS A REGISTER TO THE MEDIATED

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **strongest new mechanism in the cluster.**
*Reformulated from starting hypothesis F, which was "translation can produce meaning".*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S2 (read in full).** Two passages carry it. Drawings in the Lobby Gallery
"consider the role that sign language interpreters play in **making Kim 'sound' different**".
And *Prolonged Echo* (2023) derives its imagery from the ASL sign for "echo", because "there is
a delay in relaying information as ASL interpreters '**echo**' what she signs."

**MECHANISM.** A mediating layer does not only delay, degrade or reframe content — it can
**assign a voice, register or persona to the person it mediates**. The mediated party is heard
in someone else's diction, at someone else's pace, with someone else's habits of emphasis, and
is then judged on the result as though it were their own.

**QUESTION.** Whose register is the mediated person being heard in, and who is held responsible
for it?

**CARRIERS.** An interpreter or translator's own idiom, and whether the audience can tell.
Automated transcription that normalises dialect. A summarising layer between a person and a
decision-maker — case notes, a recruiter's write-up, a triage note. A press office speaking
for someone. A model paraphrasing a user's request before it is acted on. Any record where the
speaker's own words are not what the reader receives, and the reader does not know it.

**FALSE MOVE.** "All translation is authorship." Too broad, and it makes the instrument fire
everywhere. This requires a **register or persona** attributable to the mediator, and someone
downstream forming a judgement of the *mediated person* on that basis.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The mediating layer is transparent to the receiver — its authorship is
visible and separable, or the mediated person controls the register and can correct it. Then
mediation is happening but no register is being assigned.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 3 (communication & mediation); reaches Axis 13 (gaze — being read).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** PINA primarily.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** This is the increment over Axis 3 and over PR002. Axis 3 asks what mediation
**changes**. PR002-01 asks whether information arrived **in time**; PR002-02 asks what the
**rate** does to what survives. **None of them asks whose voice the mediated person arrives
in** — which is a question about attribution, not about fidelity or timing.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) **Recruitment:** a candidate summarised by an agency in the agency's
house style, then assessed on "communication skills". (2) **Clinical or legal records:** a
patient's or claimant's account entered in a professional's summarising idiom and later read as
that person's own account.

**BOUNDARY.** Rests on a press release's characterisation of two works. Kim's own writing and
the works themselves were not obtained. It is a strong reading of a thin evidence base and
should be tested against the artist's primary material before promotion.

### PR004-03 — DOES THE EXPLANATION BREAK WITHOUT IT?

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE
*From starting hypothesis G.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S4 (secondary, read)** on **S5**. Ludwig's assessment is that the show
recasts art history "as **not an account of individual geniuses or aesthetic teleology, but
rather a series of dialectical relations between moldable materials and fallible bodies**" —
and he supplies the worked instance: Hannah Wilke, "most known for her work on sexuality, is
historiographically reconfigured by *For Dear Life* as a woman wrestling with her illness",
while Felix Gonzalez-Torres, "more commonly historicized through their medical conditions", has
his interpretive scope "expanded dramatically". So the test cuts **both ways** — a factor can
be added to one artist's account and *removed as sufficient* from another's.

**MECHANISM.** A factor can either be **added to a history** — more names, more examples, same
causal story — or it can **change the explanation**, so that removing it leaves the history
unable to account for what happened. The two are routinely confused, and the second is rare.

**QUESTION.** If this factor disappeared from the account, would the explanation still work —
or only the list of names get shorter?

**CARRIERS.** A survey's stated organising principle, before and after. An individual figure's
interpretation shifting rather than their inclusion. A causal claim about why a form or method
emerged. Acquisition and commissioning records. A canon's own periodisation. A revised edition
of a standard history, compared with its predecessor.

**FALSE MOVE.** "Disabled artists were secretly responsible for everything." Also its quieter
form — treating the *presence* of a revisionist claim as evidence that the revision succeeded.
S4 is one reviewer's judgement, not a settled historiographic result.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The standard causal history survives intact and the factor contributes
only additional makers and examples — the same story, longer.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 10 (participation & authorship); reaches Axis 11.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** SHARED. No mind gains distinctively.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** A **counterfactual test for explanations**, which the library does not have.
PR001-09 asks whether a category has become an explanation of its own data. This asks whether an
explanation depends on a factor at all — a different and cleanly checkable operation.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) **Technology history:** whether a standard's adoption story survives
removing a single firm, or merely loses an anecdote. (2) **Epidemiology or economics:** whether
a published account of a trend still holds when one contributing variable is removed, or whether
it was carrying the explanation.

**BOUNDARY.** The evidence is **one review of one exhibition**, and the exhibition's own
materials were read only partially. The mechanism is general; the support for it here is not.

### PR004-04 — DID THE PRACTICE MIGRATE, OR STAY WITH THE PROJECT?

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE
*From starting hypothesis H.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S6 (secondary, reporting the museum's own account).** A documented
sequence at the Van Abbemuseum: from **2014**, the *Special Guests* programme — multisensory
tours for blind and partially sighted visitors, tours in sign language led by **deaf guides**,
"outspoken" tours for visitors with aphasia, unforgettable tours for people with dementia, later
sensory-friendly hours. Then the museum "recognized something missing in this method" and moved
from programme-specific services to institution-wide change, producing *Delinking and Relinking*
— reported as the Netherlands' first fully multisensory **collection display**, with 25+
multisensory tools — and a stated shift "from an **eyes-only space** to spaces that acknowledge
the body as a whole". Finnegan Shannon's benches (**S3**) have also been installed there.

**MECHANISM.** An access practice developed around one artist, work or programme either stays a
project-bounded accommodation or **migrates into how the institution ordinarily operates** —
into permanent display, staffing, scheduling, furniture, commissioning terms. Which of the two
happened is answerable, and the answer is usually decided after the funding ends.

**QUESTION.** Did this remain an accommodation attached to one project, or did it change how the
place ordinarily works?

**CARRIERS.** A practice appearing in the permanent collection display rather than in a
programme. A role that became a job. A provision that survived the grant. Furniture that stayed.
Commissioning or residency terms rewritten. A budget line that moved from project to core. And
the negative carrier: the date a practice stopped.

**FALSE MOVE.** "Every accessibility improvement is institutional transformation." Most are not.
And a second, specific to this evidence: **an institution's own account of its transformation is
not evidence that it transformed.**

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The practice is temporary and project-specific and disappears when the
artist, grant or programme ends — or persists only in marketing while the operating model is
unchanged.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 14 (access as a process, not a state); reaches Axis 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on infrastructure and what persists.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** It is the **converse of PR002-09** and completes a pair. PR002-09 says
flexibility can absorb deviation and thereby preserve an unchanged norm. This asks the opposite
question of the same situation: **when does the flexibility instead become the norm?** Neither
entry answers the other's question, and together they cover both outcomes.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A pandemic-era remote-working arrangement: policy, or a suspension that
ended. (2) A pilot programme in a health service or a school — did the practice enter standard
operating procedure, or close with the funding?

**BOUNDARY.** **Secondary provenance throughout**, reporting the museum's own claims; the
museum's own inclusion page returned HTTP 500 and no staff member is quoted on disabled people's
role in decision-making. The sequence is credible and **not independently verified.** This is
stated institutional practice, not demonstrated practice.

### PR004-05 — WHICH ONE BENDS: THE PROJECT OR THE FRAME?

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE ✦ **new — not in the starting set; produced by reading S1.**

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S1 (read in full).** Lazard, on the structural difference between large and
small arts organisations: "**Programs and exhibitions tend to bend to the frameworks presented
by large arts institutions, whereas smaller arts institutions can be redefined with each project
they engage.**" She sets this against compliance — "ADA compliance is not a set of predetermined
standards", and organisations under 15 employees are exempt, so many are "motivated by the
desire to protect themselves legally instead of viewing accessibility as indispensable" — and
against a static model of access: accessibility "demands a **malleable infrastructure that
shifts, in real time**, with the needs of the community… **Accessibility is a promise, not a
guarantee. It's a speculative practice.**"

**MECHANISM.** In any hosting arrangement, one of the two parties bends. Either the incoming
project is reshaped to fit the host's existing framework, or the host's framework is redefined
by the project. Which direction it runs is usually a function of the host's size and
bureaucracy rather than of its stated values, and it is rarely acknowledged as a choice.

**QUESTION.** In this arrangement, which side adapted — and what would have had to change for
it to run the other way?

**CARRIERS.** A brief or template applied to every incoming project. A standard contract. An
approvals chain and how many people sit in it. Whether a requirement was met by exception or by
revision. Fixed technical specifications for submissions. The distance between the person
setting the terms and the person doing the work — Lazard's own marker: at a small organisation
"the person introducing the event might also be the person who set out the seats."

**FALSE MOVE.** "Small is good, big is bad." Lazard says large institutions have more financial
resources and is describing a structural tendency, not a moral ranking. The instrument asks
which way the bending ran **here**, not which kind of organisation is better.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The host's framework demonstrably changed to accommodate the project —
a template rewritten, a standard revised — or the project was unchanged and no adaptation was
required of either party.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 14 and Axis 10; reaches Axis 5.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on infrastructure and procurement; ZENO on templates and approval
architecture.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** PR003-08 asks who defined the option set. This asks the prior structural
question — **which party is the one that adapts** — and it applies where no option set is
presented to anyone at all.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) **Platform and publisher:** a small publisher reshaping its books to a
retailer's metadata schema, versus a retailer extending the schema. (2) **Enterprise
procurement:** whether the supplier's product or the buyer's process changed to close the gap.
(3) Standards bodies and small implementers.

**BOUNDARY.** Lazard's document is an **accessibility guide for small arts nonprofits**, not an
aesthetics essay or an empirical study. The observation is offered in passing and is not
evidenced with cases. The generalisation beyond arts organisations is entirely ours.

### PR004-06 — ACCESS CAN BECOME FORM

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **weak; heavy duplication risk; see BOUNDARY.**
*From starting hypothesis A.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S3 (artist primary, read).** Finnegan Shannon's *Do you want us here or not*
(2018–ongoing) is "a series of benches for museum spaces", installed at MUDAM, Van Abbemuseum,
MMK Frankfurt, MCA Denver, Bundeskunsthalle Bonn, Arko Art Center Seoul and Carleton, carrying
handwritten texts: "**This exhibition has asked me to stand for too long. Sit if you agree**";
"Museum visits are hard on my body. Rest here if you agree"; "I wish this museum was more
hospitable to my body's needs." The benches are simultaneously usable seating, artwork, and a
statement about the room they stand in. **The artist's page carries no statement of intent**, so
the tripling is observable in the object and not claimed by the maker.

**MECHANISM.** A provision made to enable participation can also **carry an argument about the
arrangement it sits in** — functioning as accommodation and as commentary at once, without
ceasing to work as either.

**QUESTION.** Does this provision only enable participation, or does it also make a claim about
the arrangement that required it?

**CARRIERS.** A provision that names the condition it answers. Signage that states why it
exists. A workaround left visible rather than tidied away. An accommodation register that is
published. An exception log.

**FALSE MOVE.** "All accessibility is art." No. And a second: treating a provision as critique
because an observer found it eloquent. The claim has to be **in the thing** — in its text, its
placement or its form.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The underlying encounter is unchanged and the provision can be removed
without altering the work's form, interpretation or organisation. **On the available evidence
this may well be true of the benches themselves**: remove them and the exhibition is unchanged,
which is close to their own point.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 14; reaches Axis 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** None distinctively.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Possibly little. The benches' sharpest content — that museum seating is a
**curatorial choice** and not a fact of the building — is **PR003-08 (who defines the option
set)** operating on gallery furniture. That is a duplicate, and it is named here rather than
disguised.

**TRANSFER TEST.** Weak. (1) A "temporary" ramp left in place with its work order attached.
(2) A published exceptions register that doubles as a critique of the default. Neither is
compelling, which is itself evidence about the entry.

**BOUNDARY.** Kept as a candidate only because the double function is genuinely observable in
S3. **Recommended for merge into PR003-08 at owner review unless a case is found where the
provision changes the work's form rather than commenting on its absence.**

### PR004-07 — THE INSTITUTION'S ORDINARY OBJECTS ENCODE A BODY MODEL

**STATUS:** **MERGED — AXIS 1 SHARPENING.** Not a separate instrument. Retained, not deleted.
*From starting hypothesis D.*

**WHY MERGED.** Axis 1 already asks: *what body does this thing assume it is for, and where is
that assumption recorded rather than stated?* Applying it to benches, label heights, gallery
routes, standing durations and security rules produces a **carrier list**, not a new mechanism.
The library's own rule from PR003-01 applies — an orientation with a good carrier set belongs
beneath its axis, not beside it.

**THE DURABLE SHARPENING, beneath Axis 1.** Institutional furniture and procedure are unusually
good carriers because they are **assumed to be neutral infrastructure** and therefore rarely
documented as decisions. Carriers worth keeping: seating and its absence · label and control
heights · route length and sequence with no rest point · standing duration expected by a
programme · queue discipline · door and threshold specifications · toilet provision · guard and
etiquette instructions · re-entry rules. Shannon's benches (S3) are the demonstration that
**seating is a curatorial decision**, which is exactly what makes the carrier legible.

**FALSE MOVE, preserved.** "Everything is secretly ableist." Requires a **concrete action
demand** — a specific thing the arrangement requires a body to do.

### PR004-08 — REST OR PACE AS PART OF THE MEDIUM

**STATUS:** **REJECTED — duplicate of PR002.** Not deleted; kept with its reason.
*From starting hypothesis E.*

**WHY REJECTED.** Tested aggressively against PR002 as instructed, and it is that cluster in
gallery language. **PR002-07** (recovery is part of the task — an institution counts only the
visible performance interval while the body also pays preparation and recovery outside it) owns
the rest claim. **PR002-02** (the route to pace: what capacity a process assumes simply by moving
at this speed) owns the pacing claim. **PR002-01** owns expiry. A durational artwork, an
intermission, a relaxed performance and a drop-in structure are **carriers for those instruments**,
not evidence of a new one.

MMK's *Crip Time* (S8) is the obvious place a new mechanism would have appeared, and on the
available evidence it does not: the exhibition takes crip time as its **subject and framing**,
via Kafer, rather than demonstrating a temporal mechanism the library lacks. That is a finding
about the evidence available to this pass, not a judgement of the exhibition — S8 is partial,
and both the museum's page and the disabled-perspective review were inaccessible.

**WHAT SURVIVES.** The art carriers are donated to PR002: durational work · intermission
structure · seating provision · slow routes · event spacing · quiet hours · re-entry ·
drop-in structures · performance timing.

**Second REJECTED entry in the library**, and the first rejected for being an existing
instrument in another vocabulary. Recorded as a positive result: **art did not grant novelty.**

### PR004-09 — THE TOOL AS MOVEMENT VOCABULARY

**STATUS:** **NEEDS_RESEARCH** — no evidence gathered in this pass.
*From starting hypothesis C.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **None.** The obvious sources — Alice Sheppard / Kinetic Light, and
choreographic practice built on wheelchair dynamics — were **not researched** within this pass's
bounds. Nothing below is supported.

**MECHANISM (hypothesis, unsupported).** A tool, mobility device or architectural element may
**generate forms of movement** rather than enable a pre-existing one.

**QUESTION.** Is this tool assisting an existing action, or producing a movement relation that
would not otherwise exist?

**FALSE MOVE.** Romanticising assistive technology as automatically creative.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The device only restores or enables an already-defined action and
produces no distinct movement relation.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 1 and Axis 5; connects to **PR003-04** (a tool is incorporated by what it
substitutes for) and to the **held Moser distinction** (function restored versus new action made
possible).

**CARRIERS · MINDS SHARPENED · WHAT THIS ADDS · TRANSFER TEST · BOUNDARY — WITHHELD
DELIBERATELY.** Following the precedent set by PR002-08: writing carriers and transfer tests for
an unsupported mechanism is how a hypothesis starts to look established. They are absent because
there is nothing to write them from, not by oversight.

**NOTE.** This is the cluster's clearest evidence gap, and it sits on the same fault line as the
held Moser question. Worth one targeted retrieval; **not worth an instrument until then.**

## THE BRUTUS BOUNDARY CASE

**AUTONOMOUS**, Brutus Rotterdam, 2026, curated by Ine Gevers. Curatorial text **read in full**
(S9, carried from PR001).

**Tested hard, as instructed, and the result is: IMPORTANT EXHIBITION, WEAK PERSPECTIVE
INSTRUMENT.**

**What survives.** Three located mechanisms, already recorded in PR001 and unchanged: algorithms
implicitly setting the norm and **narrowing the bandwidth of what counts as normal** (PR001-01);
neurological differences "reduced to faults or exploited as a new raw material" (PR001-04); and
the exhibition's own term **zelf-normalisatie**, self-normalisation, with the structural point
that there is no longer one oppressive power to name and unmask (PR001-06). Those were extracted
in PR001 and **do not need re-extracting here.**

**What fails.** The curatorial thesis largely **decides its conclusion in advance**. The text
argues from named contemporary political figures, tech-oligarchy and cognitive capitalism to the
position that neurodivergence is a site of resistance — and PR001 already recorded that framing
as **ST002, a source tension explicitly NOT ADOPTED**. Asked of specific works, the question
"what concrete normalising mechanism is actually shown?" is answerable for the exhibition's
*argument* but not, from the material available, for individual artworks.

**Romanticisation risk: HIGH, and named.** The text supplies the exact formulations to refuse —
"anders bedraad zijn" presented "niet als tekort, maar als bevrijding", neurodiversity as "de
meest radicale, transformatieve handeling". Crip Minds does not inherit *neurodivergence =
resistance* or *difference = superior knowledge*. The project genesis forbids exactly this:
contribution must be earned, and deciding in advance that an experience contains an insight is
the failure it names.

**No PR004 entry is built from AUTONOMOUS.**

## THE VAN ABBE INSTITUTIONAL TEST

**What survived.** The **sequence is real and documented**, at secondary provenance: 2014
separate programmes → an internal judgement that "something [was] missing in this method" →
a fully multisensory **collection display** with 25+ tools → a stated vocabulary shift "from an
eyes-only space to spaces that acknowledge the body as a whole". That is the difference between
access programming and curatorial method, and the museum crossed it at least in its own account.

**Institutional change actually shown.** *Delinking and Relinking* as permanent-collection
display rather than a programme; deaf guides leading tours in sign language; the TIK-TIK indoor

the evidence that the *institution* changed is the museum's own narrative. "Van Abbe cares about
inclusion" would be useless; what is usable is the **specific claim that access moved from the
tour into the collection display**, and the specific gap that the operating model is undocumented.
