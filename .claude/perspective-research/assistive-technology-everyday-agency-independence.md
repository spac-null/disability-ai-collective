# Perspective Research 003
## Assistive Technology, Everyday Agency & Independence

**STATUS: WORKING RESEARCH — NOT PRODUCTION DOCTRINE.**
Nothing here is in force. No entry may be quoted, cited, or used to establish anything about
any subject. **Nothing is promoted; owner review comes afterward.**

**CENTRAL QUESTION.** Not "how assistive technology helps disabled people", and not a
catalogue of accessibility products. Rather: **what forms of question-asking intelligence can
disabled people's relationships with support technologies give Crip Minds about agency,
independence, tools and infrastructure in ordinary life?**

**DOMAIN, per the 2026-09-08 owner reframe.** Disabled people living ordinary, self-directed
lives. Institutional care is **not banned**, but nursing homes, residential care,
elderly-care administration and high-dependency settings are **not the default** — and this
cluster does not drift there merely because the literature is easier to find there. Care
remains shared house knowledge under Axis 6.

**NO DEVICE TERRITORY.** No mechanism below belongs to a mind because of its device.
Hearing aids are not PINA's, wheelchairs not MAYA's, screen readers not SIIRI's, adaptive
software not ZENO's. MINDS SHARPENED records which *mechanism* sharpens which instrument,
never which product.

**FIELD SCHEMA.** Entries use the canonical ten instrumentation fields from `README.md`
(MECHANISM · QUESTION · CARRIERS · FALSE MOVE · DISCONFIRMING SHAPE · EXISTING AXIS · MINDS
SHARPENED · WHAT THIS ADDS · TRANSFER TEST · BOUNDARY), under the administrative STATUS and
SOURCE BASIS headers already used in PR001 and PR002. *No contradiction was found and README
was not modified: the ten are the instrumentation; STATUS and SOURCE BASIS are administrative.*

## ENTRIES

### PR003-01 — INDEPENDENCE IS CONTROL OVER SUPPORT

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — *and possibly a foundational orientation rather than a distinct
instrument; flagged for owner review.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S1 (read in full), the definitive statement, ¶16(a):** independent living
means being "provided with all necessary means to enable them to exercise choice and control
over their lives"; it "does not necessarily mean living alone" and "should also not be
interpreted solely as the ability to carry out daily activities by oneself. Rather, it should
be regarded as the freedom to choose and control." ¶16(d) makes it operational for personal
assistance: the service "must be controlled by the person with disability", who can "design
the service and decide by whom, how, when, where and in what way the service is delivered".
¶16(c) supplies a **checkable list of the opposite**: obligatory sharing of assistants, "no
or limited influence over whom one has to accept assistance from", lack of control over
day-to-day decisions, rigidity of routine irrespective of personal will, paternalistic
service provision, supervision of living arrangements.

**MECHANISM.** Independence need not mean acting without support. It may lie in retaining
meaningful control over what support is used, when, how, and on whose terms. The
distinguishing question is therefore not *how much* support is present but *who decides its
shape*.

**QUESTION.** Where is the agency here — in doing without support, or in controlling how
support enters the action?

**CARRIERS.** Settings. Device choice. Assistant or provider choice. Override. On/off
control. Customisation. Repair choice. Transport choice. Communication mode. Consent.
Reversibility. Who recruits, trains, directs and can dismiss. Who holds the budget. Whether
one service is contractually tied to another.

**FALSE MOVE.** *"Everyone is interdependent, therefore all dependencies are equivalent."*
They are not, and this entry exists to keep the distinction: S1 spends ¶16(c) enumerating
exactly which dependencies remove control and which do not.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The person has no meaningful influence over the relevant support
arrangement, and yet experiences no corresponding loss of agency in the action under study.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5 (independence & assistance); reaches Axis 10 (authorship) and Axis
6 (care).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on the arrangement and its economics; SHARED — every mind meets
this wherever a system provides something on someone's behalf.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 5 asks what assistance was hidden inside an independent action.
This asks the adjacent question — *who governs the assistance that is perfectly visible* —
and S1 supplies an unusually concrete test for it in the by-whom/how/when/where/in-what-way
formulation.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A managed IT estate: the same laptop, one org where the user installs
what they need, another where every change is a ticket — identical capability, different
agency. (2) A tenancy where repairs are the landlord's to schedule.

**BOUNDARY.** S1 is a **normative human-rights instrument, not an empirical finding.** It
establishes what independence *ought* to mean in law, not what produces agency in practice.
Do not convert rights language into a claim about how the world works. **Cross-link, not a
new entry:** S1 ¶17 notes that providers "often wrongly describe their service using the
terms 'independent' or 'community living' as well as 'personal assistance'" while failing
the requirements — that is **PR001-09** (the name becomes the explanation) operating in this
domain, and is recorded here rather than duplicated.

### PR003-02 — SUPPORT CAN DISAPPEAR INTO INDEPENDENCE

**STATUS:** **REJECTED — already existing doctrine.** Not deleted; kept with its reason.

**SOURCE BASIS.** Owner starting hypothesis B, checked against the canon as instructed.

**WHY REJECTED.** Axis 5 of the Perspective Doctrine already states this mechanism, close to
verbatim: *"Independence is usually produced by infrastructure, and the infrastructure is
invisible in proportion to how well it works."* Its axis question — *"What assistance has
been built into this so thoroughly that the action now counts as independent?"* — is the
proposed question with different wording. PR001-05 (technology participates in making
ability, APPROVED_DURABLE) covers the technological case beneath it.

Creating a PR003 instrument here would have produced **duplicate knowledge under a new ID**,
which is exactly the failure the library's own rules exist to prevent. The right response to
"we already know this" is to say so, not to restate it more recently.

**WHAT SURVIVES.** Nothing new is needed. The proposed carriers — wheelchair, hearing aid,
glasses, GPS, lift, screen reader, medication reminder, AI assistant, automatic transmission,
battery and network infrastructure — are a **useful carrier list for Axis 5** and are
recorded here for that purpose only.

**FALSE MOVE, preserved because it is worth keeping wherever this idea appears.** *"All
independence is fake."* Absolutely not. Every functioning person is infrastructurally
dependent; the question is which dependencies were made invisible, and that is Axis 5's job.

**NOTE.** This is the **first REJECTED entry in the Perspective Library**, and it is
recorded as a positive result: the hypothesis-generator produced a candidate, the canon
check killed it, and the killing cost one paragraph instead of one instrument.

### PR003-03 — AUTONOMY HAS A MAINTENANCE LAYER

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — *absorbs starting hypothesis H; see MERGE note.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S3 (summary-level, author-reviewed)**, N=591 wheelchair users with spinal
cord injury using a chair ≥40 hours a week, surveyed Oct 2012–May 2015: most reported needing
one or more repairs within six months; repairs to wheels and casters dominated for manual
chairs and electronic/power-system repairs for power chairs; and there are **gaps between
repairs needed and repairs completed**. **S4 (abstract-level)**, 301 wheelchair users with
SCI reporting at least one repair: **76%** had all repairs completed, **7%** some, **14%**
none; the commonest reason for an incomplete repair (**30%**) was **the vendor failing to
complete it after being contacted**; **42%** experienced adverse consequences — being forced
onto a backup chair or confined at home most commonly, also missed work or school, missed
medical appointments, injury, being stranded; and repair processes often required two visits
and took **two weeks to six months**. **S2 (read)** supplies the maker's-eye version: the
disabled designer Alice Loomer described repurposing household items for wheelchair
maintenance, and argued her own tinkering and maintenance practices "kept [her] away from
nursing homes and attendants".

**MECHANISM.** A technology can expand everyday agency while making that agency contingent on
maintenance, charging, repair, parts, software or specialist knowledge that disappears from
view during normal operation. The action looks like the person's; it is in fact the person's
*plus* a supply chain, an authorisation process and somebody else's calendar.

**QUESTION.** What has to keep working behind the scenes for this action to remain
independent?

**CARRIERS.** Battery and charger. Repair interval and queue. Parts supply. Technician
availability. Firmware and software support windows. Tyres and casters. Insurance or funding
authorisation. Calibration. Maintenance schedule. The vendor's own responsiveness. A backup
device, and whether one exists.

**FALSE MOVE.** *"Dependence on maintenance cancels autonomy."* Cars, phones and every piece
of infrastructure require maintenance. The question is which dependency is **hidden**, and
what happens when it becomes **binding** — not whether maintenance exists.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Ordinary failure and maintenance can be handled locally,
immediately, and without materially changing the person's action or agency — a spare in a
drawer, a five-minute fix, a substitutable part.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5 and Axis 14 (access as a process, not a state); reaches Axis 9 via
PR002-05 (the wait changes the state it waits on — a repair queue is that mechanism with a
device in it).

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA primarily — infrastructure, procurement, and the unbooked cost.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 14 asks whether access is a certified state or a performed process.
This names **whose process it is** and what it is made of, with a measurable failure surface.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A hospital imaging suite whose throughput depends on one service
contract and one engineer's travel time. (2) A farm or fleet whose autonomy depends on
manufacturer diagnostic software.

**MERGE NOTE — starting hypothesis H, "failure reveals the real system", is merged here.**
Examined and found to be **the detection method for this mechanism, not a second mechanism**:
its question ("what became visible only when the tool stopped working?") is how one *finds*
the maintenance layer, and its carriers are this entry's carriers in the failure state. H's
warning is preserved as a rule of use: **a failure story is only usable when it exposes a
normally hidden dependency or organisation** — otherwise it is drama, and the disconfirming
shape is that failure reveals nothing beyond the component that failed.

**BOUNDARY.** The empirical spine is wheelchair-specific, US-based, and drawn from spinal
cord injury Model Systems samples; S3 is read at summary level and S4 at abstract level.
**Breakdown frequency is not the insight** — the insight is the organisation that successful
operation normally hides.

### PR003-04 — THE TOOL CAN ENTER THE BODY MODEL

**STATUS:** **NEEDS_RESEARCH**

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S7 NOT ACCESSED** — Papadimitriou's argument is known only through
secondary citation: that through learning to manoeuvre the chair a reversible relation is
established between the moving body-subject and the wheelchair, which "becomes a part of the
wheelchair user's habitual being-in-the-world". **S8 (abstract-level) is the counter-case and
is why this entry is not a candidate:** an intrinsic case study of one man, Patrick, who
underwent ableist rehabilitation to recover walking and to *break* his connection with the
chair, and only "reluctantly assumed his cyborg or posthuman condition" — with enwheelment
framed as a **continuum** and a **struggle** rather than an outcome of skilled use.

**MECHANISM (hypothesis).** Through skilled repeated use, a device may cease to function
experientially as an external object and become incorporated into how a person acts and
perceives reachable space.

**QUESTION.** Is the system treating this device as external assistance after its user has
learned to act through it as part of their ordinary bodily repertoire?

**CARRIERS.** Reach and clearance assumptions in a design. A rule that treats the device as
separable from the person (storage, transport, search, insurance). Training and skill
acquisition records. Body-schema or peripersonal-space measures. Language in a document that
locates capability in the person or in the pair.

**FALSE MOVE.** Claiming every assistive device becomes part of the body. S8 is the standing
refutation of the universal version, and the romantic reading — the chair as liberating
extension — is exactly what a single case of reluctant, contested enwheelment complicates.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Experienced users continue to treat the device as a consciously
external tool, or embodiment measures do not change with active use.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 1 (bodies) and Axis 5; connects to PR001-05.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** SIIRI on perception and reachable space; SHARED.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** *If it survives:* a boundary question — where a system draws the line
between person and equipment, and whether that line matches how the action is actually
performed. It has not survived yet.

**TRANSFER TEST.** *(Held until the primaries are read. Writing transfer tests for an
unsupported mechanism is how a hypothesis starts to look established.)*

**BOUNDARY.** The two things needed are the same two things missing: **S7 in full**, and
empirical body-schema work. Also, **do not generalise spinal-cord-injury experience to
wheelchair users generally** — both S7 and S8 are SCI-specific, and S8 is N=1.

### PR003-05 — SUPPORT WORKS DIFFERENTLY WHEN THE USER AUTHORS IT

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S6 (abstract-level, 1993 — age flagged):** of 227 adults surveyed,
**29.3%** of devices were completely abandoned; four factors were associated with abandonment
— **lack of consideration of user opinion in selection**, ease of device procurement, poor
device performance, and change in user needs; mobility aids were abandoned more often than
other categories, and abandonment peaked in the first year and after five years. Secondary
literature citing this line of work reports that lack of user involvement in selection raised
abandonment likelihood roughly **ninefold** — *reported, not verified in a primary, and used
here only as a signal of direction.* Abandonment rates across the wider literature range from
**8% to 75%** depending on context, which is itself the most disciplining number in the
entry. **S2 (read)** gives the mechanism from the maker's side: Loomer — "I made it. So I
know how to fix it… I may have failed as often as I succeeded, but **I have equipment that
fits me**."

**MECHANISM.** A technically capable device may succeed or fail in daily life partly
according to whether its user helped choose, configure or adapt it. Fit is not a property of
the device; it is a property of the relation between the device and a particular life, and
somebody authored that relation.

**QUESTION.** Who authored the fit between this tool and this life?

**CARRIERS.** Device selection and who was in the room. The prescription or procurement
process. A trial period. Configuration and custom controls. Fine tuning. Return, abandonment
and usage-duration data. Training. Whether the specification was written before or after
meeting the user.

**FALSE MOVE.** *"Users always know best"* or *"professional expertise is bad."* Neither.
The interesting question is the **relation** between expertise and user authorship — and
note that S6 lists *ease of procurement* alongside lack of user opinion, so a frictionless
supply process is no guarantee either. **Device rejection is not user failure.**

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Outcomes and sustained use are unchanged by meaningful differences
in user participation.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 10 (participation & authorship) and Axis 5; reaches Axis 14.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on procurement and provision; ZENO on what a fit is measured
against; SHARED.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 10 asks who authored the conditions. This adds the **measurable
consequence of not having done so** — a thing that was supplied, worked, and stopped being
used.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) Enterprise software procured centrally and quietly replaced by
whatever the team actually uses. (2) A clinical pathway that is technically followed and
routinely worked around.

**BOUNDARY.** The foundational study is **33 years old**, from a different device generation,
and the ninefold figure is secondary. The 8–75% spread means **no abandonment number should
be cited as a fact about assistive technology in general.** What is durable is the direction
and the mechanism, not the magnitude.

### PR003-06 — CALIBRATION IS A SITE OF AGENCY

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE — **the strongest evidence in the cluster.**

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S5 (read in full)** — a crossover clinical trial, N=28, mean age 60.2,
adults with self-perceived mild-to-moderate hearing difficulty, comparing two ways of fitting
the *same* over-the-counter hearing aid: **user self-adjustment** versus **in-situ
audiometry**. The result is the entry:

- **Equivalent** on every objective and aggregate measure — overall APHAB benefit (d = 0.2),
  overall IOI-HA satisfaction (r = 0.0), speech-in-noise performance on DIN and QuickSIN, and
  **real-ear measurements across all sound levels**.
- **Different** on two lived-outcome subscales, both favouring self-adjustment: **daily use**
  (r = −0.3) and **satisfaction** (r = −0.4).

The authors' own conclusion: "Self-adjustment may produce higher satisfaction and longer
daily use, highlighting the potential advantages of active user involvement in the fitting
process."

**MECHANISM.** A device may not have one objectively correct setting — and even where a
technical measurement can be equalised, **who is permitted to adjust it** can still determine
whether the support is used. The measurable output was the same; the relationship to it was
not.

**QUESTION.** Who is permitted to decide when this device is correctly adjusted?

**CARRIERS.** Hearing-aid fine tuning. Wheelchair seating and control settings. Prosthetic
tuning. Screen-reader preferences. Adaptive UI. AI personalisation. Threshold settings. A
locked configuration. A professional-only mode. A "reset to recommended" that cannot be
overridden.

**FALSE MOVE.** Assuming subjective preference should replace every technical measurement.
S5 does **not** show that self-fitting is better — it shows equivalence on the technical
measures and a difference in use and satisfaction. Writing "self-fitting is better" would be
overstating the same paper that disciplines the claim.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** User-adjustable settings produce no meaningful difference in use,
satisfaction, performance or control — or introduce a demonstrated safety constraint that
requires another arrangement.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5 and Axis 12 (measurement — here, a measure that is equal while the
outcome is not); reaches Axis 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** ZENO on what the instrument treats as the correct value; PINA on a
mediating device whose settings decide what arrives; SHARED. **Not assigned by device.**

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** A precise and unusually clean instance of a general shape the doctrine
lacks in this form: **an objective measure can be satisfied while the thing it was measuring
for fails to happen.** Real-ear measurement equalised; daily use did not.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A thermostat or lighting system commissioned to a standard and then
overridden daily by occupants. (2) A recommendation or safety threshold tuned centrally,
where the locally adjustable version is the one that stays switched on.

**BOUNDARY.** **N=28**, short-term, no washout period, pseudorandomised after an app
malfunction compromised randomisation, and limited to self-perceived mild-to-moderate
difficulty — all stated by the authors. Long-term outcomes were explicitly not assessable.
One device category, one population.

### PR003-07 — A TOOL CAN MOVE DEPENDENCE RATHER THAN REMOVE IT

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S3 (summary-level)** supplies a measured transfer rather than an
assertion: **a higher proportion of manual wheelchair users completed the repair at home
themselves, while a higher proportion of power wheelchair users had the repair completed at
home by a vendor.** The more capable device moved repair authorship from the user to a
company. **S4 (abstract-level)** shows what that dependency costs when it binds: the
commonest cause of an incomplete repair was the **vendor not completing it**, at 30%.
**S2 (read)** gives the counter-position from disabled makers — Whirlwind Wheelchair
International's low-cost open-source chairs "intended to be maintainable for a lifetime", and
Loomer's insistence that having made it is what let her fix it.

**MECHANISM.** A device can remove dependence on one person or system while creating a new
dependency on a manufacturer, network, professional, software stack or supply chain. The
dependency does not vanish; it relocates, and usually becomes less visible and less
negotiable in the process.

**QUESTION.** Which dependency disappeared, and where did it reappear?

**CARRIERS.** Proprietary parts. Vendor lock-in. Cloud accounts. Firmware and its update
policy. Licensing. Specialist servicing. Insurance authorisation. Battery ecosystems. An app
requirement. Diagnostic tools available only to dealers.

**FALSE MOVE.** Treating transferred dependency as proof the technology failed. **The new
dependency may be dramatically preferable** — a power chair that a vendor services may enable
a life a manual chair did not. The question is what the trade actually was, not that a trade
occurred.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The new dependency is weak, substitutable and does not constrain
meaningful choice or action — parts are generic, servicing is competitive, the account can be
left.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 5; reaches Axis 14 and Axis 10.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on supply chains and procurement; ZENO on lock-in as a
classification of who may act.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 5 asks what assistance is hidden. This asks about **movement** —
dependency as something with a before and after location, which is far more checkable than
dependency in general.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) Right-to-repair in agricultural machinery: mechanical independence
traded for a diagnostic licence. (2) A self-hosted service moved to managed cloud — the
sysadmin dependency replaced by a vendor's roadmap.

**BOUNDARY.** The measured evidence is **one domain** (wheelchair repair) and read at summary
and abstract level. Distinctness from PR003-03 rests on that manual-versus-power finding: 03
is about the maintenance layer *existing*, 07 about **where it sits and who owns it**. A
second domain would settle whether that distinction holds; without one, a merge at owner
review is reasonable.

### PR003-08 — BOUNDED CHOICE INSIDE A FIXED ARRANGEMENT

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE
*New. Not in the starting set — produced by reading S1.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S1 ¶16(c) (read in full)**, in the Committee's own words: "Institutional
settings **may offer persons with disabilities a certain degree of choice and control;
however, these choices are limited to specific areas of life and do not change the segregating
character** of institutions." The same paragraph makes the defining elements checkable —
obligatory sharing of assistants, "no or limited influence over whom one has to accept
assistance from", lack of control over day-to-day decisions, rigidity of routine irrespective
of personal will, paternalistic provision, supervision. And ¶17 names the contractual form:
"mandatory 'package solutions' which, among other things, link the availability of one
particular service to another".

**MECHANISM.** A system can offer real, exercisable choice **within** a frame the person
cannot alter, and the offered choice can make the fixed frame harder to see — not because it
is fake, but because it is genuine and satisfying at the level it operates. What is
unavailable is the choice about the frame itself.

**QUESTION.** What is genuinely choosable here, and what does the range of choices leave
untouched?

**CARRIERS.** A configurable menu with no exit. A tariff or plan selector. A consent
interface offering preferences but not refusal. A rota one may express preferences about but
not set. A "customise your experience" panel. A package that ties one service to another.
The boundary between a setting and a term of service.

**FALSE MOVE.** Concluding that offered choice is therefore a trick, or that bounded choice
is worthless. S1 does not say the choices are unreal — it says they are **limited to specific
areas of life** and do not change the arrangement's character. The reading needs both halves:
the real choice, and the specific thing it cannot reach.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** The choices on offer can in fact alter the frame — a preference that
changes the rota, an option that ends the contract, a setting that removes the constraint.
Then it is choice, not bounded choice.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 10 (participation & authorship) and Axis 11 (classification); reaches
Axis 5.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** ZENO on the boundary and what it forecloses; MAYA on authorship of
conditions.

**WHAT THIS ADDS.** Axis 10 distinguishes being admitted to conditions from authoring them.
This adds the **intermediate and commonest case**: being given genuine authority over the
furniture and none over the room. It also pairs with PR002-09 — bounded choice, like an
accommodation, can be what makes an unchanged arrangement tolerable enough to persist.

**TRANSFER TEST.** (1) A cookie or privacy panel with granular toggles and no way to decline
the underlying collection. (2) A shift-bidding system where staff rank preferences within a
roster whose shape is fixed elsewhere.

**BOUNDARY.** S1 is **normative, not empirical**, and states this about institutions
specifically. The generalisation to any bounded-choice arrangement is ours and is untested.
And the owner reframe applies: the *mechanism* is the transferable part — **this entry must
not pull PR003 back toward institutional care as its default subject.**

### PR003-09 — WHAT YOU AUTHORED, YOU CAN KEEP WORKING

**STATUS:** CANDIDATE
*New. Not in the starting set — produced by reading S2 against S3.*

**SOURCE BASIS.** **S2 (read in full).** The disabled designer Alice Loomer, a wheelchair
user, on her own repurposing and maintenance practices: "**I made it. So I know how to fix
it…** I may have failed as often as I succeeded, but I have equipment that fits me" — and her
claim that this tinkering "kept [her] away from nursing homes and attendants". Hamraie and
Fritsch read her as turning "to maintenance practices as sites for examining the frictions,
limitations, and failures inherent to technoscientific design processes". The manifesto's
first commitment is that "disabled people are experts and designers of everyday life", and it
notes the "widespread perception that access technologies are made for us by non-disabled
experts" with "little recognition of our own practices of remaking the material world". Set
against **S3's** finding that manual-chair users more often repair at home themselves while
power-chair users more often wait for a vendor.

**MECHANISM.** Authorship and maintainability are **the same capability, not two**. Whoever
configured, adapted or built a thing generally retains the knowledge required to keep it
working; whoever received it finished must summon someone else. So a decision about who
authors a tool is also, silently, a decision about who can repair it and how fast.

**QUESTION.** Does the person who has to keep this working know how it was put together?

**CARRIERS.** Documentation and whether the user has it. Open versus proprietary design. Who
holds diagnostic access. Whether adaptation is permitted or voids something. A build-versus-
buy decision and its downstream service model. The gap between the people who specified a
thing and the people who must sustain it. Handover records.

**FALSE MOVE.** Romanticising DIY. Hamraie and Fritsch are explicit — they recognise these
practices "**without glorifying do-it-yourself design practices**", and Loomer herself says
she "failed as often as I succeeded". Self-built is not automatically better; it is
differently *sustainable*, and often only because nothing else fit.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** Users who authored the thing depend on external repair at the same
rate and speed as those who received it finished — or the authored version proves *less*
maintainable because it is unsupported and idiosyncratic.

**EXISTING AXIS.** Axis 10 and Axis 14; reaches Axis 5.

**MINDS SHARPENED.** MAYA on maintenance economics; ZENO on what a design forecloses;
