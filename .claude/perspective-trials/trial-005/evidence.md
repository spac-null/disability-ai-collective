# Trial 005 — Targeted Research

## HYPOTHESIS (the frozen question, applied to the target)

**HYPOTHESIS.** A real, measurable, patterned discrepancy exists between
journalism's stated parity standard (corrections should get "as much
prominence as the error itself had") and actual practice.

**EVIDENCE SOUGHT.** Whether anyone has actually measured
correction-vs-error placement comparably (per `seed.md`'s method), and
whether real, dated, comparable instances show the predicted pattern.

**EVIDENCE FOUND.** Strong, real, and decisive — though not in the exact
artifact-form Kim's own work took (no one has produced a Kim-style visual
dataset). Three independent, real threads converge:

1. **A real systematic study exists — but it measured something else.**
   "How Complete are Newspaper Corrections?" (Media Ethics Magazine),
   analyzing 631 corrections from 70 U.S. newspapers (2005), built on a
   1986 Gannett Center methodology (D. Charles Whitney), measured
   *completeness of content* (error identification, explanation,
   prevention, apology) — not placement/prominence relative to the
   original error. Only 25.4% explained why an error occurred; only 3.8%
   included an apology. This confirms real, comparable, multi-instance
   measurement of corrections *has* been done by ordinary media-ethics
   scholarship — using a different dimension than the one the seed
   predicted.
2. **A structural policy fact, real and dated.** The New York Times has,
   since 1972, published *all* corrections on a fixed page (A2) —
   regardless of where the original story ran. Other outlets adopted the
   same convention. This is a structural mechanism that decouples
   correction prominence from original-error prominence by design, for
   every correction, not an occasional lapse.
3. **A real, adjudicated regulatory dispute, directly on point.** In 2011,
   Full Fact (a fact-checking organization) filed a complaint against the
   Daily Mail over two front-page factual errors, explicitly demanding
   front-page corrections; the Daily Mail offered page-two corrections
   instead. The UK Press Complaints Commission ruled *for* the newspaper:
   a standardized, lower-prominence corrections column can constitute "due
   prominence" even for a front-page error, weighed against factors like
   the error's "scale," "context," and whether it caused direct personal
   harm — explicitly rejecting the strict parity principle RTDNA's
   language states in the abstract. (Contrast: in Kenya, the Media
   Council's Complaints Commission ruled the opposite way in a comparable
   case, ordering a correction to run in the same section as the original
   article — showing this is a live, contested, cross-jurisdictional
   question, not a settled non-issue.)

**SOURCE STATUS.** (2) and the PCC ruling in (3) verified via multiple
independent, consistent search results; the Media Ethics Magazine study
(1) was read via a direct content summary of the article itself. None of
these were fetched from a primary court/regulator document directly in
this pass (the PCC ruling's exact case reference and date were not
independently confirmed beyond the secondary account); sufficient to
establish the pattern, not sufficient to quote as verbatim legal text.

**SUPPORTS / WEAKENS / KILLS: SUPPORTS**, decisively, for the underlying
mechanism (a real, structural, sometimes formally adjudicated gap between
stated correction-parity principles and actual practice). Does **not**
confirm the specific artifact-level claim that this has been measured the
way Kim measured it (spatial area, many dated instances, one
side-by-side visual record) — that specific comparative-visualization has
apparently not been done for this domain. The underlying mechanism the
seed predicted is real; the exact method is unreplicated.

## v1.2 PERSPECTIVE CONTRIBUTION CHECK

**Did the disability-rooted seed contribute something ordinary
journalism-ethics/media-criticism expertise would not already give us?**

No, on the evidence actually found. This is the clearest case yet of
GENERIC_DOMAIN_KNOWLEDGE among all five trials, for a specific reason none
of the earlier ones had: the exact tension the seed predicted — a stated
prominence-parity standard versus actual placement practice — has already
been **identified, formally complained about, and adjudicated** by
ordinary journalism-accountability actors (Full Fact, a professional
fact-checking watchdog; the Press Complaints Commission, a professional
press regulator) using their own ordinary methods (formal complaints,
regulatory rulings, published ethics codes), entirely independent of Kim's
work, any disability-rooted framing, or this trial. This is not a case of
"ordinary experts *could* find this if they looked" (the shape in Trials
002/003); it is a case of ordinary experts having *already* litigated it,
years before this trial, for reasons having nothing to do with disability.

Kim's specific three-part taxonomy (token/self-congratulatory presence,
exile to a separate channel, outright omission) also does not add
distinguishable explanatory precision beyond what journalism-ethics
discourse already has in its own vocabulary (the PCC's own multi-factor
"due prominence" test; the 2005 study's own seven completeness criteria) —
both already sort correction adequacy into multiple graded categories
without needing Kim's taxonomy to do so.

**What the seed *did* contribute, honestly stated:** a genuine research
direction where the cold baseline (`baseline.md`) found nothing at all.
The seed's comparative-measurement framing correctly predicted that real,
comparable, structural evidence would exist and pointed research at real
material (NYT policy, PCC rulings) efficiently. That is a real research
win over the bounded, unaided baseline probe specifically — but it is not
the same as a *distinctive disability-rooted contribution to the
mechanism's explanation*, which is what v1.2 actually tests.

**CLASSIFICATION: GENERIC_DOMAIN_KNOWLEDGE.**
