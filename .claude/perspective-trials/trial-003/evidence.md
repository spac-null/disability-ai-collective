# Trial 003 — Targeted Research

## HYPOTHESIS 1 (the only candidate that passed v1.1)

**HYPOTHESIS.** The corner-casting/twist-lock standard is universal for
equipment compatibility but assumes a constant, unvarying physical capacity
(reach, grip, balance, sustained awkward posture, often at height and under
time pressure) from the worker who has to operate it by hand — an
assumption the equipment's own "universal, cargo-agnostic" design origin
story never had to confront, because it was never designed around a body
at all.

**EVIDENCE SOUGHT.** A documented occupational-safety or ergonomics record
naming twist-lock/lashing operation specifically — not manual cargo
handling in general — as an injury mechanism.

**EVIDENCE FOUND.** Direct and decisive, from multiple independent lines:

1. **OSHA Fact Sheet FS-3583, "Freeing Inoperable Semi-Automatic Twist
   Locks (SATLs)"** (osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA_FS-3583.pdf
   — the PDF itself returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; content confirmed via
   independent secondary summaries, including a dedicated safety-training
   page quoting it directly, and cross-checked against multiple search
   results describing the same fact sheet and the same incident in
   materially identical terms). SATLs jam from broken unlocking "pigtails,"
   corner-casting damage from rough handling, upside-down installation, and
   saltwater corrosion. OSHA's own case example: "A foreman was killed
   after attempting to manually hold open a SATL while the attached
   container was hoisted. The container broke free, swung violently, and
   crushed the foreman against a handrail" — in another account of the same
   incident, he was then thrown overboard. OSHA's recommended mitigations:
   supervision, operator training, fall protection, continuous
   crane-operator/ground-crew communication, and specialized tools to force
   open stuck locks rather than holding them by hand.
2. **Peer-reviewed ergonomics literature, subject-specific.** A
   ScienceDirect paper, "A risk assessment framework based on ergonomic
   methods and AHP for prioritizing interventions to prevent container
   terminal operator's musculoskeletal disorders," identifies twist-lock
   handling (long-rod and short-rod variants) as among the *most* harmful
   tasks for terminal operators' musculoskeletal systems specifically —
   not manual handling generally.
3. A published case study, "Product-service-system design thinking for
   port healthcare: a case study with container lashers in the port of
   Antwerp," and a wearable-motion-capture ergonomic assessment of
   "Container Lashing Teams" specifically (Springer), confirm this is an
   active, named subfield of maritime occupational-health research, not an
   inference of mine from adjacent facts.

**SOURCE STATUS.** OSHA content verified via multiple independent secondary
sources describing the same document and incident in consistent detail
(direct PDF fetch blocked, 403, matching the pattern seen with other .gov
domains in this project); the peer-reviewed and case-study sources are
represented by their own publication metadata (ScienceDirect, Springer,
academia.edu), not read in full text in this pass — titles and abstracts
only. Sufficient to establish the mechanism is real and documented; not
sufficient to quote beyond what is given above.

**SUPPORTS / WEAKENS / KILLS: SUPPORTS**, decisively and specifically —
clears the disconfirming-shape bar set in `perspective.md` (this is
twist-lock/lashing-specific, not generic manual-labor material) with room
to spare, including a documented fatality.

## v1.2 PERSPECTIVE CONTRIBUTION CHECK

**What did the disability-rooted question let us see that ordinary
subject-domain analysis would not already give us?**

Nothing distinctive. Ergonomics and occupational-safety engineering is
*itself* the field whose founding question is "what does this equipment's
design assume about the body operating it, and where does that assumption
fail" — Axis 1's question, applied to a worker rather than a "user," is not
a disability-rooted reframing of an ordinary safety question; it is the
ordinary safety question. Every source found here (OSHA, the ScienceDirect
risk-assessment framework, the Antwerp lashers case study, the Springer
ergonomic-assessment paper) reached this finding using ergonomics' own
established methods — biomechanical risk-factor analysis, incident
investigation, AHP-based intervention prioritization — with no
disability-rooted question anywhere in the chain. A labor-safety journalist
or an ergonomics researcher covering "dangers of container shipping" would
find this exact material as a first step, not as something a distinctively
Crip Minds question uncovered that domain expertise would have missed.

**CLASSIFICATION: GENERIC_DOMAIN_KNOWLEDGE.**

Not BASELINE_EQUIVALENT, because baseline (this trial) correctly found
nothing at all — there is no baseline reading to be equivalent *to*. The
gap this trial demonstrates is different from Trial 002's: there,
Perspective converged on what baseline had already reached; here,
Perspective's systematic sweep reached somewhere baseline's bounded single
pass never went, but the destination is still fully explained by an
existing field (occupational ergonomics) without any disability-rooted
contribution — GENERIC_DOMAIN_KNOWLEDGE covers both shapes.
