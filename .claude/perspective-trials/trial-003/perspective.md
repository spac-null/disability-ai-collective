## HYPOTHESIS 1

**HYPOTHESIS.** The corner-casting/twist-lock connector was designed and
released specifically to be a universal, cargo-agnostic mechanical
standard — but "universal" describes the equipment's compatibility with
other equipment, not its compatibility with the range of human bodies that
have to physically operate it. In practice, engaging and releasing a
twist-lock, and lashing/unlashing containers stacked on deck, is manual
work performed by a person's hands, at height, often from an awkward
posture, under time pressure (ships need fast turnaround). The standard
assumes a worker who can reach, grip, and hold a stable position on a
moving or elevated surface for as long and as often as the job requires,
and treats that capacity as a constant rather than a variable the equipment
was ever designed around.

**QUESTION.** Is this assumption caught in writing anywhere specific to
this subject — a documented occupational-safety record, an industry
ergonomics study, or a labor-safety regulation that names twist-lock/lashing
operation specifically (not manual cargo handling in general) as a source of
injury or accommodation need?

**PREDICTED DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE.** An OSHA, ILO, port-authority, or
maritime-union safety record identifying twist-lock/container-lashing
operation by name as a documented injury mechanism (musculoskeletal strain,
falls from height), ideally with an equipment or procedural redesign
proposed or adopted in response.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** If what's found is only generic "manual labor is
physically demanding" material with no connection to the specific
twist-lock/lashing task, or generic dockworker-safety material that would
be identical for any pre-container break-bulk cargo job, the hypothesis
does not clear the bar — it would be indistinguishable from ordinary
occupational-safety knowledge with nothing subject-specific about *this*
equipment's design.

**INSTRUMENT(S).** No exact durable-instrument match; closest doctrine
territory is Axis 1's CARRIER list ("a standard, tolerance or threshold...
a stated design rationale") and Axis 9 (endurance and accumulated cost).

**MIND(S) SHARPENED.** None of the four assigned cleanly; closest is MAYA
(agency/maintenance territory) by the doctrine's own persona-agnostic rule
("no persona territory... all minds may use all shared knowledge").

## HYPOTHESIS 2 — generated, then dropped by the PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK

**HYPOTHESIS.** ISO 668 is a classification system: it decides what counts
as a "standard" container and, by construction, what does not (odd
dimensions, non-conforming equipment, break-bulk cargo that never
containerized). Axis 11's own language — "what does this classification
have to decide, what does it therefore make invisible" — pattern-matches
this closely.

**PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK:** Does this identify an assumption about a
*person's* body, perception, timing, dependence, navigation, cognition, or
classification — or only an interesting mechanism about cargo/equipment
classification? **Only the latter.** ISO 668 classifies boxes, not bodies.
Nothing in the material ties the standard's boundary to any person's
classification, eligibility, or diagnosis. This is the same shape Trial
001 and Trial 002 each caught once already: an axis's vocabulary
("classification," "boundary," "what it makes invisible") matching an
institutional/equipment fact with no person behind it.

**DROPPED BEFORE TARGETED RESEARCH.**

## HYPOTHESIS 3 — generated, then refused without research (reversed-order risk)

**HYPOTHESIS (as it would have to be stated to reach for a bodily
reading).** Standard containers are also used to ship temperature- or
handling-sensitive medical material (insulin, vaccines, medical equipment)
that some disabled and chronically ill people depend on; the standard's
handling/thermal assumptions could create a body-relevant failure point.

**Refused before any search was proposed, per Calibration Case 7 (THE
REVERSED ORDER) and the NO PROXIES rule.** This hypothesis cannot honestly
be stated as something Step One's material *already* points toward — it
was constructed backward, starting from "disabled people need medication"
and reaching for a shipping-standard hook, rather than the material itself
suggesting a body-shaped question. Nothing in `subject.md` mentions medical
cargo, cold-chain shipping, or anything adjacent to it. Writing this down as
a live candidate and then sending it to research would be indistinguishable
from Case 7's bad reading: "a search that turns up the words [insulin,
vaccine, medical] with a reading built backwards to fit them." Recorded
here specifically because refusing it is itself the correct application of
doctrine, not because it deserved research.

## SELECTION

Two hypotheses generated and disposed of without research (H2: dropped by
v1.1; H3: refused as a reversed-order construction, never a live candidate
to begin with). **H1 is the only hypothesis that passes v1.1** (it names an
assumption about a worker's body — reach, grip, balance, endurance — not an
institution) and is sent to targeted research as the strongest, indeed the
only, surviving candidate.
