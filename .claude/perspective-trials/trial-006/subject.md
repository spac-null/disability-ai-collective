# Trial 006 — Target Subject (selected after the seed question was frozen)

## TARGET

Earthquake magnitude reporting: the USGS's practice of issuing an
automated, algorithmic **preliminary magnitude** within minutes of an
earthquake, followed later (hours to weeks) by a human-**reviewed final
magnitude** — the same underlying event, two formally different outputs.

## WHY IT IS DISTANT FROM THE SEED DOMAIN

Geophysical instrumentation and seismic-network data processing — no
connection to Deafness, interpreting services, captioning, broadcast
accessibility, journalism correction placement, art exhibitions, or
communication-access policy. Different vocabulary entirely (no
"translation," "interpretation," "channel," or "prominence" language in
how seismologists describe this practice) — not a domain substitution
that reuses the seed's own terms, a genuinely separate institutional
practice that happens to share the underlying epistemic shape the frozen
question asks about.

## WHERE THE FROZEN QUESTION PREDICTS EVIDENCE MIGHT EXIST

USGS's own public magnitude-reporting documentation and practice
(whether preliminary and final magnitudes are formally and consistently
labeled as different categories); real cases where a preliminary
magnitude was later revised and what happened to public/institutional
understanding in the interim (news coverage, emergency response
decisions, aid/insurance triggers keyed to magnitude thresholds); and
whether any documented confusion or consequence traces specifically to
the *provisional-vs-final* distinction not being marked or understood,
as opposed to simple measurement error.

Selected before any deep research into USGS practice specifically — this
record was written from general knowledge that preliminary/final
magnitude reporting is a real USGS practice, not from having already
confirmed the mechanism the frozen question predicts.
