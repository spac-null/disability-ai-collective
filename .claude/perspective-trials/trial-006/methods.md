## METHOD 1 (subject-native, mandatory)

**METHOD / DISCIPLINE:** Seismology / seismic-network instrumentation
science.

**WHY SELECTED NOW:** Mandatory per the trial's own rule — the target's
own serious domain method, so the collision cannot manufacture a
mechanism the field has already fully articulated for itself (the Trial
005 failure mode).

**WHAT UNIT IT NATURALLY STUDIES:** Physical wave properties of a single
seismic event — amplitude, moment tensor, rupture duration, waveform data
from a network of stations — treated as a single object whose true
magnitude exists and is progressively better estimated as more data and
review time become available.

**WHAT KIND OF EVIDENCE IT TREATS AS STRONG:** Waveform data from more
stations, more complete moment-tensor analysis, human review against
automated algorithms; strength is measured by convergence toward a
better-supported physical estimate, not by social or institutional
consequence.

## METHOD 2 (orthogonal)

**METHOD / DISCIPLINE:** Parametric risk / actuarial and insurance
science.

**WHY SELECTED NOW:** The frozen question asks what happens when a fast,
provisional output is relied on as if it were final. Parametric
insurance and disaster-response triggers are a real institutional
practice built entirely around treating a *reported number* — not the
physical event itself — as the thing a contract or protocol acts on.
This is a genuinely different unit of attention from seismology's own,
selected because it is orthogonal to the subject's native method, not
because it is already known to produce an interesting answer.

**WHAT UNIT IT NATURALLY STUDIES:** A contractual trigger condition — a
specific number, from a specific named data source, at or by a specific
time — treated as the operative fact regardless of whether a later,
more accurate number later supersedes it. The underlying physical event
is not the object of study; the *reported value at the moment of
decision* is.

**WHAT KIND OF EVIDENCE IT TREATS AS STRONG:** Contract language defining
which data source and timing is authoritative for a payout or protocol
trigger; documented cases where a payout or response decision was made
on a value that was later revised.

## METHOD 3 (orthogonal, optional — used)

**METHOD / DISCIPLINE:** Behavioral decision science (judgment under
uncertainty; anchoring).

**WHY SELECTED NOW:** The frozen question's second half — whether a later,
accurate output has to *displace* something already settled rather than
simply complete an open record — is a claim about human/institutional
psychology, not physics or contract law. Anchoring (the well-established
finding that an initially received value continues to shape judgment even
after it is known to be revised or arbitrary) is the established method
for exactly this question, selected because it is orthogonal to both
Methods 1 and 2, not because the answer is already known.

**WHAT UNIT IT NATURALLY STUDIES:** A single decision-maker's (or
population's) judgment before and after exposure to a revised number;
whether and how much the revision actually moves belief or action.

**WHAT KIND OF EVIDENCE IT TREATS AS STRONG:** Experimental or field
evidence that a revised number failed to fully update behavior/belief
that had formed around the initial number — not merely that a revision
happened, but that it was measurably resisted or incompletely absorbed.
