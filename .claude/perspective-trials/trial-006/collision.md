# Trial 006 — Contradiction Round and Surviving Collision Question

The three independent readings (`readings.md`) are now revealed to each
other. Each method challenges exactly one other.

---

## CHALLENGE: Method 2 (parametric risk) → Method 1 (seismology)

**WHAT YOUR METHOD THINKS IT IS MISSING:** Reading 1 treats the danger as
universal — anyone consuming an unreviewed magnitude is at risk of
treating it as authoritative — but Reading 2 shows that at least one whole
class of serious, high-stakes downstream user (parametric-insurance
contracts) has already engineered around this entirely, by specifying
data vintage and timing as contract terms and simply waiting. Reading 1's
"blind spot" framing (does the label make the danger visible?) assumes the
main lever is *communication clarity at the point of consumption*; Reading
2 shows a different lever entirely — *procedural deferral built into the
downstream institution's own structure*, which doesn't depend on any
individual correctly reading a label at all.

**WHY THIS IS A METHODOLOGICAL DISAGREEMENT:** A difference in *unit of
analysis* (the individual reader interpreting a label, vs. the downstream
institution's own procedural design) and in *predicted intervention point*
(fix the label vs. fix the process).

**WHAT DIFFERENT EVIDENCE WOULD RESOLVE IT:** Whether emergency-response
protocols (not financial contracts) have any equivalent of the insurance
contract's "wait for the reviewed number" procedural rule — or whether
they, like Reading 1 assumes for "the public," rely on individual
responders correctly interpreting a label in real time.

## CHALLENGE: Method 3 (anchoring) → Method 2 (parametric risk)

**WHAT YOUR METHOD THINKS IT IS MISSING:** Reading 2 concludes the field
"solved" the problem because the *payout* is contractually tied to the
final number. But anchoring research doesn't predict effects on payouts —
it predicts effects on *belief and pricing behavior by human and market
participants* in the interim window, regardless of what a contract
eventually settles on. A market can still move on the preliminary number
even though the bond itself cannot be triggered by it.

**WHY THIS IS A METHODOLOGICAL DISAGREEMENT:** A difference in *causal
explanation* — Reading 2 treats contractual precision as sufficient
protection against the mechanism; Reading 3 treats the mechanism as
operating on human judgment independent of what the contract formally
governs, so contractual precision protects the *outcome* (the payout) but
not necessarily the *process* (pricing, reinsurance behavior, public
expectation) leading up to it.

**WHAT DIFFERENT EVIDENCE WOULD RESOLVE IT:** Documented bond-price or
reinsurance-market movement in the window between a major earthquake and
final USGS parameter publication — the "near-miss" case flagged in Reading
2 is exactly where this could be checked.

## CHALLENGE: Method 1 (seismology) → Method 3 (anchoring)

**WHAT YOUR METHOD THINKS IT IS MISSING:** Reading 3's implicit remedy —
communicate uncertainty better, since anchoring is "difficult to overcome
even with awareness" — risks treating the problem as unsolvable at the
individual level and stopping there. Reading 1's own evidence (the
errata page's existence, documenting "serious errors" from unreviewed
data reaching "response organizations and the public") shows the field
itself already tried the labeling remedy Reading 3 would consider
insufficient — and that its own record of continued errors suggests
either the label isn't reaching the point of use, or Reading 3 is right
that labeling alone can't overcome anchoring, and something structural
(closer to Reading 2's contractual deferral) is required instead.

**WHY THIS IS A METHODOLOGICAL DISAGREEMENT:** A difference in *predicted
observation* — Reading 1, taken alone, would predict that better labeling
reduces errata-documented errors; Reading 3 predicts labeling has a
ceiling regardless of clarity, because anchoring persists "even with
awareness" per its own literature.

**WHAT DIFFERENT EVIDENCE WOULD RESOLVE IT:** Whether USGS errata cases
specifically trace to *absent or unclear* labeling (which labeling could
fix) versus cases where the label was present and understood but the
preliminary number was acted on anyway (which would confirm Reading 3's
prediction over Reading 1's).

## CONVERGENCE OR NEW QUESTION?

Not convergence — the three readings disagree about where the actual
mechanism lives (a communication-design problem; a solved-elsewhere
problem; an unsolvable-by-labeling cognitive problem), and the
disagreement is genuine (different predicted observations, not different
vocabulary for the same claim).

## SURVIVING COLLISION QUESTION

**THE SURVIVING QUESTION:** Emergency-response and public consumption of
a preliminary earthquake magnitude relies on an individual correctly
reading and acting on a label ("automatic report, not yet reviewed") —
the same kind of safeguard behavioral science's own literature holds is
insufficient against anchoring even when present and understood. Does any
institutional response process that acts on earthquake magnitude in the
first hours or days (disaster response triggering, aid allocation,
early damage/casualty estimation, not financial contracts, which already
structurally defer) have a *procedural* deferral mechanism analogous to
parametric insurance's contractual one — a rule that structurally waits
for or explicitly discounts the unreviewed number, rather than relying on
whoever is reading it to correctly weigh its provisional status? Or does
real-world emergency response run structurally closer to "the public"
than to "the bond market" — informationally protected in theory by a
label, but not procedurally protected the way a sophisticated financial
counterparty is?

**WHICH COLLISION PRODUCED IT:** The three-way contradiction, specifically
the combination of Method 2's discovery that a *structural, non-labeling*
solution exists and is actually used somewhere real, and Method 3's
finding that labeling alone is known to be an insufficient safeguard
against anchoring even when clear. Neither alone produces this question:
Method 1 alone would ask "is the label good enough" (assumes labeling is
the right lever); Method 3 alone would ask "does revision change belief"
(a general behavioral question, not tied to any specific institutional
remedy); Method 2 alone would conclude the problem is solved (because it
only examined the one domain that already solved it for itself).

**WHAT ONE METHOD COULD NOT SEE WITHOUT THE OTHER:** Method 1 could not
have proposed procedural deferral as the actual fix, because seismology's
own frame treats the remedy as a communication/labeling question, not an
institutional-design question — it took Method 2's domain to show that a
non-labeling remedy exists and is real. Method 2 could not have seen that
its own "solved" conclusion needed to be checked against Method 3's
finding that labeling-based safeguards (which is closer to what
non-financial institutions use) are known to be weak — without Method 3,
Method 2's confidence that contractual precision fully protects the
process would have gone unchallenged.

**PREDICTED DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE:** Documented emergency-response,
disaster-relief, or humanitarian-aid protocols that name a rule for how
(or whether) they act on preliminary vs. reviewed earthquake magnitude —
and, if available, any documented case where a real response or aid
decision was made on a preliminary magnitude that the final review later
substantially changed.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE:** If emergency-response and humanitarian protocols
turn out to have their own procedural deferral rules (analogous to
insurance contracts' "wait for final parameters"), or if no documented
case exists where the preliminary/final distinction actually changed a
real response decision, the collision question does not survive contact
with the target's real practice.
