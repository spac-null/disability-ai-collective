# Trial 006 — Architecture (beat structure, before prose)

Carrier: event (the Van, Turkey earthquake and PAGER's alert/revision).
Tension: a system built to publish a number it knows might be wrong,
because waiting to be right costs its own lives.

## Beats

1. **Cold open, Van, Turkey, October 23, 2011.** 27 minutes after the
   ground stops moving, a red alert goes out. (F08)
2. **What just went out, and from where.** PAGER: built after the 2004
   Sumatra tsunami (F11), public since 2010 (F12), results within about
   30 minutes (F01).
3. **The choice built into the system.** Not an oversight — a stated
   position: waiting for accuracy costs more than being wrong briefly
   does (F02).
4. **How it tries to be honest about that choice.** The color-coded
   alert isn't a single number; it carries its own odds of being wrong
   (F03), and the system tells its own users to keep checking (F04).
5. **Back to Van: the number changes.** A day later, revised inputs bring
   the alert down, closer to what actually happened (F08). In-country
   estimates, with better local data, were already closer (F10).
6. **The one place the system does slow down.** A 20-minute pause, only
   for the rarest, worst alerts, reasoned explicitly against the risk of
   sending out something wrong (F05, F06, F07).
7. **What that pause doesn't cover.** The same team names the real risk
   plainly, as a lesson from their own experience: a high alert that gets
   walked back can already have moved people to act, with nothing yet to
   tell them it changed (F09). Close here — unresolved, not
   moralized. Per `ledger.md`'s causal-architecture note: F09 is stated as
   a general lesson, not narrated as something that is known to have
   happened in Van specifically; the prose must not blur that line.
