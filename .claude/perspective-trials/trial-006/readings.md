# Trial 006 — Independent Method Readings

Each method below was produced without reference to the other two. Cross-
examination happens in `collision.md`, not here.

---

## READING 1 — Seismology / seismic instrumentation

**METHOD:** Seismology / seismic-network instrumentation science.

**UNIT OF ATTENTION:** The physical event and the progressive convergence
of estimates toward it as more waveform data and review time become
available.

**WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE:** More stations reporting, more complete
waveform analysis, human review superseding automated algorithms.

**LIKELY BLIND SPOT:** Seismology's own literature treats magnitude
revision as a routine, well-understood feature of the science — a fact
about wave-propagation physics (some waves take over an hour to reach
distant stations; some networks deliver data late) — and may not register
how that routineness looks from outside the field, to someone who received
only the first number and never learned a revision was coming, let alone
why.

**READING OF THIS TARGET:** The USGS's own errata documentation states the
practice plainly: "Earthquake reports posted to UUSS and USGS web pages
should be viewed cautiously if they are labeled as automatic reports,
i.e. not labeled as having been reviewed by a seismologist." The errata
page exists specifically to document "serious errors that have resulted
in the distribution of flawed information to response organizations and
the public" — meaning the field's own record-keeping shows this warning
is necessary because, in practice, it is not consistently heeded.
Seismology's internal account of *why* magnitude changes (wave-arrival
physics, network latency) is well documented and well understood inside
the field. What is not established here, and what seismology's own
literature does not obviously address, is what "response organizations
and the public" actually *do* with the number in the window before
review — that is a question about downstream institutional and human
behavior, not about wave physics.

**QUESTION:** Does the USGS's public-facing magnitude report, at the
moment someone actually uses it, carry a label clear enough that a
response organization or member of the public would know they are looking
at an unreviewed, automatic estimate rather than a reviewed one?

**PREDICTED EVIDENCE:** USGS's own public event-page design and language;
any documented case where the absence or obscurity of that label
contributed to a documented "serious error" per the errata record.

**WHAT WOULD DISCONFIRM IT:** If USGS event pages clearly, prominently, and
consistently label unreviewed magnitudes as such at the point of public
consumption (not just in a separate errata/FAQ page), and documented
errata cases trace to something other than label visibility (e.g., outright
station malfunction, not a labeling failure).

## READING 2 — Parametric risk / actuarial and insurance science

**METHOD:** Parametric risk / actuarial and insurance science.

**UNIT OF ATTENTION:** The reported number as a contractual trigger —
what data source, and what point in that data source's revision history,
a contract names as authoritative for a payout or protocol action.

**WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE:** Contract/bond language specifying data
source and timing; documented cases where a payout depended on which
version of a number (preliminary vs. final) was used, or came close to
depending on it.

**LIKELY BLIND SPOT:** This field's entire apparatus exists to resolve
exactly the ambiguity the seed question raises — contracts specify a
named data vintage and a dispute mechanism precisely because "which
number, from when" has real financial stakes. That sophistication may
mean this field has already solved, for its own narrow purpose, a problem
that remains unsolved for the broader public/response-organization
audience seismology's own reading flagged.

**READING OF THIS TARGET:** Real-world material confirms parametric
earthquake contracts (catastrophe bonds, parametric insurance) are built
specifically around USGS-reported values, and that this field takes the
preliminary/final distinction seriously enough to contract around it:
settlement is typically set 14–30 days *after* USGS publication of final
event parameters — i.e., this field deliberately waits for the reviewed
number rather than acting on the fast one — and contracts specify "the
timing of measurement" and "the dispute mechanism" as named terms. A
real, documented case (Mexico's parametric catastrophe bond, described in
trade coverage as a "near-miss" on a M7.3 earthquake) shows the stakes are
not hypothetical: a bond can sit extremely close to its trigger threshold,
where which exact magnitude value governs determines whether it pays out
at all.

**QUESTION:** Given that this field already distinguishes and waits for
the reviewed value by contractual design, does the "near-miss" case (or
others like it) show any documented instance where the *preliminary*
value nonetheless shaped real behavior — market pricing, public
expectation, an insurer's or reinsurer's provisioning — before the final,
contractually operative number arrived?

**PREDICTED EVIDENCE:** Trade/financial reporting from the days between a
major earthquake and final USGS parameter publication, showing whether
bond prices, reinsurance markets, or public expectation moved on the
preliminary number ahead of the contractually authoritative final one.

**WHAT WOULD DISCONFIRM IT:** If markets and counterparties visibly wait
for the final number and do not price or act on the preliminary one, the
mechanism does not extend into this field even though the underlying
provisional/final gap is real here too.

## READING 3 — Behavioral decision science (anchoring)

**METHOD:** Behavioral decision science (judgment under uncertainty;
anchoring).

**UNIT OF ATTENTION:** A decision-maker's or population's belief/action
before and after exposure to a revised number.

**WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE:** Evidence that a revision failed to fully move
belief or action that had already formed around the initial value — not
merely that a revision occurred.

**LIKELY BLIND SPOT:** Anchoring research is generally conducted on
arbitrary or clearly-labeled-uncertain numbers in controlled settings; it
may not directly address a case where the "anchor" is a real,
professionally produced, institutionally credentialed estimate (a USGS
number) that carries its own apparent authority — which could make the
anchoring effect either stronger (institutional credibility deepens the
anchor) or differently shaped (people may treat an institutional number
as more revisable than an arbitrary one) than the classic experimental
literature captures.

**READING OF THIS TARGET:** Anchoring is one of the most robust,
well-replicated findings in judgment-and-decision-making research
(Tversky and Kahneman), and it is specifically characterized by
*insufficient* adjustment toward new information, not merely slow
adjustment — a documented tendency to keep integrating new information
"naively" around the original anchor rather than replacing it outright.
No study specific to earthquake-magnitude revision or disaster casualty
figures was located in this pass — this is a real, named gap, not a
confirmed transfer.

**QUESTION:** Is there any documented case — earthquake-specific or from a
comparable rapid-estimate-then-revision domain (e.g., early casualty
counts, early damage estimates) — where a revised, more accurate figure
measurably failed to displace public or institutional belief that had
already formed around a faster, earlier figure?

**PREDICTED EVIDENCE:** A study or documented case (in seismology,
disaster response, or a closely analogous domain) directly measuring
belief or decision persistence after a number was revised.

**WHAT WOULD DISCONFIRM IT:** If no such case or study can be found even
adjacent to this domain, and/or if available evidence shows revisions in
this specific domain are fully absorbed by whoever relies on them, this
reading stays a plausible transfer from general anchoring research, not a
subject-specific finding.
