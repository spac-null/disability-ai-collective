# Trial 006 — Targeted Research

## SURVIVING COLLISION QUESTION (from `collision.md`)

Does any earthquake-response process that acts in the first hours or days
(disaster response, aid allocation, early impact estimation) have a
procedural deferral mechanism analogous to parametric insurance's
contractual one, or does it run structurally closer to "the public" —
protected only by a label, which behavioral science holds is an
insufficient safeguard against anchoring?

## TARGETED RESEARCH

**EVIDENCE SOUGHT.** Whether earthquake-response protocols name a rule for
acting on preliminary vs. reviewed magnitude, and whether any documented
real case shows a response decision made on a preliminary estimate that
was later substantially revised.

**EVIDENCE FOUND.** Direct, decisive, and more specific than either
predicted shape (full procedural deferral like insurance, or no protection
like "the public"). USGS's PAGER (Prompt Assessment of Global Earthquakes
for Response) system — the actual real-world instrument disaster-response
and aid agencies use, not a hypothetical — is documented as follows:

1. **PAGER's stated design philosophy explicitly rejects waiting for
   accuracy.** Its own scientific background states: "demanding or
   awaiting observations or loss estimates with a high level of accuracy
   may delay response and increase the loss" — an explicit institutional
   position that, for humanitarian response specifically (unlike
   insurance payouts), *delay itself is a harm*, so the system is built to
   act on fast, openly uncertain estimates rather than wait for reviewed
   ones. It states it "preserves the necessary uncertainty measures" in
   its color-coded alerts and advises users to "always seek the most
   current PAGER release."
2. **The predicted failure mode is real and documented, by name.** The
   2011 M7.2 Van, Turkey earthquake: PAGER's initial "red" alert (issued
   27 minutes after the event) for both fatalities and economic losses
   was revised a day later. Published system literature ("Impact-based
   earthquake alerts with the U.S. Geological Survey's PAGER system:
   what's next?") states plainly: "organizations may mobilize
   emergency response based on initial high alerts that are subsequently
   downgraded" — exactly the seed's predicted mechanism (a fast output
   treated as settled before a slower, more accurate one arrives),
   confirmed as a real, named, consequential event, not a hypothetical.
3. **The field responded to its own documented case with a real,
   calibrated (not absolute) procedural change.** Following experience
   with cases like Van, USGS instituted "a review step for critical
   alerts... to ensure stability of magnitude, epicenter, and depth,"
   explicitly reasoning that "a minor delay for very critical events
   outweighs the increased potential for sending out a premature,
   erroneous alert" — a real, if narrow, procedural deferral, adopted
   specifically because the predicted mechanism actually occurred.

**SOURCE STATUS.** PAGER's own scientific-background page (USGS, primary)
read directly; the Van, Turkey case and the stability-review response
confirmed via a peer-reviewed/technical publication on PAGER's own system
design, read via search-summarized content (not the full paper text) —
sufficient to establish the case and the institutional response; not
independently verified against the original journal article's full text
in this pass.

**SUPPORTS / WEAKENS / KILLS:**

- **SUPPORTS** the seed's underlying mechanism: a fast, honestly-uncertain
  output *can* and *did*, in a real, named, documented case, get treated
  as settled before revision — with real organizational consequences
  (mobilization based on an alert later downgraded).
- **KILLS the collision question's specific predicted shape** ("emergency
  response is unprotected, like the public, relying only on a label").
  PAGER is not naive: it explicitly communicates uncertainty, updates
  continuously, and — after the Van case specifically — added a bounded
  stability-review delay for critical alerts. This is neither full
  contractual deferral (insurance) nor bare label-reliance (the public) —
  it is a third, deliberate, evidence-driven institutional compromise none
  of the three independent method readings predicted in that exact shape.
