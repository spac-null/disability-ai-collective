# Gevers Lineage Note — 2009/2010 → 2026 (preliminary, not PR005)

*Separate from Trial 005. Not a Perspective Library entry. No library file
touched. No final judgement made — this is a comparison to be read, not a
conclusion to act on.*

**Basis:** drawn from material this project has already verified directly
— *Difference on Display* (Ine Gevers ed., 2009/2010; primary PDF verified
by the owner directly, per `.claude/perspective-research/
measurement-classification-legibility.md`) and *AUTONOMOUS — Een
neurospicy take-over van de computergestuurde samenleving* (Gevers, Brutus
Rotterdam, 2026; exhibition text read directly in the same research pass).
Nothing below is new research; it is a comparative reading of what this
project already confirmed, done now because the owner asked the specific
comparative question.

## WHAT CHANGED IN THE INTELLECTUAL CLAIM

**2009/2010 — an interrogative, plural mode.** The book's own framing
questions, per the confirmed review material, are *"what is normal?"* and
*"who decides this?"* — an analytic stance, examining normality as
something imposed and contestable rather than asserting what should
replace it. Structurally, the exhibition's director (Fulya Erdemci)
characterized it in two words: *"plurality"* and *"polyphony"* — "literally
hundreds of voices, with a powerful accumulative effect," read as
**dialogic rather than didactic**. Disability sits *alongside* other
marginalized categories (genetics, biopower, Haraway's cyborg) inside a
broader diversity-of-difference discourse, opened by Shakespeare and
Watson's framing of disability as "the quintessential postmodern concept"
because it defies classification. The claim being made is that normality
is an operation worth exposing — not a specific alternative worth
advocating.

**2026 — a vision-forward, resistance mode.** A contemporary Dutch review
(de Volkskrant, verified in this project's own source register) explicitly
files *AUTONOMOUS* as the *second* kind of group show — one where "the
curator puts a very clear personal vision on the theme" — in direct
contrast to how the 2009/2010 project described itself. The confirmed
exhibition text frames algorithms as *normaliseringsmachines*
("normalization machines") that narrow the bandwidth of what counts as
normal, with AI named an "over-the-top" version of the same mechanism.
Neurodivergence is placed inside contemporary identity politics
specifically — alongside "sexual preferences or gender (non)identities,"
with Gen Z and Alpha "claiming DSM-5 labels" — and the object of resistance
is named directly: **"neurotypical, neocolonial and patriarchal
capitalism."** The argument runs through named contemporary political
figures, tech-oligarchy, and "cognitive capitalism."

## THE CANDIDATE MOVEMENT, CHECKED AGAINST THE ACTUAL TEXTS

The owner's proposed shape —

> NORMALITY AS SOMETHING TO EXPOSE → DIFFERENT WAYS OF THINKING AS POSSIBLE
> CONTRIBUTION / RESISTANCE

— holds up against what this project has actually verified from both
texts, on two independent axes at once: **curatorial voice** (plural/
dialogic → one curator's clear thesis) and **argumentative stance**
(interrogating what normal is and who decided it → asserting that
divergent cognition itself resists and contributes against a named
political-economic order). These are not the same axis, and both moved in
the same direction, seventeen years apart, from the same curator.

## THE FALSE MOVE TO WATCH, NAMED BY THE OWNER

**Romanticising neurodivergence as inherently liberatory or resistant.**
*AUTONOMOUS*'s own framing — "neurospicy take-over," divergent cognition
positioned as resistance to "neurotypical, neocolonial and patriarchal
capitalism" — risks exactly this: treating a cognitive difference as
*intrinsically* oppositional or virtuous, rather than as contextual and
variable (the doctrine's own Axis 8 concept: "capacity is contextual, not
fixed"), and rather than as one account among the corpus's many, not a
universal claim about neurodivergent experience. The 2009/2010 project's
more analytically cautious "what is normal, who decides" framing did not
carry this risk in the same way — it interrogated the operation without
installing a replacement virtue in its place. Whether *AUTONOMOUS* itself
falls into this trap, or holds the tension deliberately, is not settled by
what has been read so far and is explicitly not judged here.
