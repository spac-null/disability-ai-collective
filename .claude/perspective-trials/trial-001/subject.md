# Trial 001 — Subject

## SUBJECT

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault (Longyearbyen, Svalbard, Norway) — its physical design,
governance and deposit/withdrawal rules, the 2016–17 meltwater incident in its entrance
tunnel and the resulting 2019 engineering upgrade, and the 2015–2019 ICARDA withdrawal-and-
return episode (the only withdrawal in the facility's history, prompted by the Syrian civil
war).

## WHY THIS SUBJECT WAS SELECTED (recorded before either lens ran)

Picked as an ordinary, well-documented piece of real infrastructure with a genuine
institutional-process angle: a facility built around one design assumption (permafrost as a
permanent, maintenance-free freezer) that turned out to be wrong within a decade, plus an
unusual ownership/access rule (the vault operator never opens a depositor's box; only the
depositor may withdraw), plus one real emergency use of the system that tested rules written
for a scenario nobody expected to need. That combination — a physical assumption, a
governance rule, and a real event that stress-tested both — makes it a reasonable candidate
for *some* institutional-process story regardless of whether it turns out to carry a Crip
Minds reading. No disability vocabulary informed the choice; it was picked as a "how does
this actually work, and what happened when its own rules were tested" subject, the kind of
institutional-mechanism piece any general-interest desk might commission.

It has no connection to Tollymore, WildSumaco, the Roman Space Telescope, Beetaloo, Minnie

## PLAIN WORLD SUMMARY

The Seed Vault is a storage facility built 120 metres into a sandstone mountain
(Platåberget) outside Longyearbyen, opened in 2008 at a construction cost of roughly $9
million, paid for by Norway. It exists as a backup for the world's national and
international crop genebanks: if a genebank elsewhere is destroyed, damaged, defunded, or
loses power, the seed samples it deposited at Svalbard remain available. Seeds are kept at
-18°C; the surrounding permafrost was designed to keep them frozen for a period even if the
mechanical cooling failed entirely. Capacity is roughly 4.5 million samples (about 500 seeds
per sample). Norway owns and administers the vault through its Ministry of Agriculture and
Food; NordGen (the Nordic Genetic Resources Center) operates it day to day; the Crop Trust
funds ongoing operations and helps genebanks in poorer countries pay for shipping seeds to
Svalbard.

## WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS

**Deposit and withdrawal rule.** A depositing genebank retains legal ownership of its seeds.
The vault operates on a "black box" principle: boxes are sealed by the depositor before
shipment, and nobody at the vault — not Norway, not NordGen, not the Crop Trust — opens a
box or has access to its contents. Only the original depositing institution can request a
withdrawal of its own boxes. Genetically modified seeds are barred by Norwegian law.

**The 2016–17 incident.** An unusually warm Arctic summer combined with permafrost thaw
around the entrance tunnel led to meltwater entering the tunnel in the winter of 2016–17
(most reporting places the event as first noticed in October 2016, with continued concern
into 2017). Water intruded a short distance into the tunnel entrance, then refroze in the
tunnel's own subzero air before reaching the storage chambers roughly 100+ metres further
in; the seed samples themselves were never reported at risk. Norway responded with a set of
physical fixes: removing heat-generating equipment from the tunnel, digging drainage
ditches on the mountainside above the entrance to intercept meltwater before it could reach
the tunnel, building waterproof walls inside the tunnel, and commissioning an engineering
study of the access tunnel's long-term design. Norway allocated roughly $13 million toward
reinforcing the facility, most of it going to a rebuilt, waterproofed access tunnel with its
own cooling and drainage systems, completed around 2019.

**The ICARDA withdrawal.** ICARDA (the International Center for Agricultural Research in
the Dry Areas) had deposited the great majority of its genebank's holdings — collected over
decades of research on wheat, barley, lentil, chickpea, faba bean, forage and wild crop
relatives adapted to dry regions — at Svalbard between 2008 and 2014, largely from its
headquarters genebank at Tel Hadya near Aleppo, Syria. As the Syrian civil war made
continued operation there untenable, ICARDA relocated its operations to Lebanon and
Morocco. In September 2015 ICARDA formally requested and received the first (and to date
the only) withdrawal in the vault's history: 128 boxes (roughly 38,000 samples) were sent
out — the larger share, comprising wheat, barley, lentil and chickpea accessions, to
Morocco, and a smaller share, comprising forages, faba bean, grasspea and wild cereal/pulse
relatives, to Lebanon. ICARDA used the withdrawn seed to re-establish active breeding
collections outside Syria. Rather than simply drawing down the original deposit, ICARDA
then multiplied (grew out and regenerated) new seed stock from the withdrawn material and
re-deposited it at Svalbard in tranches between 2017 and 2019, ending with a final
redeposit completed by August 2019. New deposits from ICARDA's post-Aleppo operations have
continued since.

## IMPORTANT PEOPLE / OBJECTS / SYSTEMS

- **Kingdom of Norway / Norwegian Ministry of Agriculture and Food** — legal owner and
  builder of the facility.
- **NordGen (Nordic Genetic Resources Center)** — day-to-day operator.
- **Crop Trust (Global Crop Diversity Trust)** — funds operations and depositor shipping
  costs, part of the tripartite management structure.
- **ICARDA (International Center for Agricultural Research in the Dry Areas)** — the only
  institution ever to have withdrawn seed; a CGIAR research center, formerly headquartered
  at Tel Hadya near Aleppo, Syria, now operating from Lebanon and Morocco.
- **Platåberget ("Plateau Mountain")** — the sandstone mountain the vault is built into,
  near Longyearbyen.
- **The entrance/access tunnel** — the ~150m approach passage between the portal building
  and the storage chambers; the site of the 2016–17 water intrusion and the subject of the
  2017–2019 engineering fix.
- **The individual depositing genebanks generally** — hundreds of national and
  international genebanks worldwide hold accounts at the vault; each retains sole authority
  over its own boxes.

## KNOWN UNCERTAINTIES

- Exact date the 2016–17 water intrusion was first detected varies slightly by outlet
  (some say "October 2016," some frame it as a "2017" story); this trial has not resolved
  the discrepancy against a primary Norwegian-government source.
- The precise engineering description of the completed 2019 tunnel differs in the level of
  detail given across secondary sources (all agree on waterproofing, drainage, and removal
  of heat sources; not all give identical technical specifications).
- Total current sample count and deposit activity beyond 2019 (e.g., a large 2024 deposit
  and continuing 2025 deposits referenced in passing during search) were not verified in
  detail for this trial and are not treated as established fact below.
- Whether any depositor besides ICARDA has ever *requested* a withdrawal that was not
  fulfilled, or whether ICARDA's is definitively the only request ever made, rests on
  secondary reporting rather than a primary vault-operator statement reviewed directly in
  this trial.
