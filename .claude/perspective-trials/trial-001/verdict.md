SUBJECT: The Svalbard Global Seed Vault — physical design, governance/custody rules, the
2016–17 meltwater incident and 2019 upgrade, and the 2015–2019 ICARDA withdrawal-and-return
episode.

BASELINE SAW: Nothing. The live Lens Probe, run as-is, answered "assumption: none" from the
anchor material — correctly, on this reading, since the governance and engineering facts in
front of it commit to no claim about a person's body, perception, timing, dependence, or
classification.

PERSPECTIVE SAW: Three candidates. Two (who authored the vault's operating terms; the vault
cannot verify what it stores) were real institutional mechanisms, disqualified before
research because neither named an assumption about a body — only about an institution. One
(a design assumption behind the entrance tunnel) was genuinely bodily-shaped if it had found
support, and was the only candidate sent to real research.

RESEARCH RESULT: The tunnel candidate found a real, sourced, subject-specific design
decision — the tunnel was built sloping downward for construction convenience, against local
practice of sloping upward for drainage, which is the documented cause of the 2016–17 flood.
Real and specific. Not about a body.

WHAT IT ADDED: The Perspective Library did generate one hypothesis (the tunnel/navigation
candidate) that the baseline Lens Probe never considered, because the baseline stopped at
"none" and never proposed a search at all. Perspective's structured axis sweep produced a
concrete, falsifiable, researchable question the baseline's single bounded pass did not —
and that question led to a real, previously-undocumented-in-this-trial fact (the downward
slope decision) that turned out to explain the flood mechanically. That is a genuine research
gain. It did not, in this trial, produce a publishable Crip Minds reading.

WHAT IT GOT WRONG: Two of the three Perspective candidates (authorship of the operating
terms; the black-box verification gap) were generated by pattern-matching an axis's *shape*
onto an institution rather than a body, and had to be caught by a category check rather than
by the axis definitions stopping them automatically. The doctrine's own axis text does say
these are about "a body has to live inside," but nothing in the generation step forced that
check before the hypothesis was written down — it took a second, deliberate pass to catch
it. A less careful run could plausibly have sent an institution-only mechanism into research,
or worse, into a ledger, on the strength of the axis's vocabulary alone.

ONE POSSIBLE CHANGE FOR NEXT TRIAL: Add an explicit, mandatory checkpoint immediately after
Perspective hypothesis generation and before hypothesis selection — "does this hypothesis
name an assumption about a body, perception, timing, dependence, or classification of a
PERSON, or does it name an assumption about an institution, a process, or a system in the
abstract?" — as a required field on every generated hypothesis, not a judgment applied only
if the trial happens to think of it.
