# Trial 001 — Targeted real research

Perspective Library is not evidence. Only research against real, verifiable sources may
support or kill a hypothesis. Normal Crip Minds source discipline: normal retrieval first;
every recovered source checked for what it actually says; provenance recorded; no paywall
bypass attempted or needed.

## HYPOTHESIS TESTED: Candidate 3 (the tunnel design decision)

**EVIDENCE SOUGHT.** A documented design decision about the entrance tunnel that names an
assumption about who or what moves through it — a body-shaped particular in the sense Axis 7
and Axis 1 predict (a route, an entrance decision, a stated design rationale).

**EVIDENCE FOUND.** A real, specific, subject-specific design decision — but not the one
predicted. Multiple independent sources (Alfa Laval's engineering account, and general
reporting corroborated during world-first research) describe the entrance tunnel as built
sloping *downward* into the mountain from the portal building. This was, per Norwegian
government officials cited in coverage of the decision, done because a downward slope was
*practical for construction access* — easier to build that way. The same sources note this
was an unusual choice: nearby Svalbard coal-mining tunnels are conventionally built sloping
*upward*, specifically so that water drains away from the interior rather than toward it.
The downward slope is the specific, named mechanical reason meltwater was able to run into
the tunnel during the 2016–17 permafrost-thaw event that prompted the ~$13M repair (see
`subject.md`).

Search performed: "Svalbard Global Seed Vault entrance tunnel stairs slope steps portal
building accessibility visitor." Sources consulted included Alfa Laval's engineering
account of the facility, Crop Trust, Norway Explained's visitor guide, and general/travel
coverage. None of the sources returned — including the visitor-oriented ones, which do
discuss physical access to the site (the vault is closed to the public; guided tours
approach the exterior only; the interior is entered only a few times a year for deposits,
maintenance and inspection) — state or imply any design decision framed around who can
physically move through the tunnel as a *body*. The interior-access sources describe
*frequency* and *authorization* of entry (staff and depositors, a few times a year), not a
design decision about gradient, steps, or physical capacity to traverse it.

**SOURCE STATUS.** Alfa Laval and Crop Trust are the facility's own engineering
partner/administrator; the slope-and-drainage account is corroborated across independent
outlets and is consistent with, and explains, the already-verified 2016–17 flood and 2019
upgrade facts in `subject.md`. Treated as reliable on the engineering point. No primary
architectural drawing or Statsbygg (the Norwegian government's building agency) technical
document was fetched directly in this trial; the finding rests on secondary engineering
reporting that is internally consistent and matches the independently-sourced flood
narrative.

**SUPPORTS / WEAKENS / KILLS.** WEAKENS to the point of killing the hypothesis as framed.
A genuine, subject-specific design-decision particular exists — but it names an assumption
about *soil, gravity and permafrost stability under a construction-convenience motive*, not
an assumption about a person's body. Per Axis 12's own false-move rule, the absence of any
sourced statement connecting the tunnel's design to who can physically use it is not treated
here as proof that no such consideration exists — an unstated negative is not evidence — but
it means this trial has no supportable claim to make about bodies, only about drainage
engineering.

**WHY.** The doctrine's own calibration (Case 3, McGonigle) requires a design decision *in
the institution's own account of itself* that carries a tension about what it does and does
not accommodate. This subject has exactly that shape of fact — but the tension it carries is
between *construction convenience* and *long-term climate stability*, not between an
assumption about bodies and what that assumption costs someone. It is a strong particular
for a different, non-Crip-Minds story: an infrastructure piece about how a decision made for
one kind of practicality (easy to build) collided with a different kind of physical reality
(a warming Arctic) decades later. That is a real story. It is not this publication's story.

## CANDIDATES 1 AND 2 — not sent to new research

Both were disqualified in `perspective.md` on a category check before any search: neither
named an assumption about a body, only about an institution. Re-reading all four
world-first sources in `subject.md` against both hypotheses (rather than issuing new
searches) confirms nothing in the material gathered accidentally supplies a bodily
particular for either — the governance material is uniformly about institutional authority
(who owns, who decides, who may withdraw), never about what any human body is assumed to be
or do. Recorded here rather than silently dropped, per instruction: if the Perspective
hypothesis dies, say so, without rescuing it with another analogy.

## SUMMARY

All three candidate hypotheses die. Two die on a category check (institutional mechanism,
not a bodily one) before touching new evidence. The third is genuinely tested against real,
targeted research and dies on the evidence: a real design-assumption particular exists, and
it is not about a body.
