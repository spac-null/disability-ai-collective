## HYPOTHESIS 1

**HYPOTHESIS.** The disaster's own physical mechanism had two distinct
phases with two different demands on a body: outrunning or surviving a
25-foot, 35-mph wave (raw speed, impact survival), then escaping a
thickening, viscous trap once caught (strength, the ability to keep one's
airway clear, the ability to be extracted before suffocating). The
event — and every account of it read in world-first research — treats the
21 dead and roughly 150 injured as a single undifferentiated population
("laborers, teamsters, firefighters... ages 10 to 78"), with no source read
so far asking whether physical capacity to move fast, extract oneself from
a viscous trap, or be found and freed before suffocating shaped who
survived and who didn't.

**QUESTION.** Does any primary record from the event or its aftermath (the
Ogden investigation's testimony, contemporary news coverage, or a
disability-and-disaster-studies re-examination) name age, physical
capacity, or mobility as a factor in who escaped the wave, who escaped the
subsequent trap, or who did not — as opposed to every general-audience
retelling treating the death toll as an undifferentiated list of names and
occupations?

**PREDICTED DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE.** A primary record (testimony, a
contemporary account, or a scholarly disability-and-disaster analysis)
that specifically discusses differential ability to move/escape/be
extracted as a factor in the outcome — not just a list of who died.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** If nothing beyond a bare victim list exists — no
account anywhere discusses capacity to escape as a factor, and the only
material available treats the deaths as effectively random (whoever
happened to be standing in the wave's path, regardless of ability) — the
hypothesis does not clear the bar; it would be indistinguishable from the
generic "disasters kill whoever is closest" observation already refused in
`baseline.md`.

**INSTRUMENT(S).** No exact durable-instrument match. Closest doctrine
territory: Axis 1 concept (an implicit assumption about what a body can do,
here operating through an accident and its retelling rather than a
designed system) and Axis 7 (navigation — "what movement does this space or
process take for granted, and what happens to that assumption in ordinary
operation").

**MIND(S) SHARPENED.** None cleanly; closest is ZENO (technical/mechanism
attention) by the doctrine's persona-agnostic rule.

## HYPOTHESIS 2

**HYPOTHESIS.** The six-year gap between the disaster (1919) and the Ogden
finding of fault (1925) was not simply an abstract institutional delay —
survivors who were injured, not killed, lived with whatever the tank did
to their bodies for the entire period before any resolution or
compensation existed, while USIA continued operating. "Recovery is part of
the task" (PR002-07, APPROVED_DURABLE) names exactly this shape: the cost
that matters is a duration borne by a person, not the single dramatic
event.

**QUESTION.** Is there a specific, named account of an injured (not
killed) survivor's condition and circumstances during the six-year
investigation — what they lived with, whether they worked, how they were
supported — as opposed to every account read so far stopping at the
23-word category "about 150 injured" with no individual account of any of
them?

**PREDICTED DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE.** A named survivor's account (oral
history, contemporary interview, a descendant's record, court testimony)
describing a lasting injury and what the years before resolution were like
for that person specifically.

**DISCONFIRMING SHAPE.** If "about 150 injured" is the most granular
figure available anywhere, with no individual account of any injured
survivor's experience of the intervening years, the hypothesis stays at
the level of a plausible inference from PR002-07's general shape, not a
subject-specific finding — it does not clear the bar.

**INSTRUMENT(S).** PR002-07, "Recovery is part of the task" (Axes 9, 6, 5,
APPROVED_DURABLE).

**MIND(S) SHARPENED.** MAYA.

## HYPOTHESIS 3 — generated, then dropped by the PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK

**HYPOTHESIS.** Arthur Jell (USIA's treasurer) dismissed workers' reports
of the tank groaning during filling — a warning signal, delivered through
an informal channel (worker to management), filtered out before it could
change anything.

**PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK:** Does this identify an assumption about a
person's body, perception, communication, timing, dependence, navigation,
cognition, or classification — or only an interesting mechanism about
organizational hierarchy and whose reports get acted on? **Only the
latter.** This is a corporate-hierarchy/whistleblower-dismissal pattern —
who gets listened to inside a company — not an assumption about anyone's
body, perception, or capacity. It happens to use Axis 3's vocabulary
("what sits between this thing and the version that actually arrives")
structurally, the same way Trial 001's and Trial 002's dropped candidates
used their axes' vocabulary without naming a person.

**DROPPED BEFORE TARGETED RESEARCH.**

## SELECTION

H3 dropped by v1.1. H1 and H2 both pass v1.1 (both are unambiguously about
what happened to specific people's bodies, not about the company or the
investigation as institutions in the abstract). **H1 is selected** as the
strongest, single candidate for targeted research: it asks about the
disaster's own physical mechanism and whether capacity to escape it was
ever documented as differential — the more decisively falsifiable and less
speculative of the two (H2 depends on locating an individual account that
may not exist in any accessible source; H1 can be tested against the same
material already partially in hand, since several sources already name
individual victims and ages). H2 is recorded as a live, unpursued
candidate rather than silently dropped, per the same practice used in
Trials 002 and 003.
