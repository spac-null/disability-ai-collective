TRIAL: 002

SUBJECT: The FAA's NOTAM system — the January 2023 outage, and the
longer-running notice-format/readability problem.

BASELINE SAW: Not "none." Given a realistic already-collected source pack
(including titles that already gestured at the readability problem), the
live Lens Probe named a real assumption — that the channel treats delivery
as safety, not comprehension — and pointed at the correct kind of carrier
(a formal institutional record), without yet knowing one existed.

PERSPECTIVE SAW: Three hypotheses. One (Delta's non-use of its own backup
system "out of deference to the FAA") was dropped before research by the
v1.1 PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK — institutional deference, not a body. Two
(H1: uniform-reader/no-priority-registration assumption; H2: the coded
format as an unacknowledged translation layer) passed the check. H1 was
selected as the stronger, more decisively falsifiable candidate.

HYPOTHESES DROPPED BY PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK: 1 — the Delta-deference
candidate, an institution-shaped hypothesis wearing an axis's vocabulary.

TARGETED RESEARCH RESULT: H1 was confirmed decisively — NTSB/AIR-18/01 (Air
Canada 759, SFO, 2017, a near-miss over four loaded jets) found exactly the
predicted carrier: a formal safety investigation naming NOTAM/flight-release
*presentation*, not system uptime, as a causal factor, backed by a
seven-incident ASRS pattern and a formal, numbered safety recommendation
(A-18-23) to the FAA. This is the strongest, most decisive evidence found in
either trial to date. H2 was also checked and found real but thin — a
generic modernization narrative, not incident-specific.

SURVIVING READING: NEITHER — but not for the reason Trial 001's was NEITHER.
The evidence for H1 did not fail; it succeeded and then failed a different
test. The NTSB's own account of the mechanism is explicitly general-human
(serial position effect, attention under fatigue/workload), not
differential — it does not distinguish any body or mind from any other. A
real attempt was made to find an actual disability differential (FAA
medical certification's treatment of ADHD as presumptively disqualifying
for pilots) rather than assume none existed; it is real, but no source ties
it to this incident or this pattern, and inventing that connection would
have been the WildSumaco failure — an accurate adjacent fact standing in for
a mechanism no source supports.

WORTH: NOT_RUN — nothing survived Step 5 to feed it. Not bypassed, correctly
skipped, same discipline as Trial 001.

FINAL STAGE REACHED: Step 5 (surviving-reading selection).

WHAT IT ADDED: In this trial, Perspective did not out-reach the baseline the
way it did in Trial 001 — the baseline, given a realistic source pack,
independently proposed essentially the same underlying carrier and pointed
at the right category of record. What Perspective's independent sweep
contributed on top of that was the disciplined PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK
catching an institution-shaped candidate (H3) before it could cost any
research budget, and a second live candidate (H2) recorded rather than
silently dropped. The more interesting result this trial produced was not
"Perspective beat baseline" but that both lenses can converge on strong,
real evidence that still isn't a Crip Minds story — which is a finding
about the doctrine's Section 7 bar, not about either lens's search quality.

WHAT IT GOT WRONG: Nothing in the process failed. The v1.1 check worked
exactly as designed on H3. What the trial surfaces is a gap the check does
not cover: a hypothesis can be unambiguously about a person's cognition,
perception, and timing (clearing v1.1 cleanly) and still be a universal
design flaw rather than a disability-differential one. Catching that
currently depends on applying Section 7 honestly and by hand at the
surviving-reading step, the same way the person-assumption distinction had
to be applied by hand in Trial 001 before v1.1 existed.

ONE POSSIBLE CHANGE FOR NEXT TRIAL: A second checkpoint, after the
PERSON-ASSUMPTION CHECK and after targeted research but before surviving-
reading selection — "does the evidence found show this assumption operating
*differently* on different bodies/minds, or operating uniformly regardless
of ability status?" — as a named, explicit question rather than something
only caught by a careful Section-7 read. Not proposed as a rule to
implement now; proposed because this trial is the first time the gap
actually appeared.
