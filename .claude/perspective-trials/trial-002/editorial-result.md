# Trial 002 — Editorial Result

## STEP 5 — SURVIVING READING

**BASELINE** and **PERSPECTIVE H1** converged on materially the same
underlying carrier: a formal record showing that NOTAM/flight-operations
information presentation, not just system uptime, has caused real
operational danger. Both are true. The question this step actually has to
answer is not "did research find something" — it did, decisively — but
"is what it found a Crip Minds mechanism."

Applying doctrine Section 7 (WHAT MAKES A READING WORTH PUBLISHING) and the
FALSE MOVE clauses on Axis 1 (bodies and normal functioning), Axis 8
(cognition and capacity), and Axis 12 (measurement):

- The NTSB's own account of the mechanism (serial position effect,
  attention-driven salience under fatigue and workload) is explicitly
  general-human, not differential. It does not distinguish any subgroup of
  pilots from any other; the finding and the seven-incident ASRS pattern
  behind it describe an already-medically-certified, structurally uniform
  population failing at a uniform rate against a uniform design flaw.
- A genuine attempt was made to find a documented differential (FAA medical
  certification's treatment of ADHD as presumptively disqualifying for
  pilots) rather than assume none existed. It is real, but no source
  connects it to this incident, this report, or this ASRS pattern. Building
  that connection myself would be the WildSumaco failure — an accurate
  record about a neighbouring subject, placed beside a claim about this one,
  inviting a conclusion no source supports.
- The honest one-sentence summary of what was found is close to "this
  information-presentation flaw put everyone at equal risk, regardless of
  ability" — which is not "disabled people are affected too" (Section 7's
  named failure) so much as its mirror image: no differential surfaced at
  all. Either way, the doctrine's bar is not cleared.

**SURVIVING READING: NEITHER.** Not because the research was weak — it was
unusually strong and decisively evidenced, stronger than Trial 001's — but
because what survived research is a real, general, well-documented
human-factors and information-design failure, not a disability-specific
mechanism. Per the master protocol's own vocabulary: this is
**GREAT GENERAL STORY, WRONG PUBLICATION**, not NOTHING HERE — there is a
genuinely strong general-audience aviation-safety story sitting in the NTSB
record (dense document design nearly caused one of the closest calls in
modern aviation history, and the fix took the FAA most of a decade after a
formal recommendation), it is simply not a Crip Minds story.

## What this trial actually adds, stated plainly

This is a different failure shape than Trial 001's, and arguably a more
useful one to have on record. Trial 001 tested whether the Perspective
Library could generate a hypothesis the baseline would never reach, and
whether real research could kill a bad one — it could, on both counts.

hypothesis that is *unambiguously about a person's cognition, perception and
timing* (so it clears v1.1 cleanly), supported by unusually strong, primary,
decisive evidence (so it doesn't fail on evidence quality either) — and
still correctly fails, on the doctrine's own worth-publishing standard,
because the mechanism it reveals doesn't distinguish any body or mind from
any other. The check that would catch this is not the PERSON-ASSUMPTION
CHECK; it is Section 7's own standard, applied honestly at Step 5.
