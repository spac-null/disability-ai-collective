# Held-out subject selection

## Candidates considered

Drawn from the live production feed pool in `automation/feeds.json` (the same feeds the
Selector uses), pulled 2026-09-04 across the Pixel Nova, Maya Flux, Zen Circuit and Siri Sage
feed sets. Roughly 70 headlines were surfaced; three were taken far enough to answer the three
selection questions.

### Candidate 1 — Justin Blinder, *Ground Truth* (SELECTED)

A 3D-printed device that clamps to the front fork of a Citi Bike and converts census
rent-burden data into physical brake resistance, so the bike becomes harder to pedal in
areas where housing costs take a larger share of income.

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Enough evidence? | **Yes.** Artist-authored primary project site (1,973 words, including his own limitations section), independent reporting from Gothamist with named figures and direct quotes, plus authoritative tertiary sources for the ACS and the HUD threshold. |
| Real story or explanatory carrier? | **Yes.** A physical object with a documented mechanism (GPS → block-group score → servo → front brake), a named maker with a documented reason for building it, a specific dataset, and two documented rides with counter-intuitive results. |
| Legitimate Crip Minds warrant? | **Yes, and not forced.** The subject is a machine that turns a classification of a *place* into a force acting on a *body*. The article is not about disabled people and makes no claim about them; the lens is epistemic — what a measure can and cannot hand to a body, and who falls out of the measure by construction. |

Chosen as candidate 1 and passed. Candidates 2 and 3 were not developed further, per the
three-attempt budget.

### Candidate 2 — *What Did Jurassic Forests Sound Like?* (NYT Science) — held in reserve

Concrete (fossil morphology → modelled acoustics), and the lens would have been the
authorisation of claims about sound built from evidence nobody could ever hear. Not developed:
the lens structure is close to a subject already used in architecture development (a record
that keeps only what translates), which would have weakened the held-out property of the test.

### Candidate 3 — *Cops Are Asking Axon to Make Their Cameras Look Different From Flock* (404 Media) — held in reserve

Concrete decision and object, good source depth. Not developed: the disability warrant was
available but thin, and would have risked the forced analogy the Worth Gate exists to refuse.

## Held-out property

Not used in Story Architecture development, and not on the excluded list: not Jia, Roman,
Smolin, Langrug, the eel article or Galton. Nothing about this subject, its dataset, its
mechanism or its lens has been used to tune any part of the current architecture.

One acknowledged adjacency: the subject was surfaced by the Dezeen feed, which also surfaced
Jia. The Dezeen article body itself returns HTTP 403 to us and **is not used as evidence** —
it is recorded only as the feed item that surfaced the subject. All evidence comes from the
artist's own project site, Gothamist, the Census Bureau and HUD.

## Why this subject rather than an easier one

It carries a real risk of failure, which is what makes it a test. The obvious article is a
gadget write-up. The material only becomes a Crip Minds article if the counter-intuitive
results hold up — the brake biting hardest in a neighbourhood with a roughly $97,000 median
household income, and letting go over public housing — and if the measure's blind spots are
documented rather than asserted. Both turned out to be documented, and by the maker himself.
