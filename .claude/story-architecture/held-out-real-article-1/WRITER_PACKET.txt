WHAT THE STORY IS
  A device that turns a census score into brake pressure ends up easing over public housing and going blank where nobody was counted.

OPEN ON
  A 3D-printed box clamped to the front fork of a docked Citi Bike, with a wire running down to the brake arm.
  The reader should first understand only this: That someone built a small machine which decides how hard a public hire bike is to pedal.

THE PATH, IN ORDER
  1. The device: where it sits on the bike, what it grips, and what a rider's legs feel.
     carried by: the box on the fork and the wire to the brake arm
     - A device made by the artist Justin Blinder attaches to the front fork of a docked Citi Bike and overrides the front brake to change how hard the bike is to pedal.
     - The servo pulls the existing front brake arm along the same axis as the rider's own brake cable; the rider's levers stay functional and always override, and the device can be removed by hand in seconds.
     not yet: anything about census data, neighbourhoods or what the resistance means
  2. What it reads: satellite position, a card of block-group scores, a servo angle, and the measure those scores come from.
     carried by: the card of scores and the servo arm on the brake cable
     - The device was built from a GPS module, an ESP32S3 microcontroller, a high-torque servo motor, an SD card reader, an OLED display and a custom circuit board, in a 3D-printed enclosure.
     - The data is the American Community Survey five-year estimates at block-group level, covering 2020 through 2024.
     - Two indicators were blended into one score: the median share of income spent on rent, and the share of renter households spending at least half their income on housing.
     - The measure follows the US Department of Housing and Urban Development's 30 percent standard, under which a household spending more than 30 percent of income on rent and utilities is counted as cost-burdened.
     explain plainly, once, here: rent burden: the share of a household's income that goes on rent and utilities, counted as a burden above thirty percent
     not yet: that the measure eases over public housing
  3. Where the thirty percent came from.
     carried by: the figure itself, and the dates it changed
     - HUD adopted the 30 percent figure in 1981; earlier public-housing programmes had used 25 percent, and the threshold is traced to a 1969 amendment by Senator Edward Brooke.
     not yet: the Windsor Terrace figures
  4. The ride around Prospect Park, and then the income and rent figures for where the brake bit hardest.
     carried by: Prospect Park West, then Windsor Terrace, then two rent figures five years apart
     - On a ride around Prospect Park the bike rolled easily along Prospect Park West and then the brake engaged in Windsor Terrace, nearly stopping it.
     - Median household income in the community district covering Windsor Terrace and Sunset Park was about $97,000 in 2024, per a NYU Furman Center analysis.
     - Median asking rent in that area was $2,250 in 2021, when Citi Bike docks arrived, and $3,800 five years later, per StreetEasy - a rise far exceeding inflation.
     - Areas registering a higher rent burden are often not low-income neighbourhoods but corridors where housing costs have risen rapidly.
     not yet: the public housing result
  5. Near public housing the resistance let go, and what the maker takes that to show.
     carried by: the bike speeding up alongside the complexes
     - Riding near NYCHA public-housing complexes, the device released resistance.
     - Blinder said that easing over public housing shows public housing is working and offsetting the burden, but does not mean residents' lives are more affordable as such.
     not yet: the suppressed block groups
  6. What the measure does not hold, and the places where it holds nothing at all.
     carried by: a block group with no published figure
     - The ACS is a sample rather than a census, and at block-group level the sample in any one area is small with correspondingly wide margins of error.
     - Where a block group's sample is too small or unreliable, the ACS publishes no estimate, and both the device and the map render this as no reading rather than as zero.
     - Blinder wrote that an absence of data is not an absence of pressure, and that showing it as flat ground would be a lie told by the visualization rather than by the data.
     - Rent burden does not measure overcrowding, housing quality, eviction risk, informal tenancy, security of a lease, harassment, feelings of safety, or how long a household has been holding on.
     - The survey covers households, so people with no housing at all are absent from it by construction.
     not yet: the brake cables
  7. What the maker found with his hands while fitting the devices, and what he says the ride does not convey.
     carried by: the loose front-brake cables on one bike in four
     - Blinder wrote that the difficulty a rider feels does not translate to the lived experiences and hardships of the people represented in the data.
     - Across dozens of Citi Bikes handled during installation and removal, roughly one in four had a loose front-brake cable that made the front brake ineffective, and Blinder corrected them.
     - Blinder is 41, lost his job during the pandemic and began earning money as a delivery worker, and had the idea then.
     - Blinder said the experience of moving through the city showed him how much its economic and psychological qualities differed block by block.
