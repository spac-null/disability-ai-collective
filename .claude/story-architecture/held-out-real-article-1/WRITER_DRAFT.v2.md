Clamped to the front fork of a docked Citi Bike is a 3D-printed box, with a wire running down from it to the brake arm. The artist Justin Blinder built it. It overrides the front brake and changes how hard the bike is to pedal.

A servo inside pulls the existing front brake arm along the same axis the rider's own brake cable pulls it. The rider's levers stay functional and always override the box. The whole thing comes off the fork by hand in seconds. What reaches the legs is resistance that arrives without being asked for, and then eases off again.

A GPS module tells the box where it is. A card inside holds one score for every block group, the smallest area the American Community Survey publishes figures for, and the servo turns the score for the ground underneath into an angle, which is to say into pressure on the brake.

The scores come from the survey's five-year estimates, covering 2020 through 2024. Two indicators are blended into one: the median share of income spent on rent, and the share of renter households spending at least half their income on housing. Both are ways of counting rent burden, the share of a household's income that goes on rent and utilities. Past thirty percent, under the standard set by the US Department of Housing and Urban Development, a household counts as cost-burdened.

HUD adopted the thirty percent figure in 1981. Earlier public-housing programmes had used twenty-five percent. The threshold is traced to a 1969 amendment by Senator Edward Brooke.

On a ride around Prospect Park the bike rolled easily along Prospect Park West. Then it crossed into Windsor Terrace, and the brake engaged hard enough to nearly stop it.

Median household income in the community district covering Windsor Terrace and Sunset Park was about $97,000 in 2024, per an analysis by NYU's Furman Center. Median asking rent in the area was $2,250 in 2021, the year the Citi Bike docks arrived, and $3,800 five years later, per StreetEasy — a rise far outrunning inflation. Areas that register a high rent burden are often not low-income neighbourhoods but corridors where housing costs have climbed fast.

Riding near NYCHA public-housing complexes, the device let the resistance go. Blinder said the easing shows that public housing is working and offsetting the burden, though it does not mean residents' lives are more affordable as such.

The survey behind the scores is a sample rather than a census. At block-group level the sample in any one area is small, and the margins of error are correspondingly wide.

Where a block group's sample is too small or too unreliable, the survey publishes no estimate for it. The device and the map both render that as no reading rather than as zero. Blinder wrote that an absence of data is not an absence of pressure, and that drawing it as flat ground would be a lie told by the visualization rather than by the data.

Rent burden does not measure overcrowding, or housing quality, or eviction risk, or informal tenancy, or the security of a lease, or harassment, or how safe a person feels, or how long a household has been holding on. The survey counts households, so people with no housing at all sit outside it by construction.

Both of those conditions arrive at the legs as the same sensation: less pressure on the pedals. In one, the measure has found a burden it reads as lower. In the other, the measure has nothing to report about that ground at all.

Blinder wrote that the difficulty a rider feels does not translate to the lived experiences and hardships of the people represented in the data.

He is 41. He lost his job during the pandemic and began earning money as a delivery worker, and the idea came to him then; moving through the city that way, he said, showed him how much its economic and psychological qualities differed block by block. Across the dozens of Citi Bikes he handled while fitting and removing the devices, roughly one in four had a loose front-brake cable that made the front brake ineffective, and he corrected them as he went. That was found by hand, one bike in four, while installing a machine built to pass on what the survey holds.
