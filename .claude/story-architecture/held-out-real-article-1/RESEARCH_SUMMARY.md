# Research summary

Feed pool → subject surfaced → primary source located and read in full → independent reporting
read in full → research need identified → targeted research on three load-bearing external
facts → evidence freeze.

The reading was done before any argument existed. The lens was not chosen until after the
primary source's own limitations section had been read, and it came out of that section rather
than being imposed on it.

## Research need, and what targeted research was sought

After the primary and independent reads, three things were load-bearing and came from a single
source each. Each was independently checked before the freeze:

1. **Is the block-group unreliability claim real, or the artist's own framing?** Sought
   independent confirmation. Found: Census Bureau training material giving a worked example
   where the estimate is 28 and the margin of error is ±33 — the margin exceeds the estimate.
   The artist's caution is if anything understated.
2. **Where does the 30 percent threshold come from?** Sought its provenance. Found: HUD adopted
   30 percent in 1981; earlier public-housing programmes capped rents at 25 percent; the figure
   is traced to a 1969 amendment by Senator Edward Brooke. It is a policy artifact with a
   legislative history, not a measurement of strain — which matters, because it is the number
   now pulling a brake cable.
3. **Do the counter-intuitive ride results come from the artist or from a reporter?** Sought
   the provenance of the Windsor Terrace and NYCHA observations. Found: both are in the
   independent Gothamist report, with the income figure attributed to a NYU Furman Center
   analysis and the rent figures to StreetEasy.

## Story material actually present

- **Documented object:** a 3D-printed enclosure on the front fork, a wire to the brake arm.
- **Documented mechanism:** GPS position → local block-group score on an SD card →
  microcontroller → servo angle → tension on the existing front brake.
- **Documented chronology:** idea during the pandemic while working as a delivery rider;
  dataset covering 2020–2024; rides in 2026.
- **Documented decision:** the choice to render a suppressed block group as *no reading* rather
  than as zero.
- **Documented consequence, twice, and both against expectation:** the brake nearly stopping
  the bike in Windsor Terrace; the resistance letting go alongside public housing.
- **Documented physical detail found by hand:** roughly one bike in four with a loose
  front-brake cable that made the front brake ineffective.
- **Named person with a stated reason:** Justin Blinder, 41, on what riding the city for money
  showed him.

## Story material sought and NOT found

- **Any rider other than the maker.** No account exists of anyone else's ride. The architecture
  therefore prohibits describing any other rider's experience, and the article does not have a
  second perceiver. This is the single largest constraint on the piece and it was accepted
  rather than filled in.
- **Any response from Citi Bike, Lyft or the Census Bureau.** Not found, and the architecture
  prohibits implying one exists or does not exist.
- **Any disabled rider, tester or consultee.** Not found, not implied, explicitly prohibited.
  The Crip Minds warrant is carried by the epistemics of the instrument, not by a claim about
  any disabled person.
