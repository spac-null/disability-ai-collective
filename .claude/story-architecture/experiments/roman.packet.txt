WHAT THE STORY IS
  A telescope was built to give its pictures away the same day it takes them, and the giving-away was engineered into the machine rather than added afterwards.

  A terabyte of data falling to Earth every day from a million miles away.

THE TURN
  Read as an instrument, this telescope is measured by how much sky it catches. Read as a pipe, it is measured by how many separate parties are meant to act on the same output - and that second measure was designed in, not bolted on.

WHERE THE MEANING CHANGES
  But immediate and public describe the pipe, not the far end of it. A terabyte a day arrives whether or not the person named to receive it has the format, the storage, the bandwidth or the machine to open it. Access designed in at the start is a different thing from access declared at the end, and the declaration is the easy half.
  The idea that does this work: Access built into a system at the design stage behaves differently from access added to a finished one: the first shapes what the thing is for, while the second can only be an intention laid over choices already made - and 'available immediately' describes a pipe, not whether anyone at the far end can carry what comes down it.
