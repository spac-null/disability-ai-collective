# Scientias explanation profile v2

Twenty-five Dutch science-news articles, 15,652 body words, across eight domains: astronomy
(4), physics (1 usable of 2), neuroscience (4), biology (4), climate (3), medicine (4),
archaeology (3), technology (2). One further candidate was a podcast landing page and is
recorded UNAVAILABLE. All paragraph boundaries are reliable.

Scientias is studied here for one question only: **how does it make complex science legible
to a non-specialist?** It is not being studied as narrative nonfiction and mostly is not any.

Site furniture — the standing lines `Leestip:`, `Uitgelezen? Luister ook eens naar de
Scientias Podcast`, `Wil je niets van Scientias missen` — was removed before measurement.
Leaving it in inflates the apparent question density more than fourfold and creates the false
impression that Scientias uses short pivot paragraphs. It does not use them at all.

## 1. Familiar entry

Two entry strategies, and the split is roughly two to one.

**Finding-first (14 of 25).** Sentence one is the result, compressed.

> *"Astronomen hebben 217 opnames van de ruimtetelescoop James Webb op elkaar gestapeld."*
> *"De iconische slagtand van de narwal blijkt opgebouwd uit twee spiralen die in een
> tegengestelde richting draaien."*

**Reader's-world-first (8 of 25).** One or two sentences inside the reader's own experience,
then the finding.

> *"Wij gooien een visje in de pan als het tijd is voor het avondeten, beetje groente erbij,
> aardappeltje misschien. Maar zo'n enorme blauwe vinvis heeft wel even wat meer nodig…"*
> *"Broccoli en blauwe bessen en zelfs koffie en chocola: ze zitten vol met antioxidanten…"*
> *"Voor veel mensen is even naar de supermarkt lopen … de normaalste zaak van de wereld. Voor
> mensen die blind zijn is zo'n tochtje een stuk ingewikkelder."*

The remaining cases are a dated event (`SC-05`, `SC-16`) or a historical hook (`SC-19`, which
opens on wartime rationing before arriving at cancer rates eighty years later).

Nothing here is a scene in the narrative sense. The "familiar entry" is a shared everyday
action, not a place with people in it.

## 2. Reason to care, and where it sits

The reason to care is placed **inside the sentence that introduces the subject**, not after it.

> *"Een Britse satelliet niet groter dan een koffertje gaat ons helpen bij het beantwoorden
> van een van de grootste vragen uit de kosmologie: wat gebeurde er in het zogeheten donkere
> tijdperk, de periode voor de eerste sterren in het heelal?"*

That single sentence carries the object (a suitcase-sized satellite), the scale translation
("no bigger than a suitcase"), the stake ("one of the biggest questions in cosmology"), the
technical term ("the dark age"), and the term's plain gloss ("the period before the first
stars"). It is dense, but it is dense in a specific way: **five different kinds of
information, none of them an interpretation.** This is worth contrasting with the compression
failure mode the owner objects to, which stacks fact + context + *interpretation* + atmosphere
+ *conclusion*. Scientias stacks orientation, not judgement.

## 3. The question

Question density after cleaning is **0.91 per 1,000 words, median 0.00** — near-identical to
the prestige-nonfiction control group (0.87) and about one-fifth of Bregman's rate. Questions
are not a general Scientias device.

Where they appear, they appear as **section hinges in question/answer pairs**:

> *"Zien we in de toekomst vergelijkbare waarnemingen? Dan groeit de statistische zekerheid…
> Blijft het bij deze ene flits? Dan verdwijnt die vermoedelijk in de achtergrond."*

And as an honest suspension in the headline-and-lede pairing (*"is dit dan toch een spoor van
donkere materie?"* → *"Misschien wel en misschien ook niet."*).

## 4. Technical term → plain gloss

Three placements, all used:

**Gloss immediately after the term, in apposition or parentheses:**
> *"een lange, rustige fase … die bekendstaat als het donkere tijdperk (the cosmic dark ages)"*
> *"Dat signaal staat bekend als de 21-centimeterlijn en bestaat uit straling van
> waterstofatomen…"*

**Gloss placed at first use, mid-article, not front-loaded:**
> *"Hersengolven zijn ritmische schommelingen van de elektrische activiteit van grote groepen
> zenuwcellen."* — paragraph 6 of 17, exactly where the term first does work.

**Gloss before the term.** The `look-elsewhere` effect in `SC-05` is the strongest instance in
the sample of a genuinely hard concept delivered to a general reader:

> *"De onderzoekers keken naar in totaal 616 verschillende donkeremateriemodellen. Hoe vaker
> je iets probeert, hoe groter de kans is dat ergens iets opvallends opduikt. Dit is het
> 'look-elsewhere-effect'. Nadat daarvoor was gecorrigeerd, bleef een globale significantie
> van 2,6 sigma over."*

Four short sentences: the count, the intuition, the name, the consequence. The name arrives
third. This is the same negative term-to-explanation distance found in Bregman's *"mental
bandwidth"*, arrived at independently in a different language and a 900-word format.

The English term is often supplied in parentheses alongside the Dutch. That is a
Dutch-language convenience, not a transferable move.

## 5. Mechanism as a causal chain of short clauses

The El Niño explanation is the reference case. Each step is one short clause, and the steps
are in order:

> trade winds blow east to west → warm water flows toward Australia → the winds weaken → warm
> water spreads back east → an El Niño begins → heavy rainfall shifts to the central Pacific →
> the western Pacific dries → the trade winds weaken further → the situation sustains itself →
> so it can last one to two years

This is the anti-compression pattern in its purest observed form: **one link per clause, no
link skipped, no interpretation inserted mid-chain.** The interpretation ("that is not good
news") is a separate paragraph seven paragraphs later.

## 6. Analogy

Used sparingly and always toward the familiar: a satellite the size of a suitcase; the far
side of the moon as a *schild* (shield) and a *schuilplaats* (hiding place); the thylacine's
jaw compared to a wolf's; grassland as *"een versleten tapijt"*. Analogies are drawn from
domestic objects and from animals, not from other sciences.

## 7. Method

Method gets its own paragraph or two, late, and it is concrete rather than procedural:

> *"…bestudeerden de onderzoekers de kern van dertien koralen van de Galapagoseilanden…
> Koralen groeien jaarlijks een tot twee centimeter door laagjes calciumcarbonaat af te
> zetten… De kernen zijn millimeter voor millimeter onderzocht. Daarbij is gekeken naar de
> verhouding tussen strontium en calcium…"*

Thirteen corals, millimetre by millimetre, a strontium-to-calcium ratio. The reader is told
what was physically done, not what design the study had.

## 8. Limitation

**This is the most transferable thing Scientias does, and the most surprising.** Limitations
get their own real estate — short, plain, consecutive paragraphs, with no hedging adverbs.

`SC-09` (breathing and brain activity, 17 paragraphs) devotes paragraphs 10–14 — about 30% of
the article — to five separate limitations:

> the subjects had electrodes implanted for epilepsy, so *"het brein van iemand met moeilijk
> behandelbare epilepsie is niet zonder meer een doorsnee brein"* · electrode placement was
> decided by doctors, so which brain regions were observed *"zijn dus deels toeval"* · the
> direction of causation is open, *"dat zegt nog niet wie wie stuurt"* · the subjects were at
> rest, so exertion and stress are unmeasured · the practical application *"is voorlopig een
> plan"*.

`SC-05` does the same for statistics: 3.4 sigma locally, 2.6 globally after correction, and
particle physics conventionally wants 5 — plus *"op dit moment is er nog geen peer-review van
het onderzoek geweest"*.

The lesson: **uncertainty gets a section, not an adverb.** A 650-word article can be honest
about what it does not know without sounding evasive, because the honesty is structural rather
than lexical. This is directly relevant to Crip Minds, where hedging currently leaks into
sentences as caveats.

## 9. De-interpretation — the move PR #62 has no category for

Scientias regularly tells the reader what a number does **not** mean:

> *"Ook dat cijfer vraagt om voorzichtigheid: het betekent niet dat deze mensen letterlijk 2,2
> jaar langer leven."*
> *"De percentages betekenen dus niet dat een baby die weinig suiker krijgt automatisch 69
> procent minder kans heeft om later leverkanker te krijgen."*

These are negative statements, and they are the opposite of the defect PR #62 was built to
remove. *"The source does not describe how any visitor perceived these spaces"* is the machine
describing its own evidence. *"That percentage does not mean these people live 2.2 years
longer"* is the world being clarified for the reader, and it prevents a specific
misinterpretation the reader would otherwise make.

Both are `does not` constructions. PR #62's provenance-frame regex catches the second along
with the first. See `PR62_CRAFT_GAP_ANALYSIS.md` §9.

## 10. Consequence

Consequences are stated plainly and are usually the last substantive move before a closing
quote: *"droogte, overstromingen, bosbranden en mislukte oogsten"*; infrastructure damage;
cholera. Consequence is enumerated, not dramatised.

## 11. Numbers

Number density 9.9 per 1,000 words. The distinctive handling is the explicit comparison marker
and the unit translation:

> *"Ter vergelijking: op een locatie verder naar het noordoosten vonden onderzoekers twee
> Regenboogslangen op 352 plekken."*
> *"…een massa van 200 gigaelektronvolt hebben gehad. Dat is 200 keer de massa van een proton."*
> *"…de wereld telt ongeveer 20.000 soorten bijen"* (set against the one species all prior
> knowledge came from).

## 12. Sentence and paragraph shape

| measure | Scientias (n=25) | Bregman (n=15) | control exemplars (n=12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean sentence length | 15.80 | 15.31 | 19.66 |
| sentence SD | **6.70** | 8.29 | 10.15 |
| commas per sentence | **0.54** | 0.88 | 1.35 |
| single-clause sentences | 48% | 60% | 44% |
| mean words per paragraph | 54.0 | 58.2 | 67.8 (n=2) |
| paragraph-length SD | **15.9** | 25.1 | 28.2 (n=2) |
| paragraphs ≤15 words | **0%** | 8% | 3% (n=2) |
| quote-bearing paragraph share | 0.23 | 0.25 | 0.48 |

The signature is **uniformity**: near-identical paragraph sizes, the lowest comma load of the
three samples, and no pivot paragraphs at all. Pacing is done inside the sentence, not on the
page.

## 13. Where it becomes press-release formula

Named plainly so it is not transferred by accident.

- **The fixed running order.** finding → background → expert quote → method → limitation →
  closing expert quote. Twenty-one of twenty-five follow it closely. It is efficient and it is
  a template.
- **Ending on a quote.** Near-universal, and it is the weakest structural habit in the sample.
  The final word is almost always an authority saying the finding matters, which is exactly the
  ending the Storygram annotators criticise in the two cases they flag.
- **The single-study frame.** The article is usually about *one paper*, so the world is
  organised around a publication event rather than around a subject.
- **No organising human.** Researchers are named as attribution. Nobody is followed. Nobody
  changes.
- **Interruptions.** `Leestip:` links break the read mid-argument. Site business, not craft.
- **Headline-as-tease** with the answer withheld to the lede. Cheap when the answer is
  "maybe".

## 14. What should transfer to Crip Minds, and what must not

**Transfer:**

1. **Uncertainty as structure, not vocabulary.** A limitations passage in short plain
   sentences, placed before the consequence, rather than hedging adverbs distributed through
   the prose. This is the single most valuable import.
2. **The mechanism as a chain of one-link clauses**, with no interpretation inserted mid-chain.
3. **The gloss placed at first working use**, and where possible before the term.
4. **The reason to care carried in the same sentence as the subject.**
5. **Unit and scale translation** with an explicit comparison marker where useful.
6. **De-interpretation** — telling the reader what a figure does not license — as a device
   distinct from evidence-state commentary.

**Must not transfer:**

1. **The macro running order.** Crip Minds is not a single-paper news outlet. Adopting the
   finding→background→quote→method→limitation→quote spine would give every article the same
   shape, which is the failure mode the campaign is trying to escape.
2. **Ending on an expert quote.**
3. **Paragraph uniformity.** Scientias' 15.9-word paragraph SD is the opposite of the "calm,
   book-like reading" the owner wants; Bregman's 25.1 is the model there.
4. **The publication event as the subject.**
5. **Zero pivot paragraphs.** Scientias' pacing solution works at 650 words and would read as
   flat at 1,500.
