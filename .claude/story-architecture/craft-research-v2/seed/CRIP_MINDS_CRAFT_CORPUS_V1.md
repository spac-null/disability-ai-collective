# Crip Minds Craft Corpus & Evidence v1

**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Scope:** 12 public Rutger Bregman pieces, 20 Scientias pieces across multiple science domains, and 16 professional craft/teaching sources.  
**Method:** qualitative reverse engineering of publicly accessible texts/snippets and explicit craft instruction. This report stores derived structure/craft observations, not copied article bodies.

## Executive finding

The current Crip Minds direction is partly validated, but the craft evidence argues against a single fixed `object → discovery → turn → Crip turn → ending` template.

The strongest cross-source model is:

**REPORT/RESEARCH FOR THE FORM YOU WANT → CHOOSE THE ACTUAL STORY/FORM → MOVE BETWEEN CONCRETE AND ABSTRACT → EXPLAIN JUST IN TIME → CONTROL INFORMATION PACE → LET STRUCTURE VARY WITH MATERIAL → EDIT AWAY VISIBLE SCAFFOLDING.**

Bregman's strongest transferable characteristic is not merely “simple sentences.” It is **controlled alternation of cognitive registers**: concrete case, question, explanation, another case, larger idea, counterevidence, consequence.

Scientias is strongest as a **complexity-explanation reference**, not a macro narrative template.

## 2. What the corpus actually says about good accessible nonfiction

### A. There is no universal opening formula

Bregman uses:
- dated scenes/events;
- explicit arguments;
- intellectual questions;
- historical artefacts;
- direct provocation.

Scientias likewise ranges from novelty ledes to familiar-life examples and historical mini-narratives.

**Engine rule:** do **not** require a scene-first opening. Choose the strongest source of narrative or intellectual tension, then give the reader something concrete quickly.

### B. Concrete and abstract should alternate, not be permanently separated

The Bregman sample repeatedly moves:

**CONCRETE → IDEA → CONCRETE → EXPLANATION → BIGGER IDEA → CONCRETE**

This is more important than average sentence length.

**Engine implication:** Story Architect should plan **register movement**, not only beats.

Suggested machine-side field:

`MODE = SCENE | EVENT | SUMMARY | EXPLANATION | CONTEXT | REFLECTION | ARGUMENT`

Long unbroken runs of `EXPLANATION/ARGUMENT` should trigger editorial inspection, not automatic failure.

### C. Report for narrative

Berkeley, Harvard and Nieman converge strongly here: scene, action, character and chronology must be obtained during reporting/research.

**Engine implication:** Research Need Gate needs story-material requests, not only source-count requests:

- `DOCUMENTED_HUMAN_ENCOUNTER`
- `SCENE_CAPABLE_DETAIL`
- `DECISION_MOMENT`
- `CHRONOLOGY`
- `REAL_CONSEQUENCE`
- `MECHANISM_DETAIL`
- `CONFLICTING_ACCOUNT`
- `OBJECT_HISTORY`

If the story needs a perceiver and the pack contains none, this is a **research need**, not a Writer opportunity.

### D. Article form should be selected before Story Architect

The craft sources explicitly teach multiple forms. The corpus also shows that strong popular nonfiction can be narrative, reported essay, explanatory feature or polemic.

Recommended gate:

- `NARRATIVE_FEATURE`
- `REPORTED_ESSAY`
- `EXPLANATORY_FEATURE`
- `SHORT_FEATURE`
- `BRIEF`
- `HOLD_NO_STORY`
- `HOLD_WRONG_PUBLICATION`

A Jia-like object-led piece should not be failed merely for lacking a human protagonist if `REPORTED_ESSAY` or `EXPLANATORY_FEATURE` is its honest form.

### E. Explain when the reader needs the explanation

The Open Notebook and Scientias strongly converge on:
- familiar thing first;
- reason-to-care before jargon;
- technical term at first meaningful need;
- just enough background at that moment.

**Engine implication:** replace some machine-centric beat planning with:

`READER_NOW_WONDERS`

and:

`ANSWER_NEEDED_NOW`

Example:

`READER_NOW_WONDERS: what was the salt room actually for?`

Only then provide the facts needed to answer it.

### F. Information pacing deserves its own layer

The owner's “give the reader breathing room” aligns with professional craft guidance about pacing information.

**Breathing room means:**
- a fact can stand before interpretation;
- a difficult idea can have ordinary sentences around it;
- a concrete object can exist before symbolism;
- not every sentence must advance the abstraction.

It does **not** mean padding, decorative detail or invented atmosphere.

### G. Scene, summary and exposition should be separate modes before prose smoothing

A major source of Crip Minds overcompression has been trying to make one sentence perform several jobs.

Craft sources explicitly distinguish:
- **scene** — specific moment/action;
- **summary** — compresses time/material;
- **exposition** — pauses to explain;
- **reflection/argument** — makes meaning.

**Engine implication:** tag passage function first; let the Writer/Prose Finisher make transitions natural later.

### H. Explicit thesis is not inherently bad

The corpus disproves a hard “never state the thesis” rule.

Bregman often states arguments directly. What failed in Jia was **mandatory serialization of an architecture field after the reader already understood the point**.

Better rule:

> State an idea when stating it helps the reader. Never state it merely because the schema contains a thesis/lens field.

The current `lens realization, not lens announcement` direction remains correct.

### I. Transitions should be functional and nearly invisible

The craft evidence supports the owner's rejection of phrases that expose the outline.

Diagnostic:
> If a transition announces the architecture, try deleting it before improving it.

Do not blacklist words like *so*, *which*, or *but*. Judge function.

### J. Endings should be found, not manufactured

Professional craft sources explicitly advise testing whether the story ended earlier than the draft thinks.

**Engine implication:** ending stage should test:
1. current ending;
2. one paragraph earlier;
3. two paragraphs earlier.

Prefer the **last necessary moment**, not the cleverest final line.

## 3. Transferable Bregman craft profile

This is **not** a style-imitation specification. It is a profile of general craft mechanisms observable across the public sample.

### Macro

- Wide research; aggressive selection.
- A concrete case often carries a much larger idea.
- Multiple examples are distributed through long pieces rather than dumped together.
- Frequent alternation between story, explanation, research, argument and consequence.
- Explicit questions often open causal problems.
- Hard concepts are surrounded by ordinary prose.
- Numbers are translated into familiar scales or consequences.
- Researchers/historical people often **do something** rather than appearing only as citations.
- Research can itself become narrative when searching/discovery genuinely contains events.
- Argument may be explicit; concrete material then earns and tests it.

### Sentence / vocabulary profile

Transferable tendencies:
- common-core verbs;
- active subjects;
- necessary technical nouns, but plain syntax around them;
- ordinary causal connectors;
- familiar comparisons/analogies;
- concrete nouns before abstract labels;
- low dependence on bureaucratic nominalizations.

Useful direction:
- `use` over `utilise`
- `shows` over `demonstrates` when equal
- `people` over `individuals` when equal
- `later` over `subsequently`
- `about` over `approximately` where precision permits
- verbs over nominalized phrases

**Important:** many English Bregman book/article texts are translated/edited collaboratively. Treat this as a **Bregman/translator readability profile**, not a fingerprint of one person's vocabulary.

### What NOT to inherit mechanically

Bregman also uses:
- rhetorical questions;
- fragments;
- direct address;
- explicit morals;
- provocative claims;
- repeated calls to action.

The owner prefers a calmer Crip Minds surface. These devices stay optional.

## 4. Scientias clarity profile

Scientias is most useful for **micro-explanation**.

Recurring useful moves:
- familiar observation before mechanism;
- finding before term;
- term followed by immediate plain-language gloss;
- reason-to-care before methodological detail;
- short causal chain;
- explicit limitation/caveat near the point where it matters.

Particularly useful models in this corpus:
- habits → everyday routines → brain circuits;
- wintersleep memory → surprising result → define synapse → fundamental question → experiment;
- quantum gravity → explain two theories in plain language → show where experiment sits;
- sharks → observational blind spot → why current tools fail → why tagged animals solve it;
- Arctic tipping point → define concept → ice loss → sunlight → nitrate → food web.

Do **not** inherit Scientias's frequent science-news macro-template:
`new study → researchers → method → result → quote`.

## 5. Evidence-backed engine changes to test before production integration

### Keep from #62

- evidence-bound ledger;
- Worth Gate;
- minimal Writer packet;
- no fact minting;
- negative/absence contract;
- lens realization;
- Continuity Editor;
- sentence lineage;
- semantic/relational delta checks;
- research invisibility.

### Modify / add

#### 1. Article Form Gate

Select honest form from material before story architecture.

#### 2. Research-for-Story Need Gate

Ask for missing narrative material explicitly.

#### 3. Reader Question Map

For each section:
- what does reader now know?
- what do they naturally wonder?
- what minimum material answers it?

#### 4. Mode Map

Plan:
`SCENE / EVENT / SUMMARY / CONTEXT / EXPLANATION / REFLECTION / ARGUMENT`

Do not let any mode become a mandatory paragraph.

#### 5. Abstraction Movement

Track movement on:
`CONCRETE ↔ ABSTRACT`

Flag:
- long runs stuck in abstract middle;
- repeated fact→meaning compression;
- multiple new hard concepts before a concrete anchor.

#### 6. Ending Upstream Test

Before manufacturing an ending, ask whether the article is already finished.

## 6. Owner taste vs craft evidence vs safety

This distinction should become explicit in the doctrine.

### CRAFT EVIDENCE

Supported by professional craft sources / corpus:
- report for narrative;
- choose structure from material;
- control information pace;
- alternate narrative/exposition when useful;
- explain just in time;
- move concrete↔abstract;
- transitions must express real logic;
- scene requires reported detail;
- different article forms need different structures.

### OWNER TASTE

Crip Minds-specific:
- calmer than typical Bregman;
- fewer rhetorical flourishes;
- more breathing room;
- plain factual description before interpretive flourish;
- avoid paragraph-by-paragraph punchline rhythm;
- trust the reader;
- Crip lens usually realized rather than announced.

### SAFETY INVARIANTS

- no fabricated scenes;
- no unsupported causality;
- no invented people/reactions;
- no negative-world claim from silence;
- no CUT leakage;
- no provenance/machine reasoning in reader prose;
- downstream grounding/fact check unchanged.

## 7. Regression / training exercises

1. **Reverse outline**
   - Label the job of every paragraph in a strong feature.

2. **Abstraction ladder**
   - Mark each paragraph concrete / middle / abstract.
   - Identify stretches stuck in bureaucratic middle abstraction.

3. **Mode necklace**
   - Encode `S` scene, `M` summary, `E` exposition, `R` reflection/argument.
   - Inspect monotony.

4. **Reader-question map**
   - Before every explanation, write the question the reader is likely asking.

5. **Minimum explanation**
   - Remove explanation until a nonexpert first becomes confused; restore only what is needed.

6. **Research CUT**
   - Give Story Architect 30 good facts and require it to justify the small subset used.

7. **Scene-capable evidence**
   - Proposed scene cannot enter architecture without source-supported setting/action/detail.

8. **Chronology vs braid**
   - Build both from same material; blind compare comprehension and momentum.

9. **Transition deletion**
   - Delete explicit signposts; restore only where a real logical gap remains.

10. **Ending upstream**
    - Compare ending at current point / -1 paragraph / -2 paragraphs.

11. **Jargon timing**
    - Test technical term before versus after reason-to-care.

12. **Semantic decompression**
    - Identify sentences doing 3–4 intellectual jobs; split without adding facts.

## 8. Proposed Crip Minds craft stack v1

```text
SELECTION
  ↓
BROAD RESEARCH
  ↓
PRELIMINARY STORY DISCOVERY
  ↓
ARTICLE FORM GATE
  ↓
RESEARCH-FOR-STORY NEED GATE
  ↓
TARGETED RESEARCH
  ↓
FINAL EVIDENCE FREEZE
  ↓
FINAL WORTH GATE
  ↓
STORY / READER-QUESTION ARCHITECT
    - story carrier
    - form
    - chronology/causality where supported
    - reader-question map
    - mode map
    - concrete↔abstract movement
    - USE / CUT
    - lens realization
  ↓
MINIMAL WRITER PACKET
  ↓
WRITER
  ↓
NATURAL PROSE / CONTINUITY EDITOR
  ↓
SEMANTIC + FACTUAL DELTA
  ↓
GROUNDER
  ↓
FACT CHECK
  ↓
HUMAN READER
```

The key doctrine becomes:

> **Do the reporting that the story requires. Choose the form the evidence can honestly support. Give the reader something concrete to hold. Explain only when the reader needs it. Move between concrete and abstract. Let the research disappear.**

## 10. Evidence confidence

**High confidence**
- reporting must support narrative material;
- multiple article structures/forms are legitimate;
- just-in-time explanation;
- information pacing matters;
- concrete↔abstract movement;
- scene/summary/exposition separation;
- transitions and endings are structural craft issues, not word-choice issues.

**Medium confidence**
- exact Bregman-specific lexical profile; current v1 is qualitative.
- exact ratios of scene/exposition/argument across Bregman's work.

**Not yet claimed**
- statistically meaningful sentence-length, verb-frequency, nominalization or paragraph-function distributions.
- an author-specific “Bregman lexicon.”

Those require a clean machine-readable public-text corpus and quantitative pass. v1 deliberately does not invent those numbers.
