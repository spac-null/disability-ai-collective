### S01 — QWERTY letter-arrangement persistence

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** Christopher Latham Sholes, a Milwaukee printer and newspaper
editor, developed the QWERTY letter arrangement through the early 1870s across multiple
prototype layouts. E. Remington and Sons put the Sholes and Glidden typewriter, using this
layout, on the market in 1874. Remington's own training materials, typing schools, and
competing manufacturers seeking workforce compatibility subsequently standardized on the same
arrangement. A commonly cited design rationale is that separating certain frequently-adjacent
letter pairs reduced mechanical typebar clashes in early machines; the original 1878 patent
does not state a rationale. The layout carried over unchanged onto electric typewriters and
later computer keyboards, and remains the default for most Latin-alphabet keyboards,
physical and on-screen, worldwide.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The 1874 Sholes and Glidden letter arrangement and its
persistence onto later devices.

### S02 — NOAA Coral Reef Watch Degree Heating Week thresholds

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** NOAA's Coral Reef Watch program produces a daily, global, 5km
satellite product called Degree Heating Week (DHW), accumulating sea-surface-temperature heat
stress associated with coral bleaching over a rolling 12-week window. NOAA publishes specific
thresholds for this metric: at 8 °C-weeks, reef-wide bleaching with mortality of
heat-sensitive corals is described as likely; at 12, multi-species mortality; at 16 and above,
severe multi-species mortality in more than half of corals; at 20 and above, near-complete
mortality. A related satellite product, Bleaching Alert Area, maps where this heat stress
currently reaches those levels. Separately, marine scientists conduct in-water visual
surveys — diver transects assessing individual coral colonies — used to calibrate and
corroborate what the satellite thermal-stress product predicts.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The Degree Heating Week thresholds and the separate in-water
visual-survey method used to calibrate them.

### S03 — Toyota Production System andon cord/button

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The andon cord is a documented feature of the Toyota Production
System: a physical rope running the length of an assembly line that any line worker may pull
to signal a problem, which can stop the line. It is described by Toyota as an expression of
"jidoka" — automation with a human touch — in which either a machine detects an abnormality
and stops itself, or a person stops the line manually. Toyota's own account states that
workers have both a right and an obligation to pull the cord on discovering a problem;
supervisors and engineers are expected to respond immediately when it is pulled. Since 2014,
Toyota has been progressively replacing the physical cord with a wireless andon button at
some facilities.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The andon cord/button mechanism and who is authorized to use it.

### S04 — NYC Subway ADA elevator settlement

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The New York City subway system was built almost entirely without
elevators; in the mid-2020s, most of its 472 stations lacked step-free access. In June 2022,
plaintiffs (Center for Independence of the Disabled New York and other organizations) and the
Metropolitan Transportation Authority signed a settlement agreement, given final court
approval in April 2023, committing the MTA to make at least 95% of the subway's 364
then-inaccessible stations accessible by 2055, via a staged schedule (81 stations by 2025, 85
more by 2035, 90 more by 2045, 90 more by 2055) and a capital-plan funding commitment of at
least 14.69% of each five-year capital plan toward station accessibility. The MTA separately
publishes real-time data on elevator and escalator outages across the system.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The 2022/2023 settlement's staged station schedule and the
separately published elevator/escalator outage data.

### S05 — ITU-R BS.1770 loudness and the *Death Magnetic* mastering controversy

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** From the 1990s through the 2000s, commercial music mastering
increasingly used dynamic-range compression and limiting to raise a recording's average
loudness. Metallica's 2008 album *Death Magnetic*, mastered by Ted Jensen at Sterling Sound,
became a widely publicized case: fans documented audible clipping distortion, and a petition
asking for a remaster gathered over 13,000 signatures. Commentators noted that heavily
compressed mastering could make an album sound louder at a given volume setting than earlier,
less-compressed releases. ITU-R Recommendation BS.1770 defines a standardized method for
measuring perceived loudness, subsequently adopted by broadcasters and, later, by streaming
services for loudness normalization.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The *Death Magnetic* mastering choice and the BS.1770 loudness
measurement standard.

### S06 — Body Mass Index

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** In 1832, Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet devised a formula
— weight divided by height squared — as part of population-level statistical work. In 1972,
physiologist Ancel Keys and colleagues published a paper, "Indices of relative weight and
obesity," in the *Journal of Chronic Diseases*, renaming and popularizing this formula as
"Body Mass Index." BMI's category boundaries (e.g. underweight/normal/overweight/obese) are
used today by clinicians for screening, by some insurers for underwriting, by some employers
for wellness-program eligibility, and by some athletic and military bodies for fitness
classification. International bodies including the World Health Organization have revised
BMI category thresholds more than once.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** A WHO or comparable body's BMI threshold revision and the
population it reclassifies.

### S07 — GMDSS replacing Morse-code SOS watchkeeping

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** SOS, a Morse-code distress signal, was formally adopted
internationally in 1906, requiring trained radio officers on ships to maintain continuous
listening watches. Amendments to the SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea) treaty, adopted in 1988,
introduced the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS), which became fully
mandatory on February 1, 1999 for passenger ships and cargo ships of 300 gross tonnage and
above on international voyages. GMDSS replaced continuous human Morse watchkeeping with
automated distress alerting: VHF/MF/HF Digital Selective Calling, satellite communication via
Recognized Mobile Satellite Services, and 406 MHz EPIRB beacons linked to the Cospas-Sarsat
satellite system.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The February 1, 1999 mandatory transition from continuous human
Morse watchkeeping to automated satellite/digital distress alerting.

### S08 — ISO 668 intermodal shipping container

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** In the 1950s, Malcolm McLean's Sea-Land Corporation began using
standardized, stackable steel containers moved directly between ship, rail and truck without
unpacking. International standardization followed via ISO 668, formally adopted in 1968,
fixing container lengths (most commonly 20 and 40 feet), width (approximately 2.44m), height
(approximately 2.59m standard, 2.9m "high cube"), and corner-casting fittings, so containers
interchange across ports, cranes, rail cars and trucks built to those dimensions worldwide.
Cargo that does not fit the standard footprint — oversized, irregularly shaped, or requiring
different handling — is moved instead as "out of gauge" or breakbulk freight, using separate
handling arrangements and generally at additional cost compared with standard container
rates.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The ISO 668 fixed container envelope and the separate "out of
gauge" handling arrangement for cargo outside it.

### S09 — SAT/ACT extended-time flagging, ended 2003

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The College Board and ACT offer extended time and other
accommodations for standardized-test takers with documented disabilities, through an
application and documentation-review process. A 1999 lawsuit, *Breimhorst v. Educational
Testing Service*, was brought by a Stanford student born without hands who was granted extra
time on the GMAT; it was settled with ETS agreeing to stop flagging GMAT scores taken under
nonstandard conditions. In mid-2002, a separate settlement between Disability Rights
Advocates and the College Board ended the practice of flagging SAT, PSAT, and Advanced
Placement scores earned with extended time; effective October 1, 2003, the College Board
stopped noting "Nonstandard Administration" on such score reports, and ACT adopted the same
policy two weeks later.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The documentation-review process that grants extended time, and
the pre-2003 flagging practice it replaced.

### S10 — Gregorian calendar leap-year rule

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The Gregorian calendar, introduced in 1582 and now in near-universal
civil use, adds a leap day (February 29) in years divisible by 4, except century years, unless
those century years are also divisible by 400 (so 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 and 2100 are
not). This rule keeps the calendar year closely aligned with the astronomical solar year. Legal
and administrative systems that calculate ages, terms, renewal dates, or anniversaries from a
birth date or start date of February 29 must specify how those dates are handled in non-leap
years, since no February 29 exists in most years; different jurisdictions and institutions
have adopted different conventions for this (e.g., treating the "effective" date as either
February 28 or March 1).

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The leap-year rule itself and the varying institutional
conventions for handling a February 29 date in a non-leap year.

### S11 — 2019 SI redefinition of the kilogram via the Planck constant

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** In November 2018, representatives of more than 60 countries voted
at the 26th General Conference on Weights and Measures to redefine the kilogram. Effective May
20, 2019 (World Metrology Day), the kilogram is defined by fixing the value of the Planck
constant at exactly 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ kg·m²/s, rather than by reference to a physical
platinum-iridium cylinder ("Le Grand K") kept in France since the 1880s. This made the
kilogram, along with the other base SI units, defined entirely from fixed constants of nature
rather than from a physical artifact, intended to improve measurement precision, particularly
at very small mass scales.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The May 20, 2019 switch from an artifact-based to a
constant-based definition of the kilogram.

### S12 — Superpave asphalt mix-design specification

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** "Superpave" (Superior Performing Asphalt Pavements) is the result
of the asphalt-research portion of the U.S. Strategic Highway Research Program (SHRP), a
five-year, $50 million federal research effort conducted from October 1987 through March 1993.
It produced an asphalt binder specification, a hot-mix-asphalt design method, and associated
performance-prediction tests and models, intended to replace older mix-design methods (Hveem
and Marshall). By the early 2000s, most U.S. states had adopted or committed to adopting the
Superpave binder specification and mix-design method for highway pavement construction.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The Superpave mix-design method and its replacement of the
Hveem/Marshall methods across U.S. state highway agencies.

### S13 — BIFMA office-furniture ergonomic standards

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** BIFMA (Business and Institutional Furniture Manufacturers
Association), founded in 1973, develops voluntary product and industry standards for office
and institutional furniture. ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 is a widely used performance and safety standard
for general-purpose office chairs, covering safety, strength, stability and durability
(including a minimum 22-inch seat width for compliance). BIFMA G1 is a separate ergonomics
guideline addressing whole-workstation adjustability — desk height, keyboard position, knee
clearance — intended so a workstation can be adjusted to fit an individual user. BIFMA's own
published materials state that meeting a BIFMA standard is not automatic proof that a given
chair or workstation is comfortable or suitable for every individual user.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The X5.1 seat-width/safety standard and the separate G1
adjustability guideline, and BIFMA's own stated limitation on what compliance proves.

### S14 — Restaurant kitchen ticket-time / expediting system

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** In professional kitchens organized under the classical "brigade de
cuisine" system, incoming customer orders are printed or displayed as tickets at a pass or
expediting station. An expediter (often the head chef or a designated "expo") is responsible
for sequencing and timing multiple simultaneous tickets so that all components of a table's
order — dishes prepared at different stations, with different cook times — are finished and
plated together. This role requires tracking the real-time status of several dishes across
multiple cooking stations at once, and coordinating the stations verbally or via a ticket
system so their outputs converge at a single moment.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The expediter's role coordinating multiple simultaneous tickets
to a single convergence point.

### S15 — USPS ZIP+4 delivery-point coding

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The United States Postal Service introduced the five-digit ZIP
(Zone Improvement Plan) code in 1963. In 1983, USPS launched ZIP+4, adding four digits to the
five-digit code to narrow a delivery area to a specific carrier-route segment — typically a
city block, an apartment cluster, or a single high-volume recipient. Mail bearing a complete,
correctly matched ZIP+4 code can be automatically sorted by processing machinery to a specific
carrier route; by 1993, about 75% of American addresses had an assigned ZIP+4 code. An address
that does not match a recognized delivery point in USPS's address database may not receive a
ZIP+4 code and may be processed or delivered differently.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The ZIP+4 delivery-point match and what happens to an address
that does not match a recognized delivery point.

### S16 — *Larry P. v. Riles* — IQ testing and special-education placement

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** *Larry P. v. Riles* was filed in 1971 in the U.S. District Court
for the Northern District of California by the families of five Black children placed in
classes for the "educable mentally retarded" (EMR) in the San Francisco Unified School
District, on the basis of IQ test scores. At the time, Black students were about 10% of
California's general student population but roughly 25% of students enrolled in EMR classes.
On October 16, 1979, Judge Robert Peckham ruled that the IQ tests used were racially and
culturally biased, that their use resulted in disproportionate placement of Black students in
EMR classes, and that this violated equal-protection guarantees. The ruling barred the school
district from using IQ tests to identify or place Black students in EMR-type special-education
classes, and ordered monitoring of placement patterns to address the disproportionate
representation.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The IQ-test instrument's role in the EMR placement decision, and
the 1979 ruling barring its use for that purpose for Black students.

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** Tollymore is a house by Belfast practice McGonigle McGrath (Kieran

### D02 — Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope data pipeline

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** On August 30, the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched from
Kennedy Space Center on a Falcon Heavy, bound for the second Lagrange point. It carries a
300-megapixel infrared Wide Field Instrument with Hubble's sharpness but at least 100 times
Hubble's field of view, surveying the sky roughly 1,000 times faster; NASA states one month of
Roman's observations of the Milky Way would take Hubble about a century. It also carries an
untested coronagraph. Roman is designed to downlink about one terabyte of data per day, which
NASA states "will be made public immediately, allowing researchers, teachers and science
enthusiasts alike to have access." It is designed to operate alongside the Vera C. Rubin
Observatory, described by NASA as a "sibling" mission. NASA's public statements do not specify
the data format, storage cost, retention period, or hardware needed to work with a daily
terabyte, and offer no comparison to how Hubble or Webb release data.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The daily 1TB public-release data pipeline and NASA's stated
"researchers, teachers and science enthusiasts alike" access claim.

### D03 — Beetaloo Basin carbon/methane accounting

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The Beetaloo Basin is a large shale gas basin in the Northern
Territory, Australia, roughly 500km southeast of Darwin. A 2018 government inquiry led by
Justice Rachel Pepper ("the Pepper Inquiry"), commissioned after a fracking moratorium,
concluded after a 15-month investigation that risks could be "appropriately managed" subject
to 135 recommendations, including recommendation 9.8: that Northern Territory and federal
governments seek to ensure no net increase in Australia's carbon emissions from fracked
Northern Territory gas. The NT government lifted its moratorium and later approved full-scale
fracking development; an independent officer overseeing implementation has stated that
recommendation 9.8 remains outstanding. NT government officials have said that "Scope 3"
emissions — from the eventual combustion of the extracted gas, mostly occurring outside the
Northern Territory or Australia — are not directly addressed by current NT government
mechanisms, and reporting indicates no finalized offset mechanism exists for the majority of
the project's projected total emissions.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** Pepper Inquiry recommendation 9.8 (no net increase in emissions)
and its reported implementation status.

### P01 — McDermitt Caldera lithium-clay deposit

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The McDermitt Caldera, on the Nevada–Oregon border, has been
identified in published geological research as containing a large lithium-bearing clay
deposit, formed from volcanic ash weathering in an ancient lake environment roughly 16 million
years ago. Published estimates describe it as potentially one of the largest known lithium

manufacturing. Exploration and mining proposals in the caldera (including the Thacker Pass
project) have drawn environmental and Indigenous-rights concerns regarding water use, habitat,
and sites of cultural and historical significance, and have been the subject of legal
challenges over federal permitting.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The published geological survey identifying and estimating the
deposit.

### P03 — FAA insulin-treated diabetes special-issuance protocol

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** The FAA requires pilots to hold a medical certificate issued after
examination against published fitness standards. From 1996, the FAA allowed insulin-treated
diabetes for third-class certification only, which permits private-pilot privileges;
commercial and airline-transport applicants with insulin-treated diabetes were categorically
ineligible regardless of individual glucose control or flight record. In November 2019, the
FAA published a special-issuance protocol allowing individualized evaluation of
insulin-treated applicants for first- and second-class (commercial/airline-transport)
certificates, based on continuous glucose monitoring data and a structured review process
rather than a blanket exclusion. The first commercial medical certificate issued under this
protocol was in April 2020.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The FAA's 2019 special-issuance protocol and what changed from
the prior categorical exclusion.

### P04 — Hybrid III 50th-percentile-male crash test dummy

**NEUTRAL_WORLD_SUMMARY:** U.S. automotive frontal crash safety testing uses anthropomorphic
test devices ("crash test dummies") whose specifications are set in federal regulation. The
Hybrid III 50th-percentile adult male dummy, developed in 1976 and incorporated into U.S.
federal motor vehicle safety regulations (49 CFR Part 572, Subpart E) in 1986, represents a
mid-sized adult male (approximately 172 lb, 5'9" standing height), and became, and remains,
the most widely used dummy for regulatory frontal-crash testing worldwide. Other dummy sizes
exist and are used in some tests (including a smaller "5th-percentile female" dummy and child
dummies), but historically the 50th-percentile male dummy has been the primary or sole device
required in many regulatory test configurations. NHTSA has published research evaluating
dummy biofidelity and considering additional test-device requirements.

**KEY_OBJECT_SYSTEM_EVENT:** The Hybrid III 50th-percentile-male dummy's regulatory role and
the existence, and limited regulatory use, of other-sized test devices.
