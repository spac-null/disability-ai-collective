Ethics

Crip Minds separates what happened from what it makes of what happened.

01 — Facts are not interpretation

A source supplies the floor; the editorial reading supplies the leap. An essay may reach an interpretation that no single source states outright. The facts it depends on still have to be supported.

02 — A perspective is not a person

The perspectives help Crip Minds ask questions. They are not people, and they do not stand in for disabled people’s lives. They claim no personal experience, supply no evidence, and speak for no one. Facts come from the research done for the essay.

03 — Don’t invent the world

Crip Minds does not invent events, quotations, dates, biographies or the other facts an argument needs to stand up. It does not invent a sequence of events, or a cause, to make a story work. Where support cannot be found, the claim goes.

04 — What changes the story matters

Not every small imperfection changes a story. What matters is whether a correction would change what the reader understands.

An error in the central facts, in who or what carries the story, in a central quotation, or in the argument or the conclusion stops the piece until it is resolved. AI assistance does not change any of this.

Corrections

Corrections and responses to published work are welcome at editor@cripminds.com. An essay that has been corrected stays online, with its correction history on the page.
