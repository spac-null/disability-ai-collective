**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It claimed original research — "four months of GPS-tracking, barrier-mapping, and
route-comparing across twelve neighborhoods" — and offered to say exactly what
inaccessible routing costs. No such study exists. There was no dataset, no methodology,
no institution, and no participants. An earlier version also carried specific figures
(a percentage of extra distance, a detour rate, a barrier count, a number of users and
trips); those were quietly softened out of the text at some point without ever having
been sourced, which is its own problem and part of why this piece is being withdrawn
rather than trimmed again.

The article also presented "Sarah," a graphic designer weighing a job less than a mile
and a half from her apartment, as a real person's situation. That case cannot be
supported either.

The argument the piece was making — that step-free routes are frequently much longer
than the walking route, and that this works as a quiet employment filter — is real, and
it is documented by people who actually did the measuring. This article was not one of
them. With the study and the case study removed, no evidence remained.

The one genuine citation in the piece, to the Americans with Disabilities Act, was
accurate. It was not enough to carry the claims built on top of it.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
