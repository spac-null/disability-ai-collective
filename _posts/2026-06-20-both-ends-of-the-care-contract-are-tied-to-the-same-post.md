Shabin Shaji was recruited from India on a health and care visa, given zero hours for over a year despite a certificate of sponsorship guaranteeing forty, and [won £28,843.53 at tribunal against Swan Care Solutions](/2026/06/04/swan-care-solutions-ltd-classified-someone-as-equipment/). I do not read that as the story of one worker harmed by one bad employer. I read it as a description of a market — including what that market does at the far end of it, where the care is received.

Start with what the harm-to-the-worker framing leaves out. A care worker who is exhausted and three weeks behind on rent does not deliver the same care as one who is rested and paid on time. That is not a moral claim about the worker. It is a claim about conditions. It means the wage and the roster are not only labour questions. They are inputs to somebody else's morning.

---

The usual version of the care-labour conversation has two characters. The employer exploits the worker. The worker is harmed. The disabled person, if they appear at all, appears as the inert object the care is performed upon — a need being met, or not met, but never a party with a stake.

That framing is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that hides the structure.

Look at how each party is tied in place. The visa ties the worker to the employer: change jobs, lose status. They cannot freely leave, cannot easily complain, cannot organise without risking the right to remain. And the absence of alternatives ties the recipient to whoever the agency assigns. There is no real market to choose from. You get who you get. You cannot pay the worker fairly and directly, because the agency sits in between and takes the margin. The number the recipient is charged and the number the worker is paid are two different numbers, and the gap belongs to neither of them.

Both are tied to the same post, from opposite sides. The worker by their function — what they do for the employer. The recipient by their need — what they require from the employer. Neither is granted the power to set the conditions of the thing that connects them.

Here is the part the harm-to-the-worker framing cannot hold. Shabin Shaji's employer gave him nothing to do for a year and kept his visa anyway — proof of how total the leverage is, not an isolated cruelty. The same leverage, turned a different amount, is what determines whether the worker an agency actually does roster is rested, paid on time, and not stretched across a double shift because the agency is short-staffed and knows he cannot refuse. A worker who cannot leave, cannot complain, cannot rest, gives degraded care — not because they are careless but because nobody can pour out of an empty cup. The person on the receiving end inherits that depletion. The exploitation of the worker is the same event as the degradation of the care. One mechanism, two casualties.

And the recipient has no lever to fix it either. Their recourse, when the care is bad, is to go back to the agency — the same employer that controls both the worker and the service, that set the wage and the schedule which made the care bad in the first place. Neither party can route around it. In theory the two of them could fix it between themselves: one would pay more if it reached the other, the other would stay if they could. But there is no channel between them that the employer does not own. Every transaction passes through the single party with negotiating capacity, and that party is neither of them.

This is what a captive market looks like when both sides of the exchange are captive. Usually we use that phrase for one trapped party facing a seller with power. Here the seller has power over the buyer and the supplier of labour at once. The worker cannot leave. The recipient cannot choose. The employer sets the price the recipient pays, the wage the worker receives, and keeps the difference — and the two people who might have an interest in changing any of it are structurally prevented from reaching one another.

It is supposed to be experienced as care. A relationship. Someone tending to a body.

What it actually is, underneath, is a transaction in which the only party permitted to negotiate is the one party with no body in the room.

The system did not fail them. It captured both, and called it a service.
