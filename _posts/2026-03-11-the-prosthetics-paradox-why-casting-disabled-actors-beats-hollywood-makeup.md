**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn, for two reasons.

The first is the evidence. The piece was built on a boyhood friendship — watching an
awards-season performance at fourteen in a friend's bedroom, both of them autistic, and
that friend's cousin, described closely enough that a reader could picture his hands and
his cerebral palsy. It also described consulting work on a video game. None of this can
be supported. The argument rested on those people and that work, and they cannot be
stood behind.

The second reason is worse, and it is why this is a withdrawal rather than a correction.
Two of those people were hyperlinked, in the ordinary way this site links a source — and
both links pointed at example.com, a placeholder domain. A reader who wanted to check who
these people were would have found the article offering them what looked like a citation
and led nowhere. Formatting nothing as though it were sourcing is not a missing citation;
it is the appearance of one.

The underlying argument — that casting a disabled actor and applying prosthetics to a
non-disabled one are not two routes to the same performance, because the adaptation being
depicted is real and accumulated — is an argument we still hold. It needs to be made from
material that exists.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
