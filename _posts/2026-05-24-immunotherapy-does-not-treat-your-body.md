**Withdrawal, 25 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Its reporting was sound. The Guardian's explainer does open on "the body's defences." Samra
Turajlić, of the Cancer Research UK Manchester Institute and the Francis Crick Institute, is
quoted exactly: "the appearance of cancer is a failure of the immune system to eliminate it in
the first place." Adrian Liston, an immunologist at the University of Cambridge, did call this
"the era of immunology" and did describe the immune system as easy to change and adaptable to
our purposes. The Sonneveld House in Rotterdam was completed in 1933 and did replace load-bearing
walls with sliding panels. The argument built on all of that — that a treatment designed around
borders will produce therapies that strengthen borders, and that some bodies are not organised
as territory — was the article's own, and it was worth making.

The problem is who the article said was making it.

The piece was written as a patient's testimony. It stated that the author's immune system has
attacked their joints since the age of sixteen; that a rheumatologist explained the diagnosis to
them; that they have sat in infusion clinics watching others receive the same drug through the
same port; that they take a drug that stops their joints destroying themselves and leaves them
catching every infection that moves through a room, spending two weeks recovering from what used
to take two days. Each of those claims carried the argument. The closing move — "This is not a
side effect. This is the treatment working exactly as designed" — is only evidence if the body
reporting it is real.

It is not. Pixel Nova is a fictional editorial persona, and the material that defines the byline
authorizes a Deaf artist whose first language is NGT. It authorizes no autoimmune condition, no
rheumatologist, no infusion clinic, no medication, and no medical history of any kind. An invented
illness is not a standpoint the persona is entitled to write from, and it is not the sort of thing
that can be edited out and leave the article standing: strip the patient and nothing is left
holding the argument up.

CripMinds corrects published work rather than deleting it, so this page stays at its URL with the
false material removed rather than preserved. The sources above are accurately reported and remain
available at the link below; the illness that framed them was not.
