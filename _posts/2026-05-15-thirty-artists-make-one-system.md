**Withdrawal, 25 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

The exhibition is real. The Lehman College Art Gallery did present the 2026 BFA, MA and MFA
Thesis Exhibition, on view 20 to 28 May 2026, with an opening reception on 20 May from 5–8pm
at which "Light refreshments will be served" — that phrase is the announcement's own. The
themes it lists, "identity, memory, technology, migration, and social space", are quoted
correctly. The Americans with Disabilities Act does date from 1990. That is the end of what
holds.

The essay's method was to read the announcement closely — "I copy the names into a
spreadsheet and start looking for patterns" — and the patterns it reported are not in the
document.

Two quotations attributed to the announcement do not appear in it. "Exploring different ideas
and materials" and "work that combines multiple art disciplines" are paraphrases of
"conceptual inquiry and material experimentation" and "interdisciplinary forms", presented
inside quotation marks as the institution's words.

The list is described as running "Caroline Alba to Franco Lopez, alphabetized by program
level". It is not alphabetised — Kosmas Fertitta-Boutis is listed after Lia Sornoza — and the
BFA list ends with Destiny Hurtado, not Franco Lopez.

The count is wrong. The announcement names 21 BFA students and 8 MA/MFA students, not
"twenty-one undergraduates, nine graduate students".

The gallery hours — "Tuesday through Saturday, 10 to 5. Closed Mondays for installation" —
are not in the announcement, and the closure reason is not stated anywhere. The passage that
begins "I pull up the gallery's accessibility page. There's a ramp. There's an elevator.
Compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act" describes reading a page that carries no
such information; neither the announcement nor the gallery's own site states opening hours or
accessibility provision.

The article also told a dated story about "an art student who needed the building empty to
think clearly", filming at 3am under a negotiated security arrangement, who "got distinction"
before the policy was changed the following year. There is no record of this and it is offered
as documented fact.

The observation underneath — that a press release listing thirty artists says nothing about
accommodation, and that institutions confuse compliance with inclusion — is a fair thing to
notice and did not require any of the above. But the piece established that observation by
misreporting the document it was reading, and the misreporting is not separable from the
argument. It is withdrawn.
