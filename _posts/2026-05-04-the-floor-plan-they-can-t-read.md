**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It began by misdescribing the piece it was written from. "Three Canadian provinces released AI
literacy frameworks this spring. I read all three." The cited article sets out three *curriculum
models* — a dedicated subject, embedding in existing subjects, and a transversal framework —
illustrated across British Columbia, Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, Alberta and Québec. It
released no frameworks and quoted no definition of critical thinking. The claim that each of
three frameworks "uses the phrase 'critical thinking'" in a particular way, and the Ontario
definition quoted here as "the ability to evaluate claims using evidence-based reasoning" —
along with "I have the PDF" — are not supported.

The comparison it was built on cannot be found. The article described a Helsinki vocational
school piloting an AI module in early 2024 built by Reaktor Education and structured around
"algorithmic intuition," and reported that "their facilitators told me, in a February 2024
call, that their strongest students were consistently the ones with ADHD or autism diagnoses.
Not sometimes. Consistently." Reaktor is a real company with real AI education work. That
pilot, that framing and that call could not be verified, and a research-like finding was put in
a named organisation's mouth through a private conversation nobody can check.

Two attributions to real people are also unsupported. Otto Neurath is said to have written "to
a colleague in 1944 that the system worked best when the viewer brought their own logic to the
image." No such letter could be found, and the documented account of Isotype runs the other
way — towards universality and neutrality with respect to interpretation. And Ari Ne'eman,
described here as ASAN's founder, is its co-founder, from 2006; the 2009 writing attributed to
him about systems cognition going uncredited could not be located.

The classroom did not happen either: a November 2022 Rotterdam lesson observed first-hand, a
fourteen-year-old autistic student finding bias in training data in six minutes, and a teacher
whispering "she's a natural." It opens the argument and closes the piece.

What was accurate: Otto Neurath built Isotype in the 1930s, Ari Ne'eman is a real autistic
advocate and ASAN is real, and the cited article exists.

The argument is a good one — that curricula teaching pattern recognition and systems thinking
from zero are ignoring students who already have both, and that this is an architecture problem
rather than an inclusion problem. It needed one real example. It had none, so there was nothing
to correct.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
