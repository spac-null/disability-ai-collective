**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Its only external source was a study that cannot be found. The piece stated that Jian Kang, a
professor of acoustics at UCL, "published a study in 2022 showing that mechanical noise in
retrofitted public buildings consistently runs louder than in original designs," that "Kang's
team measured dozens of retrofitted European buildings," and that the pattern was "stark: the
carbon target was met, and the acoustic environment got worse." Professor Kang is a real and
distinguished building acoustician, and none of that study, its method or its finding could be
located. The essay's turn — "what Kang's research shows, without quite saying it" — was
resting on research that has not been shown to exist.

The measurements were not measurements. The article asserted that the Purcell Room's old
ventilation had a noise rating of NC-20 and the new one "closer to NC-30," and dated the
change to the Southbank Centre switching on a new system in April 2023, adding that "nobody
announced it." An NC rating is a surveyed specification, not something obtainable by standing
in a foyer, and none of this could be verified.

The closing scene did not happen. The article described visiting a newly retrofitted library
in Rotterdam in March 2025, sitting for twenty minutes in a reading room whose ceiling had
been dropped for ductwork, and a librarian saying: "It used to be quieter. People used to stay
longer. I assumed it was phones." The essay's conclusion — that she had the right observation
and the wrong cause — depends entirely on a quotation nobody is recorded as saying.

One citation was also broken: the link given for Melbourne's Arts Centre pointed at a domain
that does not exist.

The claims about the Sejong Center, the Barbican and Melbourne's Arts Centre retrofit
timelines were not verified either way and are not being asserted here.

The underlying point is a real one and worth pursuing properly: retrofit programmes measure
carbon and thermal comfort, the noise floor of a room is also measurable, and it is not on the
checklist. That argument needs a real study and real measurements. This article had neither,
so there was nothing to correct.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
