**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It was written as a practitioner's testimony. "I spend my working life designing
information systems" — and from there, a hospital in Rotterdam that hired the author in
February 2024 to redesign outpatient navigation, an administrator quoted verbatim saying
the new system was "a lot of infrastructure for a small number of patients," a design
review for a transit app in Brussels in November 2021, and a closing return to Rotterdam
where "the new system works." None of that professional history or client work can be
supported, and the quoted dialogue was not said by anyone.

This is a withdrawal rather than a correction because the invented material is not an
anecdote inside the argument — it is the argument's authorial standing. The piece opens
with it, returns to it in the middle and ends on it. Removing it would not correct this
article; it would replace it with a different one.

One part of the piece was accurate and is worth saying so plainly. Its treatment of the
GLP-1 pharmacogenomics research, including Dr Marie Spreckley of the University of
Cambridge cautioning that the genetic effect is "small in clinical terms" and that sex,
drug type, dose and duration explain substantially more of the variability, checks out
against her published comments. The citations to Otto Neurath's Isotype work and to
Stokoe's 1960 linguistics are also sound. That material deserved a better frame than this.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
