**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Everything that carried it was invented. The author claimed fifteen years of designing
digital products, awards, and interfaces "used by millions of people." No such career
exists behind this byline.

"Mara" was presented as a real deaf designer — a colleague of six years, quoted directly
at a café window and again while reading a client's dashboard. She was not fictionalized,
flagged, or sourced. She was written as a real person giving real testimony, and there is
no evidence she exists.

The financial services company, the dashboard with "forty-seven different animations and
twelve colors," the afternoon spent diagnosing it, and the redesign that followed were all
presented as a real engagement. So were its results: tasks dropping from forty-two seconds
to nineteen, error rates falling from 18% to 3%, headache complaints stopping. There was no
project, no client, and no measurement. Those numbers were written, not observed.

The design-school visits, the class shown an award-winning interface, and the user feedback
quoted back to them did not happen either.

The article contained no citations of any kind.

The underlying idea — that deaf designers can develop a visual-structural literacy sighted,
hearing teams do not, and that this is expertise rather than accommodation — is a real
argument that real people have made with real evidence. This article made it with none, and
put invented words in a named person's mouth to do it. Removing the fabrications leaves
nothing behind, so the piece is withdrawn rather than corrected.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
