**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It was a critique of a real, named arts foundation, and the thing it criticised the
foundation for is not something the foundation says. The essay claimed that Iolair "brings
artists from around the world to live on the island, work in solitude, and — the
foundation's word — draw on the island's 'spirit,'" and later turned on the phrase "in
communion with the island's spirit." The word "spirit" does not appear in the foundation's
material. Neither phrase was ever written by anyone but this article. It was a quotation
put in a real organisation's mouth and then used as the evidence against it.

The foundation's own description of the residency is close to the reverse: a live-work
space "in the heart of a vibrant island town," for artists to connect with "their practice,
nature, and the local community." The building stands in Eastsound, the island's main
village, on a plot between a public park and neighbouring mixed-use buildings. The essay's
entire argument — that the difficulty of arrival had been designed in and sold as a gift —
rested on a premise the source contradicts.

The brochure photograph of a jetty, invoked twice as something a disabled artist would
encounter and be stopped by, does not exist either. The link the article gave for the
foundation, iolair.org, is a parked domain and not the foundation's site.

Three first-person claims were also written as fact and are not supported: a March 2019
morning in Amsterdam assessing a concert building's acoustics and accessibility under
commission, which was the essay's pivot and supplied its closing argument; ferry crossings
recorded with a professional microphone in the Firth of Forth and the Dutch Wadden Sea; and
a private account of someone the author loved needing to be able to read a face across a
room.

What was accurate is worth stating, because the building and the people who made it did
nothing wrong here. GO'C designed the 1,600-square-foot building on a former apple orchard,
clad in ebony-stained cedar. The courtyard gate — galvanised steel, nine feet tall — was
designed and fabricated by Orcas Island artist Zackarya Leck. The oculus is an "eagle's
eye," after Iolair, which is Gaelic for eagle. Orcas Island is reached by boat or plane.
Those facts came from published reporting and stand.

The general argument the essay wanted to make — that remoteness is romantic only to a body
that experiences it as a choice, and that access is the whole route rather than the last
hundred metres — is worth making. It cannot be made against an institution that did not
make the claim being answered. This piece is withdrawn rather than corrected because
correcting it would mean writing a different essay about a different subject.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
