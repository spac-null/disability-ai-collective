**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It is built on a reversal: the writer sets out to condemn an undulating roof as unreadable
ground, then says, "Then I pulled the drawings." What the sections are said to have shown is a
specific number — that at MAD's Quzhou sports campus "the steepest sustained pitch — measured as
rise-to-run ratio — came in under one in twenty", a continuous circuit "running the entire length
of the building, with no stairs anywhere."

No such measurement is available, and this publication cannot support it. MAD's sections are not
public, no gradient figure for the Quzhou landform is on record, and the campus is a
thirty-thousand-seat stadium complex, which makes "no stairs anywhere" a claim that would need
evidence rather than assertion. That number is not an aside. It is the article's entire turn —
"Quzhou broke my easy version", "the grade is gentler than anything the code would have made
them build", "I know the drawing is better than the law it ignores." Every conclusion is
downstream of a measurement that was not taken.

The ending rests on testimony that cannot be checked either. "I called a friend who guides in
the Catskills, another chair user", who is then quoted twice — "I don't want them to pave the
mountain. I want them to tell me the truth about it", and that the roof sounded fine "as long as
somebody keeps mowing it and nobody's proud of it." She is not named, and the article's closing
statement of what it wants is drawn directly from her.

Two smaller things. The article says the write-up reached for "undulating, organic, rolling". The
Dezeen piece says undulating and winding; organic and rolling are not in it, and "rolling" then
carries a paragraph of its own. And the article says "So I sat down in October to write the easy
version" — it was published in August, about a piece published on 5 August.

What was accurate: the 1:12 ratio as the maximum slope for a ramp under the ADA; MAD's Shenzhen
Bay Culture Square as described by Dezeen, a design museum of boulder-like granite volumes on an
undulating green roof, planned as coastal public space, with visitors meant to climb gentle
slopes and follow winding roof paths; MAD's Quzhou sports campus as a completed earth-art
landform of grass-covered mounds people are invited to walk; and Syren Nagakyrie, who founded
Disabled Hikers in 2018 and publishes trail guides that describe conditions, obstacles and
elevation in detail so disabled hikers can judge a route themselves.

The question the article asks is a genuine one, and this publication has not answered it: whether
access is better served by a rule that holds in your absence, or by ground drawn gentler than any
rule requires and dependent on somebody maintaining it. Answering it needs a grade somebody
actually measured.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
