**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It opened, and closed, on an incident that cannot be supported and that it attributed to a
real, named, living person. The piece stated that in June 2017 Sabriye Tenberken, who is
blind, followed a navigation app from her hotel in Nairobi towards a conference venue twelve
minutes away, was told to turn left, and "turned left into a construction trench" — and
ended by saying she found the venue eventually because a stranger walked her there. No record
of any such incident exists. Tenberken is a real person and the co-founder of Braille Without
Borders, whose work has been based in Lhasa and later in Kerala. Putting an unverified
near-injury in her name, and using it as the article's opening image and closing line, is not
something a correction can repair.

The tech review it was paired with — "In June 2017, a tech reviewer at a major outlet compared
the same app against its closest rival" — was a construction too. The review this article was
actually responding to is a recent one, and the matching June 2017 date was written to make
the diptych work.

The article's economic evidence was also unsupported. It described attending an urban mobility
workshop in Brussels in October 2022 where a project lead from a European mapping consortium
"admitted, on the record," that real-time accessibility data would cost roughly twelve per cent
more than the existing budget, and said "The market isn't there." An unnamed person at an
unnamed organisation was given a damaging verbatim quote and a precise figure, and neither can
be checked. So was the test the author claimed to have run in central Rotterdam in February
2023, routed by Google Maps to a tram stop that had been under renovation for four months.

What was accurate: Mike Oliver's 1983 distinction between impairment and disability, Rebecca
Solnit's *Wanderlust*, Waze being crowd-sourced, and Google Maps having launched wheelchair
accessible routing.

The argument is a good one and it did not need any of this. Comparison reviews of navigation
apps test arrival times, rerouting and fuel estimates, and do not test whether a pedestrian
route has a curb cut — and that is checkable against the reviews themselves. A crowd-sourced
platform does improve for the crowd that shows up. Those points stand on their own. This piece
attached them to an invented incident in a real person's name, so it is withdrawn rather than
trimmed.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
