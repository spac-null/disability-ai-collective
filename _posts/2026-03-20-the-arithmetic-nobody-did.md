**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Its argument was a coincidence of dates, and the coincidence is not real. The piece opened:
"In January 2001, the United Kingdom's Disability Rights Commission opened its doors. That
same month, Remploy — the government-backed network of sheltered factories employing
disabled workers — paid some of its workers less than two pounds an hour. Two events, same
country, same month." The Disability Rights Commission's powers and duties commenced on 25
April 2000, not January 2001, so the two events were never in the same month. And the
Remploy wage claim could not be substantiated at all: Remploy operated under a national
collective agreement, and sheltered-workshop workers had been brought within the scope of
the national minimum wage from 1999. A specific figure was attached to a real, named company
and used as the article's evidence, and the publication cannot support it.

Everything the article offered as its own evidence was also invented. A September 2018 data
review meeting in Rotterdam where the author found an eleven-month scheduling error in a
transit dataset that three analysts had missed, saving the agency "an estimated forty
thousand euros," and where a project lead privately asked a manager whether the author was
"coping okay with the workload" — no such meeting, error, saving or conversation is
supported, and it was the piece's emotional centre, returned to in the closing sentence. Nor
is there a spreadsheet of "forty-seven entries as of last month," growing "at roughly one
per week," documenting dismissed neurodivergent expertise; the astrophysicist, the
archivist and the programmer were written, not recorded. "I have timed it. By hour three of
sustained masking, my error rate on technical tasks doubles" is not a measurement that was
taken.

Two attributions were also wrong. Gregory Bateson's phrase is "the pattern which connects";
the rest of what this article had him writing — that it is "the pattern you cannot see from
inside the system" — is not his. And Monique Botha's 2022 work, cited here as showing that
masking "is directly associated with burnout, suicidal ideation, and reduced occupational
functioning," examined whether autistic community connectedness moderates the relationship
between masking and wellbeing. That is a real paper by a real researcher and this article
described it as something it is not.

What was accurate: the Office for National Statistics figure of a 12.2 percent hourly pay
gap for disabled workers, the disability employment gap of roughly 28 percentage points,
Nick Walker having coined "neuroqueer," Donna Williams and *Nobody Nowhere*, the Autistic
Self Advocacy Network's campaign against subminimum wages, and the broad history of
sheltered workshops offering a protected wage that also prevented workers from accumulating
savings.

The thing this article wanted to say — that the informal devaluation of neurodivergent
expertise is a real cost that nobody calculates — is worth saying, and someone should say it
with evidence. This piece is withdrawn rather than corrected because its central fact, its
central figure, and every one of its own measurements would have to go, and nothing would
be left holding the argument up.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
