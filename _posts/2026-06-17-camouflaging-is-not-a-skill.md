**Withdrawal, 25 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Its description of what masking feels like from the inside is not the problem, and neither
is the observation that autistic burnout was named by autistic people before it was named
in journals. The problem is the claim the whole essay was built to deliver, which is false.

The article stated that the clinical literature "classifies this capability as a social
competency", that it demonstrates "autistic people can acquire social behavior when
sufficiently motivated", that the research "did not ask what the output cost to produce",
that "the price was never in the study", and that the connection between performance, load
and collapse "was not drawn, because nobody had built the apparatus to draw it".

The camouflaging literature is very largely about the cost. Cage and Troxell-Whitman's 2019
study in the *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders* measured stress and anxiety
symptoms in 262 autistic adults by camouflaging pattern, cited camouflaging as a risk marker
for suicidality, and framed camouflaging not as evidence of autistic competence but as a
coping response to stigma — "the lack of understanding produced by the neurotypical world".
Hull and colleagues' qualitative work recorded participants describing camouflaging as
physically and mentally exhausting; their later work found camouflaging predicting worse
mental health independently of autistic trait severity. There are systematic reviews
specifically on the consequences of camouflaging, and one specifically on camouflaging and
autistic burnout in relation to one another — the connection the article said nobody had
built the apparatus to draw.

The essay had no source article, and its keyword list named a researcher, Simon Baron-Cohen,
who appears nowhere in the text and whose work it does not cite or characterise.

An argument that the cost of masking is under-recognised in clinical practice, in diagnostic
instruments, or in what services actually fund is a real argument, and autistic writers and
researchers have made it. This article did not make that argument. It made a claim about the
research record that the research record contradicts, and the claim was the piece. It cannot
be corrected into something that stands, so it is withdrawn rather than patched.
