# The item that did not apply

Before the survey began, the surveyor from the 국민연금공단, the national pension service that runs these home visits, told the applicant at the table that their activity support hours were bound to fall, and that they should save their questions for the end. The applicant is quadriplegic, and has panic disorder and depression. When the survey reached the item for maladjustment to group life, they were told it did not apply to them. To fall under that item, they recounted being told, you need symptoms of schizophrenia. A medical certificate and a prescription do not qualify. They need assistance twenty-four hours a day, and after the visit they lost about 110 hours a month — roughly four hours off every day.

They found it strange and unpleasant, they said afterwards, that the cut was discussed as a matter of course before the survey was even finished.

That one item is worth 24 points. It sits in a section worth 94, out of 596 for the whole form. So 94 of its points are, for a certain kind of body, somewhere you simply cannot go — and the three-year reprieve the ministry bolted onto it afterwards is either a rescue or a clock.

Korea had sorted disabled people into six grades on the basis of medical assessment since 1988, and the complaint against it never stopped: a grade is a verdict about your diagnosis, not an account of what your life actually requires. In July 2019 the grades went. What replaced them looks at your daily living, your cognitive and behavioural characteristics, your social activity, your household environment, and sets your level of support from the result. 복지부, the welfare ministry, expected the average monthly hours per person to rise. It lifted the ceiling to 480 hours a month — sixteen hours a day — specifically, it said, to protect the most severely disabled more thickly. A number stamped on a person was replaced by a visit, a table and a conversation.

For an adult, the functional limitation part of the basic survey runs to 13 items on daily living, 8 on instrumental daily living, and 8 on cognitive and behavioural characteristics. Those last eight are the 94 points: attention, 20. Recognising and responding to danger, 18. Hallucinations and delusions, 4. Manic-depressive states, 4. Problem behaviour, 8. Aggressive behaviour, 8. Self-harm, 8. Maladjustment to group life, 24. The section assesses states of mental disability. The table allocates its points mechanically by disability type — physical, visual and hearing, mental.

An official at a branch of the pension service put it without much decoration: because there are areas of the table where points cannot be obtained depending on your disability type, it is a fact that certain types are having their hours cut. In the ministry's own figures laid before the parliamentary audit, people with physical disability were the group most likely to lose hours, at 22.6%.

서기현, who directs the independent living centre 판, is a severely quadriplegic man. Under the old assessment he was in band 2 — the bands fix how many hours you are paid for — with 440 hours a month from the ministry and another 95 from the city government (서울시) — about seventeen hours of assistance a day. His renewal came round in autumn 2019 and the new survey was applied. The result arrived in November. He went to band 6, four bands down. 330 hours. He has said that the cut means three hours of assistance a day disappear, and that not being able to eat alone would probably mean going without one meal a day, and that his health would get worse.

So the ministry wrote a special provision into notice 2019-127. If you already had activity support and the new table cut you, you keep your old hours until the next renewal survey — three years. 복지부 says it was created to relieve existing users, and I want to be fair to it, because it does exactly what it says. 서기현 keeps his old hours. The applicant at the table keeps their hours too. Four hours a day of another person's time, preserved by a clause. That is not nothing.

It does not touch the 94 points. 서기현's score on them is whatever it was in November 2019, and it will be whatever it is at the next visit, because the thing the form measures there is not the thing he has. He has said his disability does not look likely to improve over the three years, and that activity support robots do not look likely to arrive either. Worry about what comes after had already set in, he said, which is not a complaint about the future so much as a description of the present.

A new applicant with the same body gets 330 hours. Eleven hours a day, for someone who needs twenty-four. The ministry calls the new system tailored welfare.

I wanted to know what he had actually scored, item by item, and so did he. He asked his district office (도봉구청) and the 국민연금공단 for the numbers on his own form and was refused, on the ground that releasing them would seriously obstruct the fair performance of official duties. He went to the administrative court (서울행정법원) with the disability rights group 전국장애인차별철폐연대 and three other organisations to have the refusal overturned, arguing it violates disabled people's right to know. His lawyer, 정제형, pointed out that what was asked for is simply the score for each survey item, that releasing those scores cannot obstruct the fair performance of official duties, and that the matter has already been assessed and decided, so it is not some internal deliberation either. For severely disabled people, 정제형 said, this is tied to survival.

Raising the points available to the physical and brain lesion disability types — the two categories whose hours the ministry's figures showed falling — would not fix it in any case. The 596 is a fixed pie, split between types; if one side's points go up the other side's come down. Underneath the cuts is a point allocation that divides shares among disability types with no increase in the budget. 박경석, who sat on the committee reviewing the notice, has said the table alone can never solve it, and that it was manipulated to fit the money available.

The old grade said what you were. The new table asks what you need, in 596 points, and then tells you how much of your need it is able to see. Becoming legible to it is what gets you hours at all. It is also what fixes the answer, because the sections where your points would have been are measuring someone else.

At a press conference in the 해치마당 plaza in 광화문, disabled people who had been granted the three-year preservation after their cuts put ropes around their necks. They said it was no different from being given a terminal diagnosis. The special provision had worked. That was the point they were making.
