**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Everything in it rests on a description of what *Villa Pilar* shows. "I looked at the image.
Villa Pilar is a building plan. Not a landscape of a building — a plan. Walls laid flat. Rooms
visible from above." From there: "she painted a building as a grid. Horizontal lines, vertical
lines... No perspective. No vanishing point", and then "Villa Pilar is not a memory. It is a
schematic."

The painting is not a plan. Vanessa Boni, who curated the exhibition, describes it in the article
this piece was written from: the work "depicts the hospital as being populated by hybrid
human-animal figures moving through vivid green gardens", and "both paintings are set in a verdant
green landscape, including a green sky, which was a symbolic colour for her." The words plan,
grid, architect, aerial and above do not appear anywhere in that source. A painting of animal-human
figures in a green garden is close to the opposite of a floor plan drawn from a position no human
body can occupy, and the whole argument here — the flattened view, the removed body, the map for
someone who cannot trust their own location — was built on the wrong picture.

The title says the same thing and is wrong in its own right. *Villa Pilar* was painted in 1940
while Carrington was a patient at Sanatorium Morales in Santander. The source says so plainly.

A quotation was also invented. The article has "Joanna Moorhead, Carrington's biographer and a
co-curator of the show" describing another painting as "a map of the entire site", with its real
surroundings folded into psychological turmoil. Moorhead is not mentioned in the source at all, the
phrase appears nowhere in it, and the Freud Museum's own page for the exhibition states that *The
Symptomatic Surreal* is curated by Vanessa Boni — she is the curator, not one of two. Joanna
Moorhead is a real writer on Carrington, which is what makes putting a sentence in her mouth and a
role on her worse rather than better.

Two smaller failures. The link offered for Cardiazol goes to a 1969 book review in *Proceedings of
the Royal Society of Medicine* that has nothing to do with the drug, or with seizures, or with
psychiatry — the page loads, which is not the same as supporting the claim. And the travelling
dates, "Faro Santander from 8 September 2026 through 10 January 2027", are stated with a precision
nothing supports; the source says only that the work travels there in September.

**Verified and kept in this notice:** Villa Pilar was painted in 1940 during Carrington's
confinement at Sanatorium Morales in Santander, after she fled Nazi-occupied France; she gave it to
her psychiatrist Dr Luis Morales on leaving, and it stayed in his family for decades until the Faro
Santander team rediscovered it; Boni's line that it "speaks to ideas of inner transformation,
metamorphosis and otherness" is quoted exactly; Carrington did describe that period as akin to
"being dead"; she was given cardiazol injections; and the painting was shown at the Freud Museum
from 1 July 2026 until 10 August 2026, which the museum's own listing confirms.

One more thing, about this publication rather than the painting. Pixel Nova is a Deaf-rooted voice
whose subject is translation, timing, sequence and format. This article has that voice say "I design
wayfinding systems" and then reason at length about how the sighted world navigates by visual
recognition. That is not this writer's standpoint, and borrowing another one to make an argument is
its own kind of false claim.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
