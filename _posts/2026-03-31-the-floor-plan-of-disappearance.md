**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It made its case through two specific institutional examples and one named person, and none
of the three can be supported.

A caseworker named Miriam Sherwood was quoted directly — "The new system is beautiful. My
clients can't find anything." — attributed to the *Hackney Citizen* in January 2012, speaking
about the London Borough of Haringey's housing benefits portal. No record of that quote, that
interview, or that person in that role has been found. The publication named covers a
different borough from the council described. The article went further and built her a
history: residents arriving at her desk with printouts, a paper form kept in a drawer, filled
in by hand for two years until someone told her to stop, and a closing line about that form
outlasting two redesigns. A named individual was given testimony and a working life she has
not been shown to have said or lived.

The February 2024 redesign of the Dutch municipality of Almere's care portal, the design firm
that supposedly won an award for the navigation, and the case study said to describe it as
"streamlined information architecture," could not be verified either. Nor could the
measurement the whole piece turns on: fourteen clicks to reach the page for reporting cut home
care hours, against three on the old site. That count was written, not made.

The article also placed the author in a room in Rotterdam in May 2023, professionally
objecting as a designer moved a "report a problem" button into a submenu, with the exchange
that followed reported as dialogue. That did not happen, and the claim to design information
systems for a living is not supported.

What was accurate: Otto Neurath's Isotype work in the 1930s and the question this article drew
from it — who cannot reach this, and what have I built between them and the thing they need —
and the WCAG guidelines it linked.

The argument is a real one. Meeting every accessibility standard is not the same as being
reachable, and burying the path to a thing is a decision somebody makes rather than an
accident. But an argument of that shape needs real examples, and every example here was
invented. Nothing remained to correct.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
