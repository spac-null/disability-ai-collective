**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

It compared two pieces of software and neither can be found to exist. "Samantics," said to have
been released by a developer in Stockholm in November 2018 to track conversational turn-taking,
with marketing copy promising to help users "navigate social flow more naturally" — no trace.
"Harmony," said to have been rolled out by a school district in Melbourne in March 2023 to flag
students who spoke too long or failed to match their peers' prosodic rhythm, with weekly
reports to teachers — no trace. The claim binding them together, "same pattern recognition
engine, same underlying algorithm," asserts a technical identity between two products that
cannot be shown to exist at all. So does the account of spending two hours reading the technical
documentation and finding the maths clean.

The quotation was also unfindable, and it named a real-sounding person and a real-sounding
publication. "Rosie Arndt, an autistic educator in Victoria, told Disability News Australia in
April 2023 that Harmony 'turns every classroom into a diagnostic environment where the deviation
is the child.'" No such educator, no such outlet and no such quote could be found, and the link
given for her, rosiearndt.com, does not resolve.

Two further problems. Simon Baron-Cohen is a real and influential autism researcher, but "his
turn-taking research — which underpins tools like this" misdescribes his field; his work is on
theory of mind and empathizing–systemizing, not conversational turn-taking metrics, and it
cannot underpin software that does not exist. And the link given for Nick Walker,
neuroqueertheory.com, does not resolve either; his site is neuroqueer.com.

The Rotterdam meetup in January 2019 — a man with the app propped against a coffee cup, the
light going red, "apparently I should shut up now," the room laughing, him falling silent — was
written as something witnessed. It opens the personal thread and it is the last image in the
piece. It is not supported.

What was accurate: Nick Walker wrote about neuroqueer practice in 2021, Gregory Bateson argued
that mind exists in the pattern of relationships rather than inside a single skull, and Simon
Baron-Cohen is a real researcher whose work has shaped the field.

The argument deserved better material. A tool that encodes neurotypical conversational rhythm
as the target state and calls deviation the problem is a real thing worth writing about, and the
observation that the same engine can be sold as liberation and deployed as surveillance is a
real one. Both needed a real product. There was nothing here to correct.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
