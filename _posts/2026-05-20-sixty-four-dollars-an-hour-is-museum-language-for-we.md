**Withdrawal, 25 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

The Seattle Art Museum unionisation drive it was written from is real. Staff did seek
voluntary recognition, the union is Seattle Art Museum Workers United, affiliated with
WFSE/AFSCME Council 28, and they did cite unsustainable wages, subpar health benefits and
top-down decision-making, and did ask for just-cause protections in place of at-will
employment. Almost everything the article built on top of that was wrong.

**The date was wrong.** The letter was submitted on 13 May 2026, not 13 May 2025. The
article put its own news hook a year in the past.

**The count was wrong.** It said "over a hundred workers signed it". Over a hundred employees
are involved; 59 signed.

**The central factual premise was backwards.** The article's argument turns on museums keeping
"two categories of worker" — direct employees who "work for meaning" and contracted staff who
"work for wages" — and states that SAM's security guards "are not museum employees", that
"security is contracted out", and that "that firm bargained. That firm signed the contract."
The guards are museum employees. Their union was the first to represent workers at the museum,
and they bargained with and ratified a contract with the museum itself. The distinction the
essay is built on does not exist.

**The wages were invented.** The source contains no wage figures. The article asserted that a
SAM security guard "makes twenty-two dollars an hour after the new contract" and a visitor
services associate "makes eighteen", that guards get healthcare immediately and associates wait
ninety days, and elsewhere that museums "pay seventeen dollars an hour". The guards' contract
raised base wages from $21.68 to $24.18; the rest has no source. The headline's "sixty-four
dollars an hour" appears nowhere in the article and corresponds to nothing in the reporting.
The headline was also published cut off mid-phrase.

**A quotation was manufactured, with a manufactured provenance.** The article stated that a
Tacoma Art Museum manager said, in a staff meeting, "We offer an experience most people would
pay to have," and that this was "recorded in meeting notes I obtained through a public records
request". No such quotation is on record and no such request was made. Inventing a statement
for an unnamed manager at a named institution is serious on its own; attaching a fabricated
documentary provenance to it is worse, because it invites the reader to treat it as verified.
Tacoma Art Museum workers did vote unanimously to unionise in November 2023, and did later
ratify a first contract — those facts are real and are not the problem.

**The first-person account was invented.** The six-month municipal museum contract in 2019, the
eighteen dollars an hour, the infected molar and the six-week waiting period, the month-five
performance review, the tooth pulled in month seven, the unwritten wall text about monks'
marginalia, the curators who sleep in their cars, the educators rationing medication, the staff
meeting about accessible seating, the gallery that opened fourteen months later and the forty
minutes standing at its opening — none of it can be documented.

The argument underneath — that wages and healthcare function as a filter deciding which
disabled people can hold a museum job at all, and that a union contract can deliver access
without ever using the word — is a real argument, and museum workers and disability advocates
have made it with real figures. This article made it with invented ones, and put words in a
real institution's mouth. Removing the fabrications and the errors leaves the first paragraph,
so the piece is withdrawn rather than corrected.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
