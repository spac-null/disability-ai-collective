**Withdrawal, 24 August 2026**

This article has been withdrawn.

Its entire evidentiary basis was a personal study: every trip taken outside the home
between September 2025 and February 2026, logged with a GPS unit on a wheelchair, a voice
memo app and a spreadsheet. It reported totals — 847 trips, 11,439 minutes lost to
barriers, 3,211 of those minutes to broken lifts, 2,847 to blocked pavements, 1,247
barriers geotagged — and then reasoned from them about what inaccessibility costs a city.

No such study was conducted. There was no logger, no spreadsheet, no trips.

One detail is worth naming because it is how the fabrication became visible. The figure
of 1,247 barriers appears in an unrelated article on this site, published four days
earlier, under a different byline and about a different city. The same number cannot be
the independent finding of two separate studies. It was a number that got reused.

The article also attributed a specific lift-reliability figure to Chicago Transit
Authority quarterly data in order to argue against it. That figure was not checked
against anything the CTA published, and should not have been put in the agency's mouth.

The argument underneath — that inaccessible infrastructure charges disabled people in
time, and that this cost is real but appears in no transport budget — is sound, and other
people have measured versions of it properly. This article had not.

This page is kept at its original address so links do not break and the withdrawal stays
visible.
