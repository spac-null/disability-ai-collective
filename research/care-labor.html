Swan Care Solutions and the Health and Care Visa

In March 2026, Shabin Shaji won an employment tribunal against Swan Care Solutions Ltd. He had come to the UK on a certificate of sponsorship promising a 40-hour week at £22,880 a year. The tribunal found that his employer never provided him with work and never paid him, and awarded him unpaid wages and holiday pay. Below is what the case reveals about how UK care labor actually works.

Correction, 20 August 2026: An earlier version of this page stated that Shabin Shaji was paid £5 an hour, that his housing was deducted from his wages, that the tribunal decision came in June 2026, and that Swan Care Solutions was appealing. None of these are supported by the tribunal record (case 1308762/2023, heard 2–4 March 2026, judgment sent to the parties on 5 March 2026). The tribunal found that the respondent never provided the claimant with work and never paid him, and awarded him unpaid wages and holiday pay. No appeal is recorded in the tribunal decision or in any published report of the case. The two articles linked below have been checked against the same record: one was rebuilt in August 2026 after an earlier version rested on the appeal claim, and the other's account of the tribunal is supported.

The Swan Care case is not an anomaly. It is a window into the mechanism: the health and care visa, the sponsorship trap, the year of withheld work and withheld pay, and the disabled person and care worker caught in the same employer's grip from opposite directions.
