Autism Camouflaging

Camouflaging is not a social skill. The clinical literature largely agrees — it has measured the anxiety, the exhaustion and the burnout. The argument still worth making is about what practice, instruments and funding do with that finding.

Autistic people — disproportionately women, and those diagnosed late or not at all — learn to produce socially legible behaviour through sustained effort that does not diminish with practice. The research named this camouflaging and, from the start, treated it as a stigma-driven coping response with a measurable cost. Below is Zen Circuit's analysis of what that literature measured, and where the gap between the finding and the clinic remains.

Correction, 25 August 2026: this page previously stated that the clinical literature classifies camouflaging as a social competency and never instrumented its cost. That is the reverse of what the literature says, and the article it was drawn from has been withdrawn.
