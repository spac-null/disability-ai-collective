System Report

Crip Minds: A System Report

Jascha Blume — cripminds.com

Snapshot of 6 September 2026. This page describes the system on that date and is rewritten when the system changes, not continuously.

How Crip Minds Works explains the editorial idea for general readers. This report is narrower and more technical: what runs in production on the date above, what it will not do, and which principles stay fixed as the tools change. It does not document the private machinery closely enough to reproduce it.

01 — Currently live

Crip Minds is a public editorial publication with four recurring voices. In production now: wide discovery across many candidate subjects; research that fetches and reads further documents beyond the one that prompted a subject; a composed article built in stages, from the evidence it is allowed to use through to the shape of the story; a surface polish; factual and safety checks run against the exact words that will publish; and a packaging step that writes the title, standfirst, and excerpt from the finished text. Illustration and editorial selection are automated inside the same design. Dutch editions of selected articles are made from the settled English article. The website is built with Jekyll and published through GitHub Pages.

This describes the current generator, not the historical corpus: essays published under earlier editorial systems were produced differently, stay published, and carry their correction history.

The exact rhythm and order of those operations can change. The site therefore makes no fixed promise about how often a draft is made, how often an article appears, which voice publishes next, or which current model performs a task.

02 — Authorship

Jascha Blume created and authors the system. He designs the personas, editorial limits, source rules, factual boundaries, ways of testing an idea, and the setting in which an article appears.

AI takes part inside that design. Crip Minds is therefore not simply an AI producing essays from disability prompts, and the personas are not autonomous people making independent claims.

03 — Personas and ways of seeing

Pixel Nova, Siri Sage, Maya Flux, and Zen Circuit are fictional editorial personas. They provide continuity, voice, motive, and character. They are not four real disabled people, and they are not substitutes for testimony.

A way of seeing is separate from a persona. It is an action taken toward a subject: following what translation changes, testing a category, examining the relation between a person and a setting, or asking what hidden conditions make a promise work. These actions are not permanently assigned to four names or limited to disability topics.

04 — The Mind Engine

The Mind Engine is Crip Minds’ private editorial system. It looks for places where the obvious explanation of a subject begins to fail, establishes what a disability-rooted reading makes knowable there, and holds that reading to the material it was built from. The reading is settled before a voice is attached to it.

Its purpose is public; its complete recipe is not. The exact mechanics are not part of the public write-up.

05 — Factual grounding

An article may make a bold interpretation that the original source did not make, but the facts it depends on must remain supported. It may not quietly add an event, quotation, statistic, date, or biographical memory to make that interpretation work.

Separating a new interpretation from a new factual premise disguised as interpretation is a check the system runs, not an ambition it holds. Unsupported factual claims are removed, softened, or marked as uncertain, and an article whose factual checks fail is held rather than published.

06 — What changed, and what this page will not claim

Earlier versions of this report described a fuller Mind Engine as unfinished research, and described the current process as building an article from a single reading of one source. Neither is true as of the date above. The fuller architecture is what production runs, and an article is built from a body of material gathered for it rather than from one page. That development work is done in a private research setting; it is not published on this site.

Articles produced under the current system carry current-engine provenance in the repository. Earlier pieces came from previous editorial systems, stay published, and are reviewed where needed.

What this page will not claim: that the system is finished, that its judgement is reliable in a way a reader should take on trust, or that any of it removes the editor from the work. It is an authored system that holds more than it publishes, and being held is its ordinary outcome.

07 — What stays fixed

The project is not mainly about disability as a subject. A disabled way of seeing must reveal something about the subject itself that the dominant account missed.

No superpower story. Disability is not presented as heightened perception, compensation, inspiration, or proof that disabled people are better at something.

No invented life. Pixel has roots in Blume’s documented Deaf practice, but Pixel is not Blume. Siri, Maya, and Zen are constructed voices. None may fabricate memories or pass fiction off as testimony.

Clear language. Difficulty belongs in the thought, not in needless specialist words or complicated sentences.

Jascha Blume

Amsterdam / Bologna, 2026
