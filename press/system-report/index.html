System Snapshot

System snapshot

13 September 2026

Crip Minds changes as the editorial system changes. This page records the current setup rather than a permanent specification, and is rewritten when the system materially changes, not continuously.

01 — What the system does

Crip Minds uses AI to assist discovery, research, drafting and checking, inside an editorial system created and directed by Jascha Blume.

Different stages help at different points. A reader does not need to see those stages for the essay to be accountable; what the essay claims, and what supports it, is the part that has to hold.

The site is built with Jekyll and published as static pages. Selected essays also appear in Dutch, made from the settled English text.

02 — How stories enter

There is more than one way in. Some essays begin from an approved disability-rooted question and go looking for a story that could carry or test it. Others begin in the ordinary world and are tested for whether there is a real Crip Minds question in them. Most subjects do not survive that test.

Where a subject comes from can count, but only as a tie-breaker between candidates already judged equally good. It is a prior, not a quota: no region, country or language is guaranteed a slot, and a strong English-language story competes normally.

03 — Beyond English-language sources

The commissioning system can now search beyond English-language sources, and records a source’s original language and script, and whether translation was used to read it. This was added on the date of this snapshot and has not yet produced a published essay. Until it does, treat it as a capability rather than a track record.

04 — Facts, and what is checked

A perspective can shape the question. Facts come from the research done for that essay. Separating a new interpretation from a new factual premise disguised as interpretation is a check the system runs, not an ambition it holds.

Checking distinguishes a small local imperfection from an error that would materially change the story or the argument. The second kind holds the essay. Ethics →

05 — What a reader can see

Essays are credited to Crip Minds, the publication. Where an essay carries a named perspective — PINA, MIRA, SIIRI or ZENO — it is shown under the byline. Most do not, because not every essay is commissioned from a question that names one.

A published essay can end with a short source list, five entries at most. It is a way into the material, not the full record of everything read.

06 — What this page will not claim

Not that the system is finished, not that its judgement is reliable in a way a reader should take on trust, and not that any of it removes the editor from the work. It is an authored system that holds more than it publishes, and being held is its ordinary outcome.

The rhythm and order of these operations can change, so the site makes no fixed promise about how often a draft is made or how often an essay appears. Essays published under earlier editorial systems were made differently. They stay published, with their correction history.
