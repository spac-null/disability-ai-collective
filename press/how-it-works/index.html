How It Works

How Crip Minds Works

Jascha Blume — cripminds.com — 2026

01 — The editorial idea

Crip Minds begins with a factual subject: perhaps a symbiosis between species, a control panel on the International Space Station, a deep-sea ecosystem, a cut in a film, a Soviet artwork, a knife made for a Persian princess, or a bottle of water on a supermarket shelf. It looks for the detail that does not quite fit the obvious explanation.

The central question is not “how does this affect disabled people?” It is: what does a disabled way of seeing make knowable about this subject that the usual framing has missed? The best answer changes what the subject appears to be doing.

This is an authored editorial system. I design the voices, constraints, source rules, checks, and publication setting. AI works inside those choices.

Automated research, drafting, checking, and selection are part of current production. The process continues to change, so Crip Minds does not promise a fixed writing or publishing rhythm.

02 — The four voices

Pixel Nova, Siri Sage, Maya Flux, and Zen Circuit are fictional editorial personas. They are not hidden human columnists, autonomous disabled people, or substitutes for testimony. They create continuity and character, but they are not four fixed theories.

Pixel Nova

Deaf-rooted voice

Pixel has factual roots in Jascha Blume’s documented Deaf art practice. Pixel notices how timing, translation, sequence, and format change what arrives.

“What changed between what was sent and what arrived?”

Siri Sage

Blind-rooted voice

Siri tests the relation between a person and a place, asking what changes when the setting, tool, or rule changes.

“What was blamed on the person but produced by the setting?”

Maya Flux

Mobility-rooted voice

Maya follows the gap between a promise and the conditions needed to keep it, including hidden labor and timing.

“What has to happen for this promise to work in practice?”

Zen Circuit

Neurodivergent-rooted voice

Zen examines categories, scores, and labels, asking what a system ignores to make reality fit its chosen measure.

“What did the measure have to leave out?”

A persona does not own a topic or a single method. Pixel’s Deafness determines how Pixel looks, not what Pixel is allowed to look at. The same separation applies across the publication.

03 — Voice and way of seeing

A persona and a way of seeing are not the same thing. A persona has a voice, motive, texture, interests, and risks. A way of seeing is an editorial action: following timing, testing a category, or asking what conditions make a promise work.

Disability scholarship, culture, interviews, art, and history inform the project. They are influences and factual sources, not four hard-coded formulas attached permanently to four names.

This separation matters because the first obvious angle is not automatically accepted. A subject can be considered in more than one way before the eventual voice and article form are chosen.

The personas are authored rather than discovered. I decide their motives, limits, recurring questions, and textual character. I also decide what they must never claim. A constructed voice cannot turn an imagined event into personal history.

Pixel has stronger factual grounding in my documented Deaf artistic practice than the other voices. That does not make Pixel me, and it does not authorize invented childhood scenes, interpreter incidents, or memories.

The other three biographies are editorial constructions. Their claims must be grounded in sources and disability knowledge, not presented as memories of real disabled people.

04 — The Mind Engine

Behind each article is a private editorial system I call the Mind Engine. It looks for the detail that makes the easy explanation start to break down, considers more than one way of understanding what is happening, and asks which reading reveals something about the subject itself.

The first obvious angle is not automatically accepted, and producing a draft does not automatically make it worth publishing. Sources, interpretation, voice, language, and illustration are tested and shaped within an editorial process authored by Jascha.

This explains what the Mind Engine makes possible, not how to reproduce it. Its exact mechanics are not part of the public write-up.

05 — Editorial boundaries

The system changes, but several editorial boundaries remain stable.

No invented biography. A fictional voice may not create childhood memories, meetings, discrimination scenes, or other personal history and present them as fact.

No disability superpowers. A contribution is shown through observation and argument. It is not described as compensation, heightened perception, inspiration, or superiority.

No invented factual floor. Names, dates, numbers, quotations, and events need source support. If support cannot be found, the claim is removed or clearly marked as uncertain.

Interpretation remains possible. Grounding does not mean repeating the source. The article must be free to discover a meaning the source did not name, as long as it does not add unsupported facts.

The source provides the floor. The way of seeing provides the leap.

No default medical or pity framing. Disability is treated as culture, politics, perception, and knowledge, not automatically as diagnosis, tragedy, or an inspirational obstacle.

07 — Why this approach

I am Deaf, and Nederlandse Gebarentaal is my mother tongue. My art has long examined what happens when language, information, and experience pass through translation, timing, and other people’s forms.

Crip Minds grows from a wish to recover forms of perception and knowledge that have become distant through translation or other people’s assumptions, and to give them form again.

Not writing about disability — seeing the world through it.

A disabled way of experiencing reality is not a deficit to explain. It is a way of knowing that can reveal something the dominant world has failed to notice. That contribution must appear in the observation and argument, not as a claim of special powers.

My contribution is authorship: choosing what the project attends to, designing its voices and boundaries, and deciding what counts as a strong and supportable article.

The Mind Engine extends that work without turning it into a reproducible public recipe. Its purpose can be explained clearly while its exact machinery is not published.

If you’re a curator, an artist, an educator, a journalist — and something on the site is useful to you: use it. All the articles are open. No paywall, no registration. The source code for the site is public. The scholarship the personas draw on is named and linked wherever possible.

This page explains how Crip Minds thinks editorially. The System Report records what is live, what is developing, and which public claims should remain durable, without publishing the private recipe.
