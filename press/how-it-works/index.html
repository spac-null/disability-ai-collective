Every essay starts as a subject with something unresolved in it. This is what happens between that and a published piece.

01 — Find the story

Crip Minds begins with something real: an event, a person, an object, an institution, a study, an ordinary system. It needs a tension inside it — the detail that does not quite fit the obvious explanation.

Many more subjects are read than will ever become essays. Most stop here, and that is where they should stop.

02 — Ask a better question

Disability is not only a subject here. It can change the question. A form, for instance, can be read for what it assumes about attention — and the answer is often about the form, not about disabled people.

Four disability-rooted perspectives — PINA, MIRA, SIIRI and ZENO — are the ways Crip Minds sharpens that question. Not every essay starts from one. Perspectives →

03 — Research it

The question guides the research. It does not decide the answer.

Sources are gathered for that specific story. The piece that prompted a subject is rarely enough, so further documents are found and read alongside it. Everything the essay treats as fact comes from there. A perspective can raise a question; it cannot make anything true.

04 — Build the essay

The essay follows the story, not the order in which the research happened. Something concrete has to carry the idea — a person, an event, an object. Hard ideas arrive one at a time, so the reader keeps moving.

It may reach further than any single source concluded. It may not invent the ground it stands on. AI assists the drafting inside those rules.

05 — Check what matters

Before publication the essay is checked against its research. A small local imperfection is not the same as an error that changes the story, the people in it or the argument. That kind of error stops the piece.

Producing a draft does not make it worth publishing. An essay that fails is held, not fixed into publishability. Ethics →

06 — Publish with sources

A published essay ends with the sources behind it — primary and authoritative first, five at most. It is a way into the material, not the full record of everything read.
