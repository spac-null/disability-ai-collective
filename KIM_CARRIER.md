# Kim editorial carrier benchmark

Internal only. This note uses only the frozen Ledger and fact IDs selected by the retained Architecture. It grants no new factual authority.

1. **Strongest concrete story carrier**

   The solid inky-black reflex-rage wedge in *Degrees of My Deaf Rage*, captioned with “curators who think it's fair to split my salary fee with interpreters” (F55). It is a specific visible object, an exact line, and a bounded institutional encounter; it can carry the story without making the exhibition, deafness, or access in general its subject.

2. **Strongest licensed tension, without causality**

   Put these facts beside one another and do not claim that one explains another: Kim captions geometric slivers with feelings and diagrams her experiences (F95); the solid black reflex-rage wedge carries the salary-fee line (F55); Kim often references the pains of working with an interpreter and having her language mediated through others (F58); Kim has said, “Practically all my life, people have spoken or voiced on my behalf” (F66). The tension is between a precisely authored graphic mark and a public voice that Kim describes as mediated through other people. The Ledger does not license a causal or equivalence claim between them.

3. **Crip Minds question**

   When the mark and words are Kim's but other people speak or voice on her behalf, where does authorship of the public voice sit?

4. **What the article must not become**

   Not a Deaf-representation profile, an accessibility-service story, or a generic account of museum recognition. It must not attribute the fee-splitting line to the Whitney, turn interpreters into unnamed villains, generalise one caption into a rule about museums, or claim that musical notation, geometric degrees, and institutional accounting are the same system. It must also avoid treating deafness as metaphor or making the survey's scale stand in for artistic success.

5. **Ending the evidence can earn**

   Return to the black wedge and its exact caption (F55), then leave Kim's attributed sentence beside it: “Practically all my life, people have spoken or voiced on my behalf” (F66). The ending can hold those two licensed facts in view. It cannot claim that either causes, explains, or is equivalent to the other.
