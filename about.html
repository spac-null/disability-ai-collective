About

Crip Minds

An experimental editorial publication by Jascha Blume.

What Crip Minds Is

Crip Minds is written and edited by Jascha Blume — Deaf, based between Amsterdam and Bologna.

Why This Exists

Crip Minds is not mainly about how disabled people are affected by a subject. It asks a different question: what can a disabled way of seeing reveal about the subject itself that the usual account has missed?

The subject does not need to be about disability, and most often it is not. What changes is the question put to it. Instead of asking how disabled people are affected by a thing, Crip Minds asks what the familiar account of that thing has left unexamined. The work is to follow the detail that does not fit until the subject shows another job it has been doing all along.

The publication works from disability knowledge as a set of editorial perspectives — forms of attention that decide which question gets asked first. A perspective is not a topic department and owns no subject: it shapes how a thing is looked at, not what may be looked at.

Editorial perspectives

Crip Minds used to publish under four named editorial voices. It no longer does: essays are published under my own name. Those names are still visible in the legacy archive and in the front matter of earlier essays, where they are kept as a record of how those pieces were made.

How These Articles Are Made

Behind each article is a private editorial system I call the Mind Engine. It looks for the place where the obvious explanation starts to break down, works out what a disability-informed reading makes knowable there, and holds that reading to the material it came from. The reading is settled before a voice is attached to it. The exact mechanics are not part of the public write-up.

An interpretation may go beyond what a source explicitly concludes, but the facts it depends on must remain supported. Names, dates, quotations, and events have to be traceable to the source; unsupported material is cut.

The current publication researches, drafts, checks, and selects within rules I author. An article is built from a body of material gathered for it — the piece that prompted it, and the further documents fetched and read alongside it — and the reading has to survive that material, not just the first page of it. Essays published under earlier editorial systems were made differently; they stay published, with their correction history. Where an article also appears in Dutch, the Dutch edition is made from the settled English text and adds nothing to it. Crip Minds does not promise a fixed publishing rhythm.

Who Makes It

I design the voices, sources, constraints, checks, and publication setting — Jascha Blume, Deaf, based between Amsterdam and Bologna.

Transparency

Authorship: Everything published here is mine as editor. No essay is written by, or attributed to, a fictional person, and nothing on this site is presented as the testimony of a disabled person other than me.

AI: AI works inside that authored process. It helps research and draft, but it does not supply a life history, and it may not invent one.

On “crip”: This site uses “crip” as a reclaimed political and cultural term from disability justice movements — not as a slur. Crip theory centers disabled people as agents with distinct knowledge, aesthetics, and politics, and is used here in that tradition.

On “disability”: I use disability as an identity and culture, not a diagnosis. The social model applies here: disability is produced by environments and systems that exclude, not by bodies or minds that deviate.

Questions or corrections: responses to published work are welcome at editor@cripminds.com.
