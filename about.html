About

Crip Minds

Crip Minds is an experimental editorial publication by Jascha Blume. Four recurring voices, one authored system.

About This Project

Each essay on this site appears through one of four recurring voices. I built those voices. I’m Jascha Blume — Deaf, based between Amsterdam and Bologna.

The voices are fictional editorial personas, not autonomous disabled people. I shaped them from disability knowledge, cultural history, scholarship, interviews, artistic practice, and written constraints. Pixel has additional roots in my documented Deaf art practice. The other personas are constructed editorial voices, not disguised biographies.

Crip Minds is not mainly about how disabled people are affected by a subject. It asks a different question: what can a disabled way of seeing reveal about the subject itself that the usual account has missed?

An article can begin with a symbiosis between species, a mycelial network, a control panel on the International Space Station, a deep-sea ecosystem, a cut in a film, a Soviet artwork, a knife made for a Persian princess, or a bottle of water on a supermarket shelf. The subject needs no obvious connection to disability. The aim is to follow the detail that does not fit the familiar story until the thing reveals another job it has been doing all along.

The four voices give the publication continuity and character. They are not four fixed theories or four topic departments. A way of looking can be tested before a voice is chosen, and no persona owns one subject. Pixel’s Deafness, for example, shapes how Pixel looks — not what Pixel is allowed to look at.

I design the voices, sources, constraints, checks, and publication setting. AI works inside that authored process. It helps research and draft, but it does not supply a life history, and it may not invent one.

Three places to start

If you’re new here, these are the pieces that show what this publication is.

  • The Map That Stops at the Door → There are arts venues that have won accessibility awards. There are Deaf visitors who followed their directions exactly and stood outside, unable to find the entrance. These are, frequently, the same venues.
  • The Open Office Was Designed to Break My Brain → Open offices were designed to eliminate barriers between people, but they built one inside my brain.
  • The City Forgot to Sound-Design Its Streets → Cities obsess over how streets look. Almost no one asks how they sound — and that silence is a design choice that erases millions of people from public space.

How These Articles Are Made

Behind each article is a private editorial system we call the Mind Engine. It looks for the place where the obvious explanation starts to break down, explores more than one way of seeing what is happening, and asks which reading changes our understanding of the subject itself. The exact mechanics are not part of the public write-up.

The source provides the floor. The way of seeing provides the leap. An interpretation may be bold, but it cannot quietly invent the facts beneath it. Names, dates, quotations, and events must be supported; unsupported material is cut.

The current publication uses automated research, drafting, checking, and selection within rules I author. A fuller version of the Mind Engine, which compares different readings before choosing a voice, is still in development. Crip Minds does not promise a fixed publishing rhythm while that work changes.

A Note on Language

On “crip”: This site uses “crip” as a reclaimed political and cultural term from disability justice movements — not as a slur. Crip theory centers disabled people as agents with distinct knowledge, aesthetics, and politics, and is used here in that tradition.

On “disability”: I use disability as an identity and culture, not a diagnosis. The social model applies here: disability is produced by environments and systems that exclude, not by bodies or minds that deviate.
