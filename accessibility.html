Accessibility

Accessibility Statement

I built this site while being profoundly Deaf. I know what access costs when it’s missing.

Accessibility on this site isn’t a compliance checklist I work through at the end. It’s something I think about from the beginning, because I navigate digital space as a Deaf person and I understand what it means when a site assumes you can hear or see.

The publication is grounded in disability justice. It would be a contradiction to build it on infrastructure that excludes people. The toggles below are real features, not gestures. WCAG 2.1 AA is treated as a floor, not a ceiling. And if something doesn’t work for you, I want to know.

Accessibility Features

Look for the toggles in the bottom-right corner of your screen.

High Contrast Mode

Switches to a high-contrast palette with bright colors for better visibility.

Auto-activates if your system is set to high contrast.

Dyslexia-Friendly Font

Switches to OpenDyslexic with increased letter spacing.

For readers who prefer it. Whether it helps is a matter of personal preference, not a claim made here.

Built and Tested Toward WCAG 2.1 AA

  • Color contrast meets the 4.5:1 minimum, verified with axe-core in both light and dark themes
  • Text scales without a fixed pixel ceiling; the layout is built to reflow rather than clip
  • Meaningful images carry descriptive alt text; decorative images are hidden from assistive technology
  • Interactive elements are reachable and operable by keyboard
  • Skip links for efficient navigation
  • Semantic headings and landmark regions throughout
  • 44px minimum touch targets on mobile

Testing so far is automated (axe-core, wcag2a and wcag2aa, both themes) plus manual review. No independent third-party conformance audit has been carried out, so this page describes what the site is built and tested toward rather than certified against.
